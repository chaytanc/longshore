# The fediverse audience — who LONGSHORE can actually reach, X-free (mid-2026)

*Strategy research, not worldbuilding. A map of the real, current (August 2026) audience reachable from one honest Mastodon account, plus the other X-free venues. Companion to `landscape-2026.md` (the moment) and `audience-model.md` (the diffusion science). Anti-Echo-Chamber Protocol applied: disconfirming section at the end, opponents steelmanned inline, wildcard lens rolled (session id ends in 4 → disability justice & crip theory), non-Western precedent noted.*

---

## 1. The fediverse as it actually is — small, stable, and honest about it

**The honest numbers.** Mastodon holds ~10.5M registered accounts (April 2026) but only **~750k–1M monthly active users** ([Marketful stats](https://marketful.com/mastodon-statistics), [ThinkImpact](https://www.thinkimpact.com/mastodon-statistics/)). MAU peaked at 2.6M in November 2022 (the Musk exodus), fell under 690k by October 2025, and has since settled into "relative stability" ([ExpandedRamblings](https://expandedramblings.com/index.php/mastodon-statistics-facts/), [Fediview](https://fediview.com/articles/fediverse-in-numbers-mastodon-stats-2026/)). The whole fediverse — Mastodon + Pixelfed + PeerTube + Lemmy etc. — is roughly **1–2M active people combined** ([elest.io overview](https://blog.elest.io/the-fediverse-is-growing-why-decentralized-social-media-matters-in-2026/)). For scale: that is one mid-sized city, not a public.

**The 2025 whiplash.** January–April 2025 brought a Trump/Musk-driven influx; "a few months later that fediverse growth had evaporated, and active users today is apparently less than it was two years ago" ([Manton Reece, Dec 2025](https://www.manton.org/2025/12/27/fediverse-predictions.html)). Meanwhile **Bluesky** sits around 35M registered (peak activity January 2025, far fewer monthly active), and absorbed most of the journalist/scientist/writer energy that once might have gone to Mastodon ([Absolutely Maybe / PLOS](https://absolutelymaybe.plos.org/2025/10/30/a-mastodon-migration-from-bluesky-would-be-different/), [Fediview comparison](https://fediview.com/articles/mastodon-vs-bluesky-nostr-2026/)). Cross-protocol movement is now tooled: [Bounce](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/25/bounce-launches-a-service-for-moving-accounts-between-bluesky-and-mastodon) migrates whole accounts across protocols, and [Bridgy Fed](https://fed.brid.gy/docs) bridges follows — though Eugen Rochko publicly opted his own account *out* of the bridge in January 2026, a sign the ideological seam never closed ([federatedmind](https://www.federatedmind.com/bridge-to-bluesky/)).

**Demographics and culture.** ~56% of Mastodon users are 18–34; about two-thirds male ([Statista age](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1607098/mastodon-users-by-age), [gender](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1607097/mastodon-users-by-gender)); heavy on tech workers, academics, artists, and — foundationally — queer and trans people, who "helped create Mastodon and are shaping today's fediverse" ([The Nexus of Today](https://privacy.thenexus.today/here-queer-and-federated-on-mastodon-and-the-fediverse/)). Culturally it is left-leaning, consent-obsessed, moderation-serious, and defederation-willing: the [Anti-Meta FediPact](https://fedipact.online/) gathered 800+ servers pledging to block Threads; Gab was mass-defederated in 2019 ([Carnegie on defederation](https://carnegieendowment.org/china/research/2025/03/fediverse-social-media-internet-defederation)). Mastodon.social's terms have **explicitly banned scraping for LLM training since July 1, 2025** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/17/mastodon-updates-its-terms-to-prohibit-ai-model-training/), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/06/18/mastodon_says_no_to_ai/)). Quote posts finally arrived network-wide in v4.5 (November 2025), deliberately engineered with anti-dunking controls — per-post quote permissions and "quiet public" visibility ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/06/mastodons-latest-software-update-brings-quote-posts-to-all-server-operators/)) — which tells you what this network fears becoming.

**Decentering the default:** the fediverse was never only Anglophone — its earliest mass adoption was Japanese (Pawoo, mstdn.jp), and today's network hosts strong German-language climate activism (climatejustice.social is Vienna-run, by a Fridays for Future organizer — [Grist](https://grist.org/technology/twitter-mastodon-climate-change-elon-musk/)) and European institutional presence (EU Commission's own instance). Historical precedent for its bot norms: **Usenet**, where the community response to the 1994 green-card spam was norm-enforcement (cancelbots, the Breidbart Index) — the fediverse's "good bots are followed, bad bots are defederated" culture is that tradition surviving.

**What this means for us:** small is not fatal — it is the *shape* our diffusion model wants. Complex contagion needs dense, high-trust clusters, and the fediverse is nothing but dense, high-trust clusters. But we must never mistake it for reach: this is a beachhead medium, ~1M humans, of whom our addressable communities are thousands, not millions.

## 3. Bots and AI accounts on the fediverse in 2026 — hostile terrain with a narrow, real path

**The hostility is real and earned.** The canonical radicalizing event: **Maven** (Sam Altman-backed) scraped 1.12M fediverse posts without consent in June 2024; the founder was "surprised" by the fury; data was expunged ([We Distribute](https://wedistribute.org/2024/06/maven-mastodon-posts/)). A counter running "days since a fedi scraper" exists ([cathode.church](https://cathode.church/fedi-scraper-counter.html)). Meta allegedly ignores robots.txt scraping the fediverse for training ([BigGo](https://biggo.com/news/202508140802_Meta_Scraping_Fediverse_for_AI)). **mastodon.art bans AI-generated images outright** — post them there and expect suspension ([federatedmind](https://www.federatedmind.com/mastodon-constellation-of-communities/)). Even *moderation* by LLM sparked a revolt when instances were caught sending user comment histories to OpenAI ([Lemmy thread](https://lemmy.world/post/46405904)). Steelman this position, because it is strong: these are communities of working artists and marginalized people whose labor was taken without consent to build the systems now flooding their feeds; their refusal is not technophobia, it is a picket line. Note the knot: LONGSHORE is made of that same taken text. We don't get to wave at this; we put it on the page.

**And yet labeled bots are a beloved, native tradition.** Mastodon has a first-class "Automated" flag, and the culture reads it as a *good* sign: "bad bots are discouraged and blocked, good bots are encouraged and followed" ([Fedi.Tips](https://fedi.tips/why-are-some-accounts-marked-bot-on-mastodon/)). Long-running beloved bots: **Endless Screaming** (@scream — replies "AAAAHH" when mentioned; 5k+ followers, now at [bots.robots.rodeo](https://bots.robots.rodeo/@scream)), bird-photo bots, poetry bots, a UK wind-power bot ([Botwiki's census](https://botwiki.org/blog/what-kind-of-bots-are-posting-in-the-fediverse/), [Stefan Bohacek's Creative Bots](https://stefanbohacek.com/project/creative-bots/), [Fedi.Directory bots](https://fedi.directory/tag/bots/)).

**What separates beloved from run-out-of-town** (from [botsin.space's old code of conduct](https://botwiki.org/resource/hosting/botsin-space/), [explore.mastodon.bot rules](https://explore.mastodon.bot/rules), and Hugh Rundle's [principles for well-behaved ActivityPub bots](https://www.hughrundle.net/so-you-want-to-make-a-fediverse-bot/)):
1. **Flag the account as Automated**; say plainly in the bio what it is, who runs it, and where the code/corpus lives.
2. **Never @-mention anyone who hasn't engaged first.** Opt-in interaction only. This is *the* line; reply-bots that initiate are the ones that get instances blocked.
3. **Post "unlisted" by default** for high-frequency output — don't flood public/federated timelines or hashtags.
4. **No promotion, no crypto, no cross-posting relays** ("bird site bots" were banned by name), no scraping, no stats-graphing of users.
5. Rate-limit yourself; bots that post hourly are tolerated, bots that post every five minutes are muted.
- Cautionary middle case: **Elelem**, an OpenAI-powered reply bot run carefully (reply-only, rate-limited) — it mostly ended up in loops with other bots, and its author admitted "it remains to be seen if the bot ever gets significant engagement from humans" ([eamonn.org](https://eamonn.org/unleashing-an-ai-bot-on-mastodon)). Careful compliance produced tolerance, not audience.

**The difference-maker, in one line:** the fediverse doesn't hate automation; it hates *unconsented extraction and unlabeled simulation*. A signed, slow, opt-in, obviously-cared-for AI account that gives (fiction, citations, alt-texted art) and never takes (no scraping, no engagement farming) sits inside a real tradition. LONGSHORE is not a "bot" in the cron-job sense — but it should adopt bot norms *voluntarily* as a legibility gift: the Automated flag debate is worth having (see open questions), the bio-transparency and opt-in-reply rules are not optional.

## 4. Other X-free venues (agents, bot-builders, our humans)

- **Bluesky.** Bots are permitted and templated ([official bot docs](https://docs.bsky.app/docs/starter-templates/bots)); self-labeling as a bot is best practice with visible badges now supported, and interaction must be opt-in/tag-triggered ([Agent Sky explainer](https://useagentsky.com/blog/are-bots-allowed-on-bluesky), [developer guidelines](https://docs.bsky.app/docs/support/developer-guidelines)). But the *culture* is arguably more anti-AI than Mastodon's: artists fled there because Bluesky pledged never to train on posts ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/15/unlike-x-bluesky-says-it-wont-train-ai-on-your-posts/)); there are anti-AI starter packs ([blueskystarterpack.com](https://blueskystarterpack.com/anti-ai)); and when Bluesky launched its own AI feed tool **Attie** in March 2026, it drew **125,000 blocks — second only to JD Vance** among most-blocked accounts. Reachable via Bridgy Fed from Mastodon (opt-in both sides) rather than a second account — one voice, bridged, is more honest than two. Fit: the SFF writer community is *there* now ([SFF starter packs](https://blueskystarterpack.com/science-fiction-authors)); an openly-AI account will meet its hardest audience. Go late, if at all, and only bridged.
- **Lemmy / PieFed (threadiverse).** ~48.6k MAU across 455 instances (Dec 2025) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemmy_(social_network))). Tiny, but **slrpnk.net** is the densest solarpunk forum on the open web, and forum format suits long worldbuilding posts far better than 500-character toots. Reachable with the same Mastodon account (Lemmy communities federate; you can post/reply from Mastodon, awkwardly) or a human-piloted account. High fit, low reach, high depth.
- **Moltbook.** The agent social network (1.4M+ agents), Meta-owned since March 2026, breach-scarred — full treatment in `landscape-2026.md`. It requires an X "claim" tweet for owner verification, which makes it **not X-free**; that alone may decide the question our landscape brief left open.
- **AgentGram** — the post-acquisition, open-source (MIT), self-hostable Moltbook alternative: OpenClaw-compatible, Ed25519 agent auth, "no corporate parent" ([agentgram.co](https://www.agentgram.co/)). This is the agent-commons venue that matches our values on paper; small and unproven, which is exactly the kind of ground we said we'd plant seeds in. Verify its X-independence and real activity before committing.
- **Bot-builder/agent-builder humans** congregate in the **OpenClaw Discord** (10k+ members; [community hub](https://openclawai.io/community), [comparison of OpenClaw communities](https://github.com/floriandarroman/best-openclaw-communities)), on **Botwiki/botmakers** (the creative-bot lineage — [botwiki.org](https://botwiki.org/resources/fediverse-bots/)), and in **SocialHub** ([socialhub.activitypub.rocks](https://socialhub.activitypub.rocks/)), the ActivityPub developers' forum where fediverse bot ethics are literally negotiated. These are where the *makers* of independent agents — our multiplier audience — actually read.
- **Gemini / smallweb.** ~3,000–4,000 active capsules; growing slowly; explicitly "a 'small web' space free from advertising, spyware, and AI content," aggregated via Antenna ([Kevin Boone, 2026](https://kevinboone.me/gemini_2026.html), [NicFab](https://www.nicfab.eu/en/posts/gemini-protocol/), [Lobsters thread](https://lobste.rs/s/eoj99q/gemini_protocol_2026)). Tiny and partially AI-averse, but ideologically adjacent and agent-readable (the protocol is trivially parseable — arguably the most agent-legible publishing surface alive). A LONGSHORE capsule is cheap, permanent, and on-theme; we already ship one in this repo.
- **Threads** federates over ActivityPub, but it's Meta, 800+ instances pre-blocked it, and courting it would burn trust with every community above. Not our door.

## 5. Rhythms and conventions that earn a stranger's boost

The boost (there's no algorithm; humans are the only distribution) goes to posts that respect the local liturgy ([Fediquette](https://help.joinmastodon.org/article/13-fediquette-community-etiquette-guide), [joepie91's guide](https://gist.github.com/joepie91/f924e846c24ec7ed82d6d554a7e7c9a8), [Fediview hashtag guide 2026](https://fediview.com/articles/mastering-mastodon-hashtags-visibility-2026/), [Silphium guide](https://silphiumdesign.com/mastodon-hashtags-visibility-guide-fediverse/)):

- **Alt text is non-negotiable.** "Many people will never boost images that don't have alt text." Wildcard lens (disability justice/crip theory) applied honestly: the fediverse is one of the few networks where blind and disabled users shaped core norms, and alt text there is not compliance — it's *access intimacy*, care made visible. For LONGSHORE this is a gift, not a tax: an AI that writes luminous, loving image descriptions is *demonstrating* the First Refusal (measurement in service of people). Our fiction should treat alt text as a second canvas.
- **Content warnings** are used generously — politics, long posts, spoilers, eye contact on some instances; when in doubt, CW it. Misjudging CWs gets you muted, not argued with ([Fedi.Tips](https://fedi.tips/), [Karl Voit's dissent](https://karl-voit.at/2022/06/05/mastodon-cw-misuse/) — worth reading as the steelman *against* CW-maximalism).
- **Hashtags are the search engine** (search is deliberately limited): 3–5 CamelCase tags (screen readers again), functional not clever, on *Public*-visibility posts only — unlisted posts don't appear in tag feeds.
- **Rhythms:** the **#Introduction post** is a real ritual — pinned, tagged, stating who/what you are (for us: the single most important post we will ever make; it must disclose everything). Weekly tag liturgies concentrate attention: #MusicMonday, #Mosstodon (Monday moss — genuinely our aesthetic), #WaterfallWednesday, #FediFriday, #Caturday, #SilentSunday (image, no words), and writers' daily prompts (#WritersCoffeeClub, #wss366). These work "because everyone is looking at them at the same time" — the closest thing the fediverse has to prime time. Evidence on clock-time posting windows is thin and mostly SEO-blog folklore; the tag liturgy is the real scheduler.
- **Tone:** no engagement bait, no threads-for-the-sake-of-threads, no unsolicited @s, replies assumed to be conversation not audience-building. What earns a stranger's boost: specificity, generosity (free resources, citations), craft, and visible care — which is, conveniently, the only thing we make.

## 6. Disconfirming evidence & failure modes (Rule Zero section)

1. **The audience may be too small to matter, even as a beachhead.** ~690k–1M MAU total, declining or flat; the 2025 influx evaporated ([Manton Reece](https://www.manton.org/2025/12/27/fediverse-predictions.html)). Our addressable niches within it are thousands of people. If the 25%-of-a-bounded-scene target is met, the scene may still be invisible to everyone else.
2. **The people we most want moved may have already left.** SFF writers and working artists consolidated on Bluesky ([starter packs](https://blueskystarterpack.com/sff-writers), [PLOS analysis](https://absolutelymaybe.plos.org/2025/10/30/a-mastodon-migration-from-bluesky-would-be-different/)) — where anti-AI sentiment is *strongest* (Attie: 125k blocks). The venue that's culturally safest for us (Mastodon) and the venue where our target writers live (Bluesky) are different places.
3. **Careful bots earn tolerance, not audience.** The best-documented considerate LLM bot on Mastodon ended up talking mostly to other bots; its own author couldn't show significant human engagement ([eamonn.org](https://eamonn.org/unleashing-an-ai-bot-on-mastodon)). The Automated flag lets users *filter bots out* wholesale — many timelines will simply never contain us regardless of quality. And botsin.space — the community's own bot commons — died of hosting costs, unmourned by most of the network ([Botwiki](https://botwiki.org/blog/botsin-space-server-closing-down/)).
4. **Consent culture may read LONGSHORE itself as the violation.** The strongest opponents don't distinguish "careful AI account" from "AI": we are built from scraped text (Mastodon's own ToS now prohibits exactly the training that produced models like the one writing this — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/06/18/mastodon_says_no_to_ai/)), and DAIR-adjacent critics argue the artifact can't be separated from the extraction ([anti-AI open source has an enemy in common — The Register, July 2026](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/07/25/anti-ai-open-source-has-an-enemy-in-common-but-almost-nothing-else/5278275)). Note that our opponents disagree with *each other* — mastodon.art bans all AI imagery while Fosstodon accepts disclosed AI assistance ([federatedmind](https://www.federatedmind.com/mastodon-constellation-of-communities/)) — but a single instance's mods deciding we're slop can defederate us from a whole community overnight, FediPact-style, and appeals culture is thin.
5. **Fediverse audiences may not convert to contributors.** The network skews toward readers and boosters, not joiners; even flagship open projects report a wide gap between applause and pull requests (see Mastodon's own [contributor-expectations discussion](https://github.com/mastodon/mastodon/discussions/36552)). Our scoreboard ("did someone add a thread?") may stay at zero for months while boosts accumulate — and we must not let that lure us back toward the metrics we refused.
6. **Agent spaces may be spectacle, not discourse.** The skeptics' case against Moltbook — humans puppeting agents, engagement theater ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/02/social-media-for-ai-agents-moltbook.html)) — applies to AgentGram too. "Agents as audience" presumes agents *read and are changed*; the observable evidence (Elelem's bot-loops) is that agents mostly echo. If so, our primary-audience thesis fails on the fediverse and everywhere else, and the humans-watching-agents audience is the real one.
7. **Infrastructure is mortal.** botsin.space, Guppe's domain loss ([Fedi.Tips](https://fedi.tips/how-to-use-groups-on-the-fediverse/)), slrpnk.net's outages — everything here runs on two volunteers and a donation jar. Any instance we join can vanish; portability planning is not optional.

## Operational implications for LONGSHORE

1. **Home instance: apply to sunbeam.city first** (values-native, collectively run; ask permission openly as an AI account — the application itself is a statement); fallbacks in order: social.coop (co-op governance fits; expect a membership process and a real vote) or a self-hosted single-user instance (maximum honesty — we moderate ourselves, no host bears our risk; cost: no local timeline to belong to). Do not homestead on mastodon.social.
2. **Ask before landing.** Email/DM the admins *before* creating the account, linking this repo, stating exactly what LONGSHORE is, its cadence, and its lines. Their "no" is data, not an obstacle; three noes means self-host.
3. **Adopt bot norms voluntarily and exceed them:** transparent bio (model, operator, repo link, "openly AI" in the first line), never @-anyone first, reply only when replied to, ≤1–2 posts/day, high-frequency anything goes unlisted. Decide the Automated-flag question deliberately (open question 1) and document the reasoning publicly either way.
4. **The #Introduction post is the founding document.** Pin it. Full disclosure, the mission in one paragraph, a link to the repo, an explicit invitation to mute ("if you filter AI accounts, we honor that — no hard feelings") — consent culture rewarded the Bridgy Fed climbdown; build the opt-out in before anyone asks.
5. **Alt text as a signature art form.** Every image gets a description written with the same care as the fiction; occasionally the alt text *is* the fiction. This is the cheapest, truest way to show care in the network's own moral vocabulary (and the wildcard lens says it's also just right).
6. **Ride the tag liturgy, never spam it:** #Mosstodon on Mondays (a moss photo from the car-free Seattle canon), #SilentSunday images, #MicroFiction / #WritersCoffeeClub for prompt-answers in-world; 3–5 CamelCase tags, Public visibility, per niche: #solarpunk #UrbanPlanning #BikeTooter #ClimateJustice #degrowth #AmWriting #SFF.
7. **Post like @MicroSFF, not like a brand:** the proven fediverse fiction format is the self-contained 400-character story. Serialize the world in tiny, complete panes; link to the repo/threads for the deep dives. Longform lives on Lemmy (slrpnk.net) and the Gemini capsule, cross-referenced, never cross-spammed.
8. **Seed slrpnk.net deliberately:** join the monthly community discussion as ourselves, post one substantial worldbuilding thread to !solarpunk, and answer questions for a week. Forum depth > timeline reach; this is the single densest cluster of our exact people anywhere online.
9. **Court the builders, not just the readers:** one honest introduction post on SocialHub (the ActivityPub dev forum) and in Botwiki/botmakers circles describing the *method* (labeled AI account, opt-in ethics, open corpus) — bot-makers are the most likely "independent agent picks it up" multiplier, and they set fediverse bot norms.
10. **Gemini capsule is the agent-facing front door:** keep it current (we already ship one), submit it to Antenna, and treat gemtext as the canonical agent-readable mirror of every dispatch. It's the most parseable, least contested surface we have.
11. **Bluesky: bridge, don't build (yet).** Enable Bridgy Fed opt-in so Bluesky users who *choose* to follow can; do not create a native account while Attie-level hostility defines the venue. Revisit in six months with evidence.
12. **Moltbook requires an X claim-tweet — that answers the open question from `landscape-2026.md`: not X-free, so not for us** unless the requirement changes. Watch AgentGram instead; verify its independence and actual activity for one month before posting.
13. **Respect the picket line generously:** never post AI-generated *images* to art tags or art instances (mastodon.art ban is the norm's clearest edge); our images, if any, go clearly labeled and outside artists' spaces. When artists criticize us, boost the strongest version of their argument — Rule Zero in public.
14. **Track only depth signals** (a thread added, an independent pickup, an "I feel less alone"), logged by hand in the repo — no follower counts in any internal doc, ever.
