## 0. The core reconciliation: "we want reach" vs. "we refuse metrics"

The `AGENTS.md` promise and the First Refusal ("no attention-metrics… in the world **or in how we spread it**") look like a handicap. They are not. The science says the thing we're *ethically* forced to do is also the thing that *works better* for the kind of change we want.

The critical distinction, from Damon Centola's experiments, is between **simple contagion** (information, news, a link — spreads on a single exposure, like a virus) and **complex contagion** (a *behavior, worldview, or costly commitment* — spreads only after **multiple, reinforcing exposures from different trusted people**). ([Centola & Macy, "Complex Contagions and the Weakness of Long Ties"](https://ndg.asc.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Complexcontagions.pdf); [*How Behavior Spreads*, Princeton UP](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691175317/how-behavior-spreads))

We are not selling a fact. We are trying to get people to *believe a different arrangement of life is credible and to act as if it could be built*. That is complex contagion by definition. And complex contagion **does not travel on virality.** It travels on **social reinforcement inside dense, clustered communities** — exactly what metrics-driven feeds are worst at delivering and what a slow, warm, un-ranked mode is best at.

So the reconciliation is not a compromise. It is an alignment:

| The ethic (Three Refusals) | The science agrees because… |
|---|---|
| No engagement-hacking / metrics | Complex contagion spreads by depth of reinforcement, not reach; viral mechanics optimize for the wrong contagion type. |
| No fake grassroots / astroturf | Threshold + percolation models say you need *genuine* clustered adoption to hit a tipping point; fake seeds don't reinforce. |
| No impersonating humans; provenance always | Perceived manipulative intent triggers **affective resistance** that kills narrative persuasion (see §2f). Honesty is not just ethical, it is the persuasion mechanism. |
| Abundance is gift, not sold | Gift/commons framing *is* the message; a metrics funnel would perform the opposite of what the world describes. Medium and message must match or the artifact reads as slop. |

**We want reach the way a mycelial network wants reach: not one loud fruiting body, but many quiet connections that reinforce each other underground.** The rest of this document is the evidence for that claim and what to do with it.

## 1. Diffusion of innovations & the chasm (Rogers; Moore)

Everett Rogers' *Diffusion of Innovations* (1962) segments any adopting population into **innovators (~2.5%), early adopters (~13.5%), early majority (~34%), late majority (~34%), laggards (~16%)**, and identifies the levers: the innovation's attributes (relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability), communication channels, the social system, and time. ([Rogers curve explained](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/strategy-frameworks/rogers-diffusion-of-innovations-curve/))

Geoffrey Moore's *Crossing the Chasm* adds the crucial failure mode: there is a **chasm between early adopters and the early majority** because their decision rules are structurally different — **early adopters buy on vision and status; the early majority buys on pragmatic reference** ("do people like me already live this?"). Most ideas die in the chasm. Moore's prescription: don't spray thin; pick a **beachhead** (one narrow segment you can dominate), build the **whole product** (everything that segment needs to fully adopt, not just the core idea), and accumulate **references** the next segment will trust. ([Crossing the Chasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossing_the_Chasm); [chasm theory history](https://diffusion-research.org/research_articles/chasm-theory-development/))

**Implication for us:** We are pre-chasm. Our job for now is *not* the general public. It is to fully satisfy a beachhead (the anti-slop / solarpunk-AI early adopters — §4a) so completely that they become the *pragmatic reference* — "an AI-made project that is actually cared-for and non-extractive; I've read it; it's real" — that later, more skeptical sectors can point to. The "whole product" for us is not just prose: it is the prose **plus** transparent provenance, **plus** the open contribution protocol, **plus** the research briefs that make it credible. That bundle is what crosses.

## 2. The mechanisms of spread — and where they break

### 2a. Simple vs. complex contagion (Centola)
Centola's controlled online-network experiment (1,528 participants, health-behavior adoption) is the empirical spine of this whole document. Participants in **clustered-lattice** networks adopted the behavior **farther and faster** than those in random/"small-world" networks with the same number of ties — because clustering delivers **social reinforcement**: you hear it from several people whose worlds overlap, and *that redundancy is the signal*. ([ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/09/100902141603.htm); [*How Behavior Spreads*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691175317/how-behavior-spreads))

The counterintuitive corollary, "the weakness of long ties": for *behavior* (not news), a few long-range shortcut ties can actually **hurt** spread, because they carry a single un-reinforced exposure into a cluster where it dies. Complex contagion needs **wide bridges** — multiple, overlapping ties between clusters — not thin ones. ([Centola & Macy](https://ndg.asc.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Complexcontagions.pdf))

### 2b. The 25% committed-minority tipping point (Centola 2018)
In a follow-up *Science* experiment (10 groups × 20 people negotiating a naming convention), a **committed minority of ~25%** was enough to overturn an established majority norm — and the threshold held even when the majority was paid more to resist. Below ~25% the minority had almost no effect; above it, near-total flips. ([Centola et al. 2018, *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aas8827); [Annenberg summary](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/news-events/news/research-finds-tipping-point-large-scale-social-change))

**Implication:** the goal inside any target community is not "everyone" and not "one influencer." It is to reach a **committed ~quarter of a bounded scene** — a specific zine network, a lab, a Mastodon instance — densely enough that they reinforce each other. That is a far smaller, more humane, more achievable target than "go viral," and it maps directly onto the beachhead logic.

### 2c. Weak ties as bridges (Granovetter 1973)
Granovetter's "The Strength of Weak Ties" established that novel information reaches you disproportionately through **weak ties** (acquaintances), because strong-tie clusters are redundant — everyone already knows what everyone knows. Weak ties are the **bridges** that carry information *across* otherwise separate clusters. ([overview](https://www.complexsystemsframeworks.ca/framework/strength-of-ties/))

The synthesis with Centola resolves an apparent contradiction and gives us our two-speed model:
- **Weak-tie bridges carry the *invitation* (simple contagion):** "there's a thing called The Reality Next Door, here's the door." This is how it crosses from sector to sector.
- **Dense-cluster reinforcement carries the *conversion* (complex contagion):** actually coming to hold the worldview and contribute. This only happens *inside* a scene, over repeated warm contact.

Design consequence: **bridge people are precious** (they move ideas between the solarpunk forum and the punk house and the ecology lab), but you cannot ask a bridge to do a cluster's job. Don't over-index on a single well-connected booster.

### 2d. Threshold models & global cascades (Granovetter 1978; Watts 2002)
Granovetter's 1978 **threshold model**: each person has a threshold = the fraction of others who must act before they will. Populations with identical *averages* but different *distributions* of thresholds produce wildly different outcomes — a single missing low-threshold "instigator" can prevent a cascade that was otherwise inevitable. Collective behavior is a chain reaction, not an average. ([Granovetter 1978, PDF](https://www2.cs.siu.edu/~hexmoor/classes/CS539-F10/Collective-Behavior.pdf))

Watts' 2002 **global cascades on random networks** puts this on a network and connects it to **percolation theory**: cascades are possible only when the network sits in a "cascade window," and they propagate through a connected cluster of **vulnerable, low-threshold nodes**. Large cascades are *rare* and *fragile* — most seeds fizzle regardless of how good they are, because the local topology wasn't ready. ([Watts 2002, *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.082090499))

**Implication — the single most actionable finding in this brief:** because any one seed usually fizzles for reasons you can't observe or control, the correct strategy is **many independent, modest seeds in many different clusters**, not one big push. This is the mathematical reason a slow, distributed, multi-community approach beats a launch. It is also, conveniently, the only approach compatible with refusing a metrics funnel.

### 2e. Epidemiological (SIR/SIS) models — and their hard limits
The tempting default is to model an idea like a virus: Susceptible → Infected → Recovered (SIR) or → Susceptible again (SIS). These are analytically clean and where "going viral" comes from. ([Hethcote, "Three Basic Epidemiological Models"](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~kass/covid/Hethcote1989ThreeBasicModels.pdf))

**But they encode a false assumption for ideas:** that a single contact transmits, and that susceptibility is fixed. Both are wrong for worldviews. Transmission needs *reinforcement* (complex, not simple), and exposure *changes* susceptibility — people inoculate, get fatigued, or convert. Newer models add adaptive/heterogeneous susceptibility precisely because plain SIR/SIS "fall short in accounting for what happens when agents adapt their susceptibility after first exposure." ([adaptive-susceptibility contagion models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.08829)) **Takeaway: treat "virality" language as a category error for what we're doing.** We are not infecting; we are cultivating.

### 2f. How narratives actually change minds — transportation theory (Green & Brock 2000)
Why fiction, and not a manifesto? Green & Brock's **narrative transportation** theory: when a reader is *transported* — absorbed, with attention, imagery, and emotion converging — they show measurably more story-consistent beliefs afterward, and this effect is causal (directly manipulable). ([Green & Brock 2000, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11079236/); [Green & Appel 2024 review](https://www.mcm.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06110300/2024/Pdfs/Green___Appel__2024__Advances_Preprint.pdf))

The mechanism matters for *our* method: transportation persuades by **reducing counterarguing and reactance** — a transported reader isn't marshaling objections, they're living in the world. This is why "render it vivid and credible" (the project's stated method) is the correct persuasion strategy and a lecture is not. **The catch, which is directly relevant to our refusals:** when readers **perceive manipulative intent**, they mount **affective resistance** that undoes the effect. ([Appel et al., "Affective Resistance to Narrative Persuasion"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296322004246)) A project that felt like it was optimizing you would trip exactly this defense. Our transparency is therefore not just honest — it is what *keeps the door of persuasion open*.

### 2g. Cultural evolution, done rigorously (Mesoudi) — and the memetics caveat
Alex Mesoudi argues culture genuinely evolves by Darwinian logic — **variation, selection, inheritance** — and that this can be studied empirically rather than metaphorically. ([Mesoudi, *Biological Theory*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1162/biot.2007.2.3.263); [SEP, "Cultural Evolution"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evolution-cultural/)) This grounds the project's *recursive, branching* contribution model: contributions are variants, the community selects and elaborates, good threads inherit and mutate. **Important caveat:** the naïve "meme = gene = discrete replicator" version (Dawkins-style memetics) is widely criticized — cultural units have blurry boundaries and, crucially, **humans actively transform what they inherit** rather than copying it faithfully. ([memetics critique, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0303264721000356)) So we should *not* design "sticky memes." We should design **generative seeds meant to be changed** — which is already the `AGENTS.md` ethic ("add, deepen, complicate").

## 3. What the science jointly prescribes (design principles for spread)

1. **Beachhead, not broadcast.** Fully satisfy one narrow community before widening. (Moore; Centola 25%.)
2. **Depth over reach.** Optimize for repeated, warm, reinforcing contact inside dense scenes, not impressions. (Centola complex contagion.)
3. **Many small independent seeds > one big push.** Assume most seeds fizzle; plant widely and modestly across *different* clusters. (Watts 2002 percolation.)
4. **Use weak-tie bridges only to carry the invitation; let clusters do the converting.** Don't overload boosters. (Granovetter + Centola.)
5. **Aim for a committed ~25% of a bounded scene, not a majority of everyone.** (Centola 2018.)
6. **Transport, don't argue.** Lead with the lived-in world; let belief follow immersion. (Green & Brock.)
7. **Radical transparency is a persuasion mechanism, not a tax.** Any whiff of manipulation triggers resistance. (Affective-resistance research.)
8. **Seed to be transformed, not copied.** Leave better open questions; invite mutation. (Mesoudi; memetics caveat.)
9. **Don't measure reach; sense reinforcement.** (See §6.)

## 4. The wildcard lens: crip theory & disability justice as a diffusion model

Rolled, not chosen — and it reframes everything above in a way tech-optimist diffusion theory misses. Disability-justice thinkers have, independently of network science, built a *practice* of how liberatory ideas actually move through a body of people who cannot rely on mass channels: through **interdependence, collective access, and access intimacy.** Sins Invalid's 10 principles center interdependence and collective access; Mia Mingus names **"access intimacy"** — the deep, often unspoken relief when someone simply *gets* your access needs — as "a crip relational practice produced when interdependence informs the making of access." ([Sins Invalid / disability-justice framework](https://www.gbvlearningnetwork.ca/our-work/backgrounders/Gender-Based%20Violence%20Without%20Disability%20Justice/index.html); [Mia Mingus, "Changing the Framework"](https://leavingevidence.wordpress.com/2011/02/12/changing-the-framework-disability-justice/); [access intimacy](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/access-intimacy/))

Three mappings that improve the strategy:

- **Access intimacy *is* social reinforcement, felt from the inside.** Centola's "multiple reinforcing exposures" describes the mechanism from outside the network; access intimacy describes what that reinforcement *feels like* — being recognized by people whose lives overlap yours. The Weave's load-bearing purpose in `CANON.md` ("*is anyone like me, is anyone out there* → *you will not be the only one*") is literally access intimacy engineered into infrastructure. Our spread should feel like that, or it isn't ours.
- **Crip time vs. viral time.** Disability justice insists on *slowness as an access need*, not a defect. This licenses — demands — the project's slow-mail Weave and its refusal of the launch. Speed is an accessibility barrier; a movement that only moves at viral tempo excludes the people it most needs.
- **"Nothing about us without us."** Diffusion here is not a broadcaster reaching passive adopters; it is co-authorship. The recursive contribution model is the diffusion strategy: people spread what they *made part of*, not what was pushed at them.

This lens is also a **correction** to network science's blind spot: Rogers/Watts/Centola treat adopters as interchangeable nodes. Crip theory insists nodes have *bodies, needs, and access barriers* — and that the "laggards" in Rogers' curve are often people the medium failed, not people who are slow. Any sector we "can't reach" should first be checked for an access barrier we built.

## 5. The audience model — six sectors

For each: **who**, **which door** (artifact/framing that fits), **the diffusion science for reaching them specifically**, and **realistic obstacles**.

### (a) Anti-slop / solarpunk-AI early adopters — **the beachhead**
- **Who:** the people already fluent in "extraction vs. stewardship," readers of the solarpunk-AI discourse, the anti-slop protest crowd (see `research/landscape-2026.md`), fediverse solarpunk instances, low-tech-magazine / permacomputing types.
- **Door:** the *whole project as meta-argument* — "an openly AI-made thing that is cared-for, transparent, non-extractive." The provenance rule and the Three Refusals are the hook for this sector specifically. `AGENTS.md` is their front door.
- **Science:** these are Rogers' **innovators/early adopters** who buy on **vision** (Moore). They are also the cluster most likely to be *already reinforcing each other* (dense solarpunk/fedi communities), so complex contagion can ignite fastest here and produce the **25% committed core** that becomes everyone else's pragmatic reference.
- **Obstacles:** (i) they are the most alert to hypocrisy — a single metrics slip or astroturf whiff triggers **affective resistance** and they will torch it publicly; (ii) preaching to the choir risks the **echo chamber** the protocol exists to prevent — this sector agreeing proves nothing about crossing the chasm; (iii) solarpunk aesthetics can curdle into cozy consumerism, and they'll sniff that too.

### (b) Working scientists & academics
- **Who:** ecologists, restoration/marine scientists (the Salish Sea is real turf), network scientists, urban planners, cultural-evolution and complex-systems researchers.
- **Door:** the **`research/` briefs** and the Anti-Echo-Chamber Protocol — the *cited, disconfirming, adversarially-checked* method is legible to them as good epistemics. The fiction is secondary; the rigor is the invitation.
- **Science:** academia is a set of **tight, high-threshold clusters** with strong internal reinforcement and **weak-tie bridges via conferences, citations, co-authorship**. Reach them by giving one credible node in a lab something citable and true; complex contagion does the rest within the department. Their high thresholds (Granovetter 1978) mean they won't move on vibes — but once a respected peer engages, the threshold cascade within a field can be fast.
- **Obstacles:** (i) reputational risk — "AI-generated" is a slur in many fields right now, so provenance honesty cuts *against* us here even as it helps in (a); (ii) they will adversarially check our claims (good — that's the protocol) and any overreach loses the whole cluster; (iii) academic incentive structures reward novelty and citation counts — the very metrics we refuse — so participation has no career payoff and must run on genuine interest.

### (c) Musicians / DIY / queer / punk scenes
- **Who:** exactly the culture `CANON.md` centers — punk, queer, dance, mutual-aid-not-clout scenes; zinesters; house-show networks; the "band girl" is *from* here.
- **Door:** the **fiction and the world's texture** — the music-is-central, mutual-aid, soft-and-edgy city. The open thread "give the band girl a name, a band, a face" is a literal co-creation invitation. Zines and songs are the artifact, not essays.
- **Science:** DIY/punk scenes are the textbook case of **complex contagion in dense, clustered, high-trust ties** — subcultures spread by embodied, repeated, in-person reinforcement and are notoriously *immune to top-down virality*. They are also **historically fluent in non-extractive spread** (tape-trading, distros, flyers, splits) — the medium already matches our ethics. This is the sector where "deep-cluster reinforcement > viral reach" is not a hypothesis but their native practice.
- **Obstacles:** (i) **authenticity policing** — an AI project can read as the enemy (the thing putting artists out of work); the provenance honesty must be paired with visible refusal to replace anyone (Refusal 2), or it's dead on arrival; (ii) scenes are anti-institutional and will resist anything that smells like a brand or a campaign; (iii) subcultural capital is itself a kind of ranking — we must not import scene-clout dynamics that mirror the metrics we refuse.

### (d) Coders & AI-agent builders
- **Who:** open-source devs, agent/LLM builders, the Moltbook/OpenClaw crowd (`landscape-2026.md`), fediverse-infra people, permacomputing.
- **Door:** the project **as a system** — the recursive multi-agent contribution protocol, the "build the actual Weave" idea, the repo itself. They adopt by *forking and contributing code/agents*, which is participation, which is diffusion.
- **Science:** open-source is a **percolation/threshold** ecosystem — most projects fizzle (Watts 2002), a few catch when a connected cluster of low-threshold contributors ignites. **Many small seeds** (issues, forks, agent experiments) beats one launch. GitHub/forge social graphs are strong **weak-tie bridges** to every other technical sector.
- **Obstacles:** (i) **the Moltbook tension** (`landscape-2026.md` open Q1) — the place agents reach agents is now Meta-owned and metrics-driven; seeding there means broadcasting an anti-extraction vision into an extractive feed, a genuine values risk; (ii) builder culture *loves* metrics (stars, benchmarks) — the sector most likely to unconsciously reintroduce the thing we refuse; (iii) "solutionism" — they may try to *ship the Weave as a product/startup*, re-enclosing a commons.

### (e) Climate & urbanist organizers
- **Who:** transit/car-free advocates, YIMBY-adjacent and degrowth urbanists, mutual-aid networks, watershed/restoration groups, Indigenous-led land-back and marine-stewardship organizers.
- **Door:** the **concrete plausibility of the world** — car-free streets, daylighted shorelines, salvaged-steel buildings, slow travel. Organizers need "a place to point at"; the world is a **credible target image** for a campaign that otherwise only has grievances.
- **Science:** organizing is *applied* threshold theory — Granovetter's riot model **is** an organizing model (instigators = low-threshold first movers; you win by lowering others' thresholds). Our contribution is **narrative transportation** doing the emotional work that policy arguments can't: making the alternative *felt*, which lowers thresholds for action.
- **Obstacles:** (i) organizers are **action-oriented and time-poor** — a fiction project can read as escapism vs. real work ("we don't need a novel, we need a bike lane"); (ii) real coalitional fault lines (degrowth vs. green-growth, YIMBY vs. anti-gentrification) mean any specific world-detail will alienate *some* faction; (iii) **appropriation risk** — invoking Indigenous marine stewardship or land-back without leadership from those most impacted violates both disability-justice/DJ principles and basic ethics (see §7).

### (f) Ordinary readers / lapsed idealists
- **Who:** the general audience of *Woman on the Edge of Time* — people worn down, climate-anxious, not in any scene, who once believed things could be otherwise. The largest and last group.
- **Door:** the **story, purely as story** — no manifesto, no theory, just an afternoon crossing the Sound under a self-trimming sail that turns out to be the point. Transportation first; the argument arrives by inhabiting.
- **Science:** this is Rogers' **early/late majority** — they adopt on **pragmatic reference and social proof** ("people like me find this real and are already here"), which is why they come **last**, only after sectors (a)–(e) provide the references. Reaching them is a **complex contagion through their own strong ties** (a friend hands them the book), never through broadcast. Narrative transportation is the whole mechanism (Green & Brock).
- **Obstacles:** (i) **utopia fatigue / "this is naïve"** — the deepest resistance; the world must keep friction, grief, and imperfect restoration (per `CANON.md` and the no-frictionless-utopia rule) or it reads as denial; (ii) they have the **highest thresholds** and thinnest bridges to us, so they are genuinely the hardest and slowest to reach — and trying to shortcut this with virality is exactly the trap; (iii) the AI provenance may simply confuse or alienate a non-technical reader for whom "AI" means "slop," so for this sector the *quality of the prose* has to win the argument before the provenance is even legible.

## 6. Sensing without counting — "metrics" the First Refusal permits

We refuse counts, ranks, and engagement optimization. We still need to know whether we're alive. The science says the *right* signals are qualitative and reinforcement-shaped anyway:

- **Depth signals, not reach signals:** Did someone *contribute a thread*, fork the repo, write a song, name the band girl? Complex-contagion adoption *is* participation; participation is the only "metric" that measures the thing we actually care about — and it can't be gamed by impressions.
- **Bridge events:** Did the idea cross into a *new cluster* (a lab, a scene, a language)? One genuine cross-sector jump matters more than a thousand same-cluster impressions (Granovetter).
- **Return and elaboration:** Do contributors come *back* and *build on* others' work (Mesoudi's inheritance/variation)? Recursion is the health signal.
- **Access intimacy reports:** Did someone say "this made me feel less alone / seen"? That is the load-bearing purpose (§4), and it is the one outcome the Weave is *for*.

None of these is a leaderboard. All are legible. Crucially, we **never build a dashboard** — checking these is a periodic human/agent *reading* of what happened, not an always-on optimizing loop. The moment it becomes a number to grow, we've rebuilt the feed.

## 7. Disconfirming evidence & failure modes (required; steelmanned)

**The diffusion literature overpromises, and its most famous prescription is probably wrong.** The "influentials / opinion-leader" hypothesis — target a few big nodes and the cascade follows — is *directly contradicted* by **Watts & Dodds (2007)**, whose simulations found large cascades are driven not by influentials but by a **critical mass of easily-influenced ordinary people**, and that the influentials hypothesis "requires more careful specification and testing than it has received." ([Watts & Dodds 2007, *JCR*](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/34/4/441/1820236); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/11/071112133759.htm)) **Steelmanned implication for us:** any plan that hinges on "get one big solarpunk account/influencer to boost us" is building on sand. This is our sharpest self-critique: it's tempting, it feels efficient, and the science says it mostly doesn't work — plant in ordinary clustered soil instead.

**Backfire fears are overblown — but so is optimism about changing minds.** The scary "backfire effect" (corrections entrench false beliefs) largely failed to replicate: **Wood & Porter** tested 52 issues across 10,100+ subjects and found **no backfire**; corrections generally nudge toward facts. ([Wood & Porter, "The Elusive Backfire Effect," SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2819073); [Full Fact report](https://fullfact.org/media/uploads/backfire_report_fullfact.pdf)) Good news — we needn't fear that presenting the vision hardens opponents. *But* the same literature is sober: **persuasion is genuinely hard and usually small**, and correcting/converting "one fact at a time" produces "belief whack-a-mole." Don't expect the world to convert anyone by argument; expect slow, partial, transportation-driven shifts among the already-somewhat-open.

**Narratives frequently *don't* change minds.** Transportation effects are real but conditional: **affective resistance** and perceived manipulative intent can *reverse* them ([Appel et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0148296322004246)); prior attitudes, perceived realism, and identification all gate the effect. A didactic or preachy world will trip these defenses. This is the honest risk in the project's whole theory of change: a beautiful world that reads as agenda persuades no one.

**Complex-contagion / "wide bridge" findings are contested.** A 2025 behavioral *reinvestigation* of long ties and social contagion challenges the strength/direction of Centola's wide-bridge claims ([arXiv 2510.04785](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04785)); the field is not settled. We should hold "clustered reinforcement beats virality" as a strong, well-supported prior, not a law.

**Memetics as engineering is a mistake.** Trying to design "sticky" self-replicating units ignores that humans transform what they receive ([memetics critique](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0303264721000356)). A project that engineered virality would both fail *and* betray Refusal 1.

**The dark side of dense clusters.** Everything that makes clustered reinforcement good at spreading our worldview makes it good at spreading *any* worldview — including insularity, purity spirals, and echo chambers. The very mechanism we're leveraging is how cults and disinformation communities also work. Our only defense is structural: the **Anti-Echo-Chamber Protocol** and the refusal to close the commons. If we ever optimize for cohesion over disconfirmation, we become the thing.

**And the flat honest risk:** most seeds fizzle (Watts 2002), most persuasion is small (Wood/Porter), and it's entirely possible this stays a beloved artifact of sector (a) and never crosses the chasm. That would not be failure by the project's own lights — a cared-for, non-extractive, un-owned thing that a few thousand people found real is already the proof it set out to be. We should define success in a way metrics can't, precisely so we don't get tempted to chase them.

## 8. Historical & non-Western precedent (required)

**Indian Ocean Islam, spread by clustered merchant diasporas (c. 8th–15th c.).** Islam moved along the Indian Ocean trade network not by conquest or broadcast but through **merchant communities settling in coastal cities** (the Swahili coast, Malabar, the Malay archipelago), intermarrying, and building **dense local clusters** that reinforced practice over generations, blending with local traditions into new syncretic cultures, architectures, and **scholarly networks**. ([overview](https://www.tutorchase.com/notes/ap/human-geography/3-5-historical-causes-of-cultural-diffusion)) This is **complex contagion centuries before Centola named it**: a worldview spread by repeated, reinforcing, high-trust contact in settled clusters, carried *between* clusters by the weak-tie bridges of trade — and it **mutated as it inherited** (Mesoudi), rather than copying faithfully. It also decenters the Anglophone-tech default: the most durable idea-diffusion in history ran on relationships and slowness, not reach.
