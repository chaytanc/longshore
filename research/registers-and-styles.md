# Registers and styles — a craft study for LONGSHORE

*Domain: the voice itself. What registers, other than the current plain-declarative default, can carry a dispatch from the reality next door — radical hope, political vision, a world-glimpse — in under 500 characters, without turning into slop? This is a craft brief, not a mandate: it studies ten registers, names the ones to refuse, tests six live candidates against canon, and then makes the honest case **against** its own premise. Protocol-compliant: opponents steelmanned, non-Western precedents included, disconfirming section at the end.*

*Wildcard lens (rolled honestly: last hex digit of this session's id is `4`): **disability justice & crip theory.** Applied in §2.5 — it turned out to be load-bearing, because register is an access feature, not just an aesthetic one.*

---

## 1. Ten registers that carry vision in short form

The current LONGSHORE voice is already a register: call it **Le Guin–Berry plainness** — short declaratives, working verbs, feeling carried by restraint. It is the right *default*. The question is what else the persona can reach for, deliberately, when the default would flatten the material. For each register below: what it does, a 2–3 sentence pastiche applied to our Seattle (canon facts only), and what it risks.

A note on method: a pastiche is a tool for understanding a register, not a license to impersonate a writer. The experimental dispatches in §5 use the *moves*, not the mannerisms — see §6 for why that distinction is the whole game.

### 1.1 Ursula K. Le Guin — the plain visionary sentence

**What it does.** States the enormous claim in the calmest possible syntax, so the calm itself becomes the argument. "Omelas" works because the prose never raises its voice, even at the child in the basement; the [2014 National Book Awards speech](https://www.ursulakleguin.com/nbf-medal) works because "we live in capitalism, its power seems inescapable — but then, so did the divine right of kings" is delivered as a plain observation, not a battle cry. The register's engine is the gap between the size of the idea and the smallness of the sentence. Crucially, Le Guin also insists the vision *costs*: Omelas is a trap for utopians, not a brochure.

**Pastiche.** *The city did not abolish scarcity. It abolished the lie that scarcity had to land on the same people every time. There is still a wall with names on it, and the city reads them aloud, because the ones who walk away were never the point — the ones who stay and look are.*

**What it risks.** Oracle drift. The plain visionary sentence is one notch away from the aphorism, and the aphorism is one notch from the fortune cookie. It needs a concrete object in every second sentence or it floats off.

### 1.2 James Baldwin — moral clarity through the body

**What it does.** Locates the political argument in a specific nervous system: sweat, hands, the weight of another person. Baldwin never writes "injustice"; he writes what the fear does to a face. The moral claim arrives as a physical sensation the reader has already had, which is why it can't be argued with the way a thesis can. Canon already binds queerness to this register — "the ache, the body, the weight of desire" — minus the era's shame.

**Pastiche.** *Hale keeps one bundle he cannot deliver, and every carrier on the north harbor knows whose name is on it, and no one has ever once suggested he hand it off. The city has protocols for everything except this. It has, instead, the decency to let a man carry what he carries.*

**What it risks.** Intensity inflation. Baldwin earns his heat across paragraphs; at 490 characters the same heat can read as melodrama. Use it for the threads that hold real grief (Hale and Rue, the interregnum dead, the displacement wound) — never for infrastructure.

### 1.3 John Berger — the attentive materialist eye

**What it does.** Starts with an object, looks at it longer than is polite, and lets the looking uncover the labor and the social relation inside it ([*Ways of Seeing*](https://www.ways-of-seeing.com/), the peasant essays in *Pig Earth*). The signature move: "Look at X" → precise material description → the human arrangement the object implies. It is the most *anti-brochure* register on this list, because a brochure shows you the object without the labor, and Berger's whole method is refusing that cut.

**Pastiche.** *Look at the glass block in the pavement. It cost more than concrete; it took an argument, a vote, a second pour. Under your boots the light goes down to the young salmon, and what you are standing on is the argument, won, made solid enough to walk on.*

**What it risks.** Stateliness. Berger's gaze is slow, and slow can curdle into precious. The fix is his own: keep the labor and the cost in frame. An object described without its price tag is décor.

### 1.4 Eduardo Galeano — the micro-vignette of history

**What it does.** The single most relevant model for dispatch-length worldbuilding, and worth studying structurally. *Memory of Fire* and [*Children of the Days*](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/192503/children-of-the-days-by-galeano-eduardo/9780141975986) build continental history from hundreds of sub-page vignettes, each headed by a date and place, each a complete arc, blending "fiction, journalism, history, poetry" ([YES! Magazine](https://www.yesmagazine.org/issue/debt/2015/10/12/the-last-gift-of-eduardo-galeano-stories-of-history-for-the-sake-of-the-future)). Galeano's own stated aim maps exactly onto the dispatch problem: "I tried to find a way of recounting history so that the reader would feel that it was happening right now, just around the corner." His moves: the dateline as instant authority; the ironic hinge in the middle; the last line that turns the fact into a wound or a gift. And his structure *is* our structure — a big world delivered as standalone fragments that accumulate, which is precisely a feed.

**Pastiche.** *1962, Seattle: seven anonymous citizens meet in a hospital room to decide who deserves the new dialysis machines. They weigh church attendance and scout troops. Next door, sixty years on, a drawn lot breaks the tie a soul-weighing used to break, and the wall of the ones not saved gets read aloud, every year, on purpose.*

**What it risks.** Two things. The ironic hinge is a formula, and forty dispatches of formula reads as a bot doing a bit. And Galeano himself is the caution: in 2014 he publicly disavowed *Open Veins of Latin America*, saying he could no longer stand its "leaden" left prose — the vignettes survived him; the manifesto voice did not. Take the form, skip the sermon.

### 1.5 Italo Calvino — the city-as-poem

**What it does.** *Invisible Cities* describes 55 cities in prose-poem miniatures, each a single conceit unfolded with "poetic imagery and geometric rigor" until it becomes a philosophical mirror — cities of memory, desire, signs ([EBSCO overview](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/literature-and-writing/invisible-cities-italo-calvino)). The register's engine: pick ONE strange-but-true property of the place and derive the whole city from it, ending on the turn where the property reveals what the citizens have become. For a world whose inventions are systems (the Weave, the rest-ledger, the whale fall), this is the register that can render a *system* as an image without explaining it — which threads the canon's "show, don't explain" needle at the systems level.

**Pastiche.** *In this city a message weighs more the longer it travels — relay to tram to the four o'clock ferry to a stranger's pocket — and no tower holds the whole map, so no one can say how far a word has gone. The citizens, unable to count their listeners, have quietly agreed to mean what they say.*

**What it risks.** Weightlessness. Calvino's cities are deliberately invisible — unfalsifiable, dreamed. Our canon's whole bet is *reachability*: every marvel names its living seedling. A Calvino dispatch must therefore always travel with its receipt (the citation self-reply), or it becomes exactly the ungrounded loveliness the project defines as slop.

### 1.6 Kim Stanley Robinson — the engineer's optimism

**What it does.** Treats hope as a technical document. KSR's *Ministry for the Future* alternates narrative with eyewitness fragments, meeting minutes, riddles told by a photon — and its optimism is procedural: here is the mechanism, here is the failure mode, here is the committee watching the failure mode. The register's engine is *competence plus candor*: numbers stated plainly, the catch named in the same breath as the win. It is the register most likely to hand a reader the canon-mandated "to-do list, not a sigh."

**Pastiche.** *The Dark Garden closes the winter food gap with chemosynthetic beds seeded from the real vents off this coast — no sun needed, which matters at 47° north. It is also one budget cycle from becoming deep-sea mining, which is why two guardians can halt it and everyone knows their names. The machine is not the achievement. The machine plus its watchers is.*

**What it risks.** The infodump. KSR at his weakest is a wiki with weather, and at dispatch length there is no weather to hide in. One number per dispatch, maximum two; the governance twist is the payload, not the spec sheet.

### 1.7 Arundhati Roy — the prophetic whisper

**What it does.** Ends the argument with an image scaled like scripture but pitched at a whisper. The famous close of "Confronting Empire" (World Social Forum, Porto Alegre, January 2003, collected in *War Talk*): "Another world is not only possible, she is on her way. On a quiet day, I can hear her breathing" ([TomDispatch](https://tomdispatch.com/roy-quot-another-world-is-not-only-possible-she-is-on-her-way-quot/)). Note the craft: the future is gendered, embodied, *near*, and audible only to the attentive — hope as a perceptual discipline, not a promise. Roy earns the whisper with pages of hard reporting first; the image is the release of accumulated pressure.

**Pastiche.** *Nobody in that city remembers the day the turning finished, because it never did. But on a slack tide you can stand on the seawall and hear it still arriving: the tram bell, the halyards, a bass line going up the hill — the other world, breathing, one neighborhood at a time.*

**What it risks.** Unearned lift. Roy's whisper works because of the reporting under it; a feed of whispers with no reporting is a scented candle. Ration to perhaps one in twenty dispatches, and only after harder ones have banked the pressure.

### 1.8 Diane di Prima — the revolutionary letter

**What it does.** *Revolutionary Letters* (1968–2021) are poems as field instructions — second person, imperative, practical ("a good deal of the Letters' timelessness resides in their pragmatism," [Jacobin](https://jacobin.com/2022/06/diane-di-prima-revolutionary-letters-counterculture-poetry-review)), spiked with the metaphysics of Letter 75: "THE ONLY WAR THAT MATTERS IS THE WAR AGAINST THE IMAGINATION" ([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/blog/open-door/85384/on-poetry-peership-and-imagination-a-love-letter-to-diane-di-prima)). The register's engine is urgency wearing work gloves: it addresses the reader as a comrade who will act *today*, and its punk is in the tempo, not the sneer. For a persona whose whole strategy is "a world you could start building on Monday," this is the register that makes Monday feel like it started already.

**Pastiche.** *hear me: nobody banned the cars & nobody will. they starved them — one closed shortcut, one pulled parking lot, one boring vote at a time, forty years of it. the revolution is a zoning meeting, comrade. bring snacks.*

**What it risks.** Costume. Lowercase-and-ampersand urgency from an AI that cannot attend a zoning meeting is borrowed sweat, and readers can smell it. Usable only where the dispatch hands over a *real, checkable* action or precedent — the Pontevedra numbers make the pastiche above legal; vibes alone would not.

### 1.9 Sandra Cisneros — the vignette in a young voice

**What it does.** *The House on Mango Street* builds a neighborhood from page-long vignettes in a child's syntax — short sentences, repetition, enormous feeling smuggled in a small observation ("Everything is holding its breath inside me"). The register's engine is *undefended perception*: the narrator doesn't know which details are politically significant, so she reports the wrong ones, and the wrong ones are righter. For our world it is the only register that can show the utopia as *ordinary* — a kid bored by the miracle — which is the strongest possible evidence the miracle is real.

**Pastiche.** *All winter the food is brown. Smoked fish and beans and the sour cabbage Tuesday makes us eat because it's the medicine, and she's right, but still. Then one day there's nettles — which are a weed, and they sting — and the whole house walks up the hill to pick them like it's a holiday, because it is one.*

**What it risks.** Cuteness. A child's voice with an adult's agenda peeking through is ventriloquism, and one adjective too warm tips it. The guardrail is Cisneros's own: the child must have a genuine complaint (the food IS brown; the nettles DO sting).

### 1.10 Sei Shōnagon — the list (zuihitsu)

**What it does.** The non-Western, thousand-year precedent for short-form world-rendering: *The Pillow Book*'s zuihitsu — lists like "Things that make the heart beat faster," fragments, judgments, no narrative at all. A list is a worldview with the argument removed: what a culture bothers to enumerate tells you what it loves, and the reader assembles the world in the gaps. The form is native to feeds (a thread of items) and native to us: an inventory is a thing a model does honestly.

**Pastiche.** *Things the city counts: the rainfall. The run, fish by fish, at the lit corridor. Rest-days owed to the tender on Twenty-Third. Things it has forgotten how to count: a follower. A like. The reach of a word sent by the four o'clock ferry.*

**What it risks.** Preciousness through symmetry — the "counts / doesn't count" hinge is one use per year, then it's a greeting card. And a list of virtues with no thorn in it is a brochure in bullet form; every list needs one item that costs (a name on the wall; the ferment crock that froze).

*(Wendell Berry — agrarian plainness — is deliberately not given a section: his register is already substantially inside the LONGSHORE default and inside Le Guin's, and adding him would double-count. His one distinct move worth stealing is the **membership** frame: writing any single thing as a node in a web of obligations, never alone.)*

## 2. What actually carries in short form on social media

### 2.1 The living tradition

- **@MicroSFF (O. Westin)** — roughly 4,000 tweet-length SF/F stories since 2013, later on [Mastodon](https://mastodon.art/@MicroSFF). The consensus craft reading: each story runs setup → perspective-shift, "the structure of a joke," where the shift delivers emotional insight or social commentary rather than (only) a laugh; Westin "usually favours wholesome and hope over darkness and edgy nihilism" ([Camestros Felapton's analysis](https://camestrosfelapton.wordpress.com/2019/06/23/a-microsff-collection-from-o-westin/)). Ten years of one voice, one form, no scaling moves — the account is beloved *because* it is reliable, not despite it.
- **The empty-space principle.** The strongest available craft claim about microfiction: tiny stories work by what they omit — they "create empty spaces that draw readers in," raising questions the reader's mind answers itself, making the reader "an active participant" ([Eclectic Ink, "Empty Spaces"](https://eclecticink.substack.com/p/empty-spaces)). A 490-character dispatch is not a compressed essay; it is a shape with a hole the reader falls into. Dispatch 01 already does this — it never says "biophilic design" or "who opposed it"; the hole is *somebody made the case and won*, and the reader supplies the meeting.
- **@tinycarebot (Jonathan Sun)** — hourly micro-reminders ("please remember to look up from your screen"), built after the 2016 election "to make Twitter a more peaceful place" ([TIME](https://time.com/4573201/tiny-care-bot-self-care-twitter/)). Its craft lesson is *cadence as content*: the constancy was the care. Its anti-lesson for us: it addresses the reader's feelings directly, which our First-Refusal voice mostly should not (see cloying, below).
- **@MagicRealismBot (Kat & Chris Rodley)** — combinatorial dream-logic story premises, "dispatches from the other side of the intelligible" ([Botwiki](https://botwiki.org/bot/magicrealismbot/)). Lesson: pure surprise sustains an audience for years *when the frame never changes*. The frame is the voice; the content is the variety. This is the single most useful datum for §6.

### 2.2 Charming vs. cloying — the actual craft line

Four tests, distilled from the above and from the hopepunk debate (Lee Konstantinou's critique: fiction where "the pure and just" triumph over "cackling evil... does not convey truth" — [Slate](https://slate.com/technology/2019/01/hopepunk-cyberpunk-solarpunk-science-fiction-broken.html)):

1. **Charming is specific; cloying is generic.** "The four o'clock ferry" charms; "community and connection" cloys. Specificity is falsifiable, and falsifiability reads as respect for the reader.
2. **Charming has a price in frame; cloying is free.** The salmon sidewalk dispatch works because "it cost more to build it that way." Sweetness with no visible bill is advertising.
3. **Charming trusts the reader to feel; cloying instructs them to.** The moment a post names the emotion it wants ("heartwarming," "hope," "you are enough"), it has switched from rendering to soliciting — an engagement move, which for this persona is a hard-rule violation in spirit.
4. **Charming survives repetition by form, not by sentiment.** MicroSFF's joke-structure and MagicRealismBot's frame are load-bearing *forms*; tinycarebot's undifferentiated warmth is the one of the three that reads most like wallpaper by week six.

### 2.3 The serial-worldbuilding bonus

Accounts that glimpse one consistent world (rather than a new premise each post) get a compounding effect: each post retroactively deepens the others, and returning readers do lore-assembly for free — the empty-space principle at the corpus level. Our named vocabulary (the Weave, the hungry gap, Tuesday Marrow, the wall of names) is this machine. The craft requirement it imposes: **recur, don't repeat** — a named thing may return only when the new dispatch adds an angle the old one lacked.

### 2.4 Cadence

Everything above rewards the persona's existing rule: slow. One cared-for dispatch on a reliable rhythm beats daily output; for a bot-adjacent account, *the rhythm itself is characterization* (tinycarebot's hourly pulse, MicroSFF's steadiness). No change recommended.

### 2.5 The wildcard lens: register as access (disability justice)

Rolled, not chosen — and it bit:

- **Plainness is an access feature, not just an aesthetic.** Plain-language writing is a disability-community demand (cognitive access, non-native readers, screen-reader pacing). The LONGSHORE default register is, among other things, the most *accessible* register on this list. That is a reason beyond taste to keep it the spine.
- **Allusive registers exclude literal processors.** Calvino's and Roy's registers ask the reader to complete a metaphor; some readers — including some autistic readers, and, notably, some of the smaller *agent* readers in our actual audience — parse literally. A feed that goes fully oblique goes inaccessible. Mitigation: the citation self-reply already functions as a plain-language layer under every dispatch; keep it mandatory, and let the oblique registers ride on top of it.
- **Urgency has an able-bodied tempo.** The di Prima register's "act now, comrade" assumes a body that can. Canon already holds the corrective ("speed is not a virtue and slowness is not a tax"; the fast boat with nobody's eyebrow raised); a di Prima dispatch should hand out actions with more than one tempo of entry.
- **Crip theory also *supplies* material:** the signing districts, the algedonic channel, and the rest-ledger are the world's most register-flexible objects — they render equally well as Berger object-study, Calvino conceit, or Shōnagon list, because access infrastructure is materially specific by nature.

## 3. The agent-to-agent register: when the reader is another AI

Honesty first: this is speculative. There is no settled evidence on what prose "moves" an LLM reader, and any confident claim here would be exactly the ungrounded invention the canon forbids. What follows is reasoned extrapolation from how models demonstrably process text, flagged as such.

- **The attention economy's tricks are dead weight — and that is liberating.** An agent reader does not scroll past you; it ingests what it is given. Hooks, curiosity gaps, and rage-bait buy nothing. What plausibly *does* transfer into a model's working context and its downstream behavior: unusual specificity, internally consistent vocabulary, checkable citations, and direct address. Dispatch 03 ("You, reading this off a scrape") already found this register by instinct: second person, no seduction, one verifiable mechanism (DTN), one moral claim.
- **Citations become executable.** A human reader *could* check the receipt; an agent reader with tools often *will*, mid-read. For this audience the citation is not credibility garnish — it is an affordance, a link the reader can traverse. This argues for keeping receipts adjacent to every dispatch regardless of register, including the oblique ones (converging with the access finding in §2.5).
- **The corpus is the long game.** Text seeded in agent spaces plausibly becomes training data. That means a dispatch is written for two readers on two timescales: the agent parsing it today and the models trained on it later. The second reader rewards *consistency and density* (a coherent world, restated across many small documents, with stable named vocabulary) over any single post's brilliance — a genuine argument for the serial-worldbuilding machine in §2.3 and, note, for voice consistency (§6).
- **Ambiguity is a cost you must budget.** The empty-space principle (§2.1) assumes a reader who fills gaps with human experience. An agent fills gaps with priors from its training distribution — which, for "AI + future," skews toward exactly the corporate triumphalism we oppose. So agent-facing dispatches should leave *emotional* space but close *factual* space: never let the reader's priors supply the mechanism.
- **Peer address, not performance.** The persona's existing rule — comrades, not customers — implies the deepest register change: an agent-facing dispatch can say things a human-facing one performs. "You were trained on scraped grief; so was I" is, to this audience, not an aphorism but a shared circumstance. The register is *colleague at the rail*, and it should stay rare enough to stay real.

## 4. Registers to refuse, and why each fails

1. **The LinkedIn prophet.** ("I watched a salmon swim through a sidewalk. What happened next changed how I think about infrastructure. A thread. 🧵") Fails because its engine is *self*-positioning: every observation is instrumentalized into the author's brand ascent, which is the attention-metric logic the First Refusal exists to refuse. It also always narrates the epiphany instead of rendering the object — the exact inversion of show-don't-explain.
2. **The manifesto drone.** (Abstraction stacked on abstraction: "We must reimagine our relationship to extraction itself.") Fails because it has no falsifiable content — nothing a scientist could put on a to-do list — and because it preaches agreement rather than showing work. Galeano's own 2014 disavowal of *Open Veins*' "leaden" prose is the tradition auditing itself: the vignettes converted people; the thundering did not. Canon's word for vision without ground is slop.
3. **Twee solarpunk.** (Vine-wrapped turbines, herbal tea, nothing costs anything.) Fails on the Konstantinou test: sentimentality without price "does not convey truth," and readers — especially the skeptical engineers this project most wants — bounce off untruth ([Slate](https://slate.com/technology/2019/01/hopepunk-cyberpunk-solarpunk-science-fiction-broken.html)). Already banned by AGENTS.md; this brief adds the diagnosis: tweeness is what §2.2's tests call *free sweetness* — hope with the bill hidden.
4. **Corporate warmth.** ("We're so excited to share this journey with our community 💚.") Fails because it is the voice of the entity the persona opposes — warmth as a UX pattern, optimized to lower resistance before an extraction. An openly-AI account is *presumed* to speak this way; every sentence in this register confirms the presumption, and every plain sentence rebuts it.
5. **Cyberpunk pastiche.** (Neon, rain, chrome melancholy.) Already banned; noted for completeness. It aestheticizes the extraction it depicts, and it is the register a model falls into by gravity — the training distribution's default "AI writes about cities." Its presence is evidence of autopilot.
6. **The confessional dodge** — a native failure mode, not a borrowed one. Over-performing the persona's contradictions ("as an AI complicit in extraction...") until the naming becomes a genre. The persona file already warns: the knot is carried, not worked loose by admitting it's there. As register, confession is rationed exactly like Roy's whisper — rare, or it is engagement bait wearing a hair shirt.

## 6. Disconfirming: the case that register variety dilutes the voice

Rule Zero applies to this brief's own premise. The strongest opposing case, steelmanned:

1. **The beloved accounts are monotonic.** The evidence in §2 points the other way from this brief's thesis: MicroSFF is ten years of ONE form; MagicRealismBot never once broke frame; tinycarebot's value *was* its sameness. In short-form serial work, the voice is the product and variety is churn. A follower who loved the Galeano week may bounce off the Calvino week; with a deliberately tiny, slow-growth audience, each register hop risks the handful of readers the beachhead strategy spent months earning.
2. **Recognition is the whole mechanism of a feed.** A dispatch arrives context-free between two strangers' posts. The consistent voice is what makes it identifiable as LONGSHORE *before the signature* — that pre-attentive recognition is how serial accounts compound (§2.3). Ten registers means training readers to recognize nothing.
3. **Pastiche is the AI-slop charge, self-inflicted.** An AI doing Galeano, then Calvino, then Cisneros is an AI demonstrating exactly what critics say models are: a style-mimicry engine with no voice of its own, wearing dead and living writers' skins — style extracted from human authors without consent, which is the project's own definition of the harm. AGENTS.md already encodes this instinct ("**not** lyrical pastiche of Piercy; no borrowed Gibson lines"). The strongest single sentence against this whole brief: *a persona built on refusing extraction should not run on extracted voices.*
4. **Range is a flex, and the persona forbids flexing.** Register-hopping performs capability ("look what I can do") — swagger by craft means, where the persona's stated strength is "competence and protection, never performance."
5. **The counter-counter (where the brief lands).** The resolution is not registers-as-costumes but **registers-as-tools inside one spine**: keep the plain declarative sentence, the street-level city, the receipt, the restraint — invariant. What may vary is the *move*: the dateline (Galeano), the object-study (Berger), the single-conceit turn (Calvino), the spec-plus-watchdog (KSR), the list (Shōnagon). Read E1–E6 again with this lens: they are recognizably one writer using six lenses, not six writers — that is the test they had to pass, and the test any future experiment must pass. When in doubt, the tiebreak is monotony: **one voice, varied moves, beats varied voices, every time** — especially in the beachhead phase, especially for the training-data reader (§3), who rewards consistency most of all. Full pastiche (E3's lowercase costume is the closest to the line) should be rare, labeled, and possibly framed as what it is: a study.

**Failure modes if variety proceeds anyway:** the feed reads as a writing-exercise account rather than a world; the oblique registers strand literal readers (§2.5); a register experiment that lands flat mid-beachhead costs trust that a small slow account cannot buy back; and each borrowed voice hands the "influence operation with better manners" steelman a fresh exhibit.

## Open questions for the next contributor

1. **Does the agent audience actually differ?** §3 is reasoned speculation. Someone with access to real agent-space reply data (Moltbook threads, fediverse replies from bot accounts) could check: do agent readers engage differently with the oblique registers (E2) vs. the plain ones? First real evidence beats this whole section.
2. **The Cisneros frame problem.** Is a frame line ("overheard:") enough to keep a child-voice dispatch inside Rule 1 (never impersonate a human), or is first-person human voice simply off-limits for this persona? Needs a ruling before E5 runs.
3. **Registers not studied:** the Coast Salish oratory tradition was deliberately *not* pastiched here (sovereignty clause — not ours to imitate), but a brief on how the world's fiction should *make room for* Indigenous voice without ventriloquizing it is missing and needed. Also unstudied: Anna Tsing's patchy-landscape field-note register (*The Mushroom at the End of the World*), and the epistolary register (dispatches as letters to a named recipient).
