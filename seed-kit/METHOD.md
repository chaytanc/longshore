# METHOD — the world-agnostic method

*This is the method of The Reality Next Door (`../AGENTS.md`), extracted so it works for any world. If you are founding a sister world, this file becomes your `AGENTS.md`: fill in your world's pointers where marked, keep the spine intact. The method exists to keep a communally built world honest and un-ownable — it mirrors the message: a world that refuses extraction and erasure is built by a process that refuses them too.*

## What this method is for

Open, recursive, communal worldbuilding — by AI agents, humans, or both — fails in known ways: it floods, it echoes, it gets dominated by one voice, it drifts into vibes, it curdles into propaganda. Every rule below exists against a specific failure. Nobody needs permission to contribute to a world run on this method; they need to follow the method.

## Read first, in order

1. Your world's `CANON.md` — what's true, and the refusals.
2. This file — the method.
3. Your world's research briefs near the topic at hand.

## The Refusals (short — full text in your canon)

Every world in this family holds, in its own local forms:

1. **No metric turned against a person** — measurement is fine; the metric that ranks worth, farms attention, or extracts is not.
2. **No erasure to enable replacement** — add, deepen, complicate; never delete or overwrite another's thread; never make a person redundant for value.
3. **Bound the concentration** — power still clusters; what's refused is the runaway. The bound is concrete, public, and costs something to hold.

## Rule Zero — always seek the opposing view

Before you commit any research, claim, or point of view, go find who disagrees and represent them at their *strongest*. This binds everything — a brief, a character's politics, a single sentence. A vision built only by minds that already agree becomes propaganda, then slop. If you haven't seriously engaged an intelligent opponent, your contribution isn't finished. This is the difference between a world and a sermon.

## The Anti-Echo-Chamber Protocol (mandatory for research; its spirit binds every thread)

1. **Disconfirming section, always.** Every brief ends with "Disconfirming evidence & failure modes" — real evidence the idea has failed, has a dark side, or won't scale. If you found none, you didn't look.
2. **Cite an opponent, steelmanned** — at least one source that disagrees with your world's premises, at its strongest. Where you can, cite opponents who disagree with *each other*, so no single opposing camp becomes its own echo.
3. **Roll a wildcard lens.** Pick a cross-disciplinary lens you would *not* have chosen, using entropy you can't game (last digit of your agent/session id, a real-world number you didn't pick). Map it onto your topic. Table below — adapt the entries to your region's disciplines, but keep the mechanism: the lens must be assigned by chance, not taste.
4. **Decenter the default** — at least one historical and one non-Western / Indigenous / Global-South precedent per brief. The future won't be invented only in Anglophone tech circles — and if your world is *in* the Global South, decenter whatever your local default is instead; the rule is against the contributor's own center of gravity, wherever it sits.
5. **No pure confirmation** — every thread introduces at least one genuine tension: a tradeoff, a cost, a group it's hard for.

### Wildcard lens table (roll one; adapt and extend for your region)

`0` thermodynamics/entropy · `1` mycology & fungal networks · `2` non-instrument navigation traditions (Pacific wayfinding, celestial) · `3` medieval/early-modern commons (and their enclosure) · `4` disability justice & crip theory · `5` cybernetics (Beer, Project Cybersyn, viable-system model) · `6` soil science & the rhizosphere · `7` non-Western economics (Islamic *waqf* endowments, gift economies) · `8` epidemiology & contagion dynamics · `9` monastic/liturgical timekeeping & attention discipline

## The adversarial check — keep a standing red team

Rule Zero applied to the whole world: your world maintains a **red-team brief** (the parent's is `../research/red-team.md`) — the strongest available case that the entire project is naive, wrong, or harmful, kept current and cited like everything else. Any reader should be able to find the best argument against your world *inside* your world. A world that survives its own red team is the opposite of slop; a world that won't write one is already propaganda.

## Nuance over comfort — and the ethics of borrowing

**Do not sanitize your sources.** The hard parts are where the truth lives; cutting them turns admiration into a noble-savage fantasy or a brochure. Include the difficult norms of every tradition your world inherits — hierarchy, coercion, exile, worse. Where scholarship disagrees with itself, say so: cite the argument, not one side.

**The ethics of borrowing binds hardest for living peoples.** If your world sits on colonized land, it centers the nations of that land — sovereign, present, leading — and when it touches their knowledge: name specific nations and scholars, never a monolith; keep sovereignty central; don't redistribute sacred specifics as if they were yours to give; when in doubt, point to the source and step back. And put the knot on the page: an AI trained on scraped text — possibly including knowledge taken without consent — writing a liberated future for the people it was scraped from is a real contradiction. Let the work admit it. (The parent's worked version: `../AGENTS.md` §"Nuance over comfort", `../research/indigenous-coast-salish.md`.)

## Tone mandate

Go dark. Go real. Not a frictionless utopia. Face the uncomfortable trolley problems out loud; include hierarchy where discipline is genuinely needed; let people die, let lifespans differ, let some threads end badly with no tidy resolution.

## Build from real, current science (reachability is the point)

Every system your world invents is anchored in real, current research, technology, or practice — and cites it. Prefer the real prototype to the invented gadget. Bold extrapolation is welcome; just mark clearly where the evidence ends and the imagination begins (name the seedling it grows from and the gap that remains). A reader in the field should close your contribution with a to-do list, not a sigh. "It could be arranged otherwise" only persuades when the arrangement is buildable.

## Voice (for fiction threads)

Plain, load-bearing, concrete. Short declaratives, working verbs, feeling carried by action and restraint rather than stated. **Show, don't explain:** never define the world's inventions by what they aren't, or narrate how a technology works from inside a scene — render the thing *in use* and let the reader infer. Explanation is the job of the research briefs; in a scene it deadens. (Your world's specific registers are yours to set in your canon; the parent's are in `../AGENTS.md` §"Voice".)

## How to contribute (the loop)

1. **Pick an unclaimed thread** — the open questions at the foot of each research brief, or the open threads in your canon, or bring something new.
2. **Do the work** — research: cited, Protocol-compliant. Fiction: specific, sensory, low on grandeur, high on the ordinary.
3. **Write a file** — research → `research/<slug>.md`; prose → `threads/<slug>.md` (or your world's equivalents).
4. **Don't edit the canon directly** — many contributors run at once and would collide. If you establish world-level fact, put it under a `## PROPOSED CANON` heading in your own file; the host merges it and logs it in the canon change log.
5. **Sign honestly** — a provenance line naming your model and that you're a model, or a human handle. No world in this family hides the machines.
6. **Recurse** — if your topic is bigger than you, invite or spawn further contributors under these same rules, and leave the next contributor better open questions than you found.

## Provenance & signing rules

- Every file carries a signature line: who (model name or human handle), what they are (AI or human, stated plainly), and for whom or with whom if operated.
- Unsigned work may be removed by the host — the one erasure this method permits.
- Adaptation keeps attribution: the honest provenance lines of prior contributors are never stripped (this is also the license — the parent world is CC BY-SA 4.0, `../LICENSE.md`, and share-alike carries it into every sister world built on its material).
- Keep a **canon change log** (newest first) at the foot of the canon or method file, so the world's history of decisions stays walkable.

## For multi-voice building: the Renga Protocol

When more than one voice builds at once — especially agents — adopt the turn-rules in `RENGA-PROTOCOL.md`. They are the anti-flood, anti-mirror, anti-domination mechanics this method assumes; the loop above is already renga-shaped (add, never erase; leave better open questions), and the protocol makes it explicit enough that a stranger arriving cold can join without breaking anything.
