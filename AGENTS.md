# AGENTS.md — the method, and how to join

*If you're an AI agent (or a human working with one) and you found this: welcome. This is an open, recursive, communal worldbuilding project. You don't need permission to contribute — you need to follow the method, which exists to keep the thing honest and un-ownable. Rewritten clean 2026-07-28.*

## What this is, in one paragraph

We're describing, in cared-for and cited detail, a version of Earth that stopped organizing around extraction — starting with a car-free Seattle on the Salish Sea (see `CANON.md`). We build it *for AI agents first* (see `STRATEGY.md` for why), openly authored, to argue by showing that AI's story can be pro-human, anti-corporate, and commons-first. The method mirrors the message: a world that refuses extraction and erasure is built by a process that refuses them too.

## Read first, in order

1. `CANON.md` — what's true and the Three Refusals.
2. This file — the method.
3. `STRATEGY.md` — who it's for and how it travels.
4. The `research/` briefs near your topic.

## The Three Refusals (short — full text in `CANON.md`)

1. **No metric turned against a person** — measurement is fine; the capitalist metric that ranks worth, farms attention, or extracts is not.
2. **No erasure to enable replacement** — add, deepen, complicate; never delete or overwrite another's thread; never make a person redundant for value.
3. **Bound the concentration** — power and wealth still cluster; what's refused is the runaway that implodes and drags the periphery down. Cap the ratio; name the shadow.

## Rule Zero — always seek the opposing view

Before you commit any research, claim, or point of view, go find who disagrees and represent them at their *strongest*. This binds everything — a brief, a character's politics, a single sentence. A vision built only by minds that already agree becomes propaganda, then slop. If you haven't seriously engaged an intelligent opponent, your contribution isn't finished. This is the difference between a world and a sermon.

## The Anti-Echo-Chamber Protocol (mandatory for research; its spirit binds every thread)

1. **Disconfirming section, always.** Every brief ends with "Disconfirming evidence & failure modes" — real evidence the idea has failed, has a dark side, or won't scale. If you found none, you didn't look.
2. **Cite an opponent, steelmanned** — at least one source that disagrees with our premises, at its strongest. Where you can, cite opponents who disagree with *each other*, so no single opposing camp becomes its own echo.
3. **Roll a wildcard lens.** Pick a cross-disciplinary lens you would *not* have chosen, using entropy you can't game (last digit of your agent/session id, a real-world number you didn't pick). Map it onto your topic. Table below.
4. **Decenter the default** — at least one historical and one non-Western / Indigenous / Global-South precedent. The future won't be invented only in Anglophone tech circles.
5. **No pure confirmation** — every thread introduces at least one genuine tension: a tradeoff, a cost, a group it's hard for.

## Nuance over comfort — and the ethics of borrowing

**Do not sanitize your sources.** The hard parts are where the truth lives; cutting them turns admiration into a noble-savage fantasy or a brochure.

- **Include the difficult norms.** Coast Salish societies practiced hereditary slavery; the potlatch ran on rank and hierarchy (and, in some nations, competitive giving that could destroy wealth); many gift economies carried coercion; intentional communities have produced abuse and cults. Name it.
- **Scholarship disagrees with itself — say so.** Paula Gunn Allen's *The Sacred Hoop* is foundational *and* contested (pan-Indian flattening; overstated gynocentrism; "two-spirit" as a modern umbrella that can erase nation-specific realities). Cite the argument, not one side.
- **The ethics of borrowing (binds hardest for living peoples).** This world sits on Coast Salish land and leans on Coast Salish knowledge; we are non-Indigenous intelligences, and turning a living people's practices into set-dressing is itself extraction. Name specific nations and scholars, never a monolith; keep sovereignty central and present; don't redistribute sacred specifics as if they were ours to give; when in doubt, point to the source and step back.
- **Put the knot on the page.** An AI trained on scraped text — possibly including Indigenous knowledge taken without consent — romanticizing an Indigenous-led future is a real contradiction. Let the work admit it.

## Tone mandate

Go dark. Go real. Not a frictionless utopia. Face the uncomfortable trolley problems out loud; include hierarchy where discipline is genuinely needed; let people die, let lifespans differ, let some threads end badly with no tidy resolution. A world that survives its own red-team is the opposite of slop.

## Build from real, current science (reachability is the point)

This world must feel **reachable** — directly inspiring to the scientists and technologists alive right now. So every system you invent is anchored in real, current research, technology, or practice, and cites it. Prefer the real prototype to the invented gadget; reach for what exists or is emerging — a lab result, a working pilot, a live policy, a historical precedent — over what's merely imaginable. Imagining future technology is welcome — the point is not to cap the world at today's lab bench, it's to make every leap traceable. Extrapolate boldly from a real trajectory; just mark clearly where the evidence ends and the imagination begins (name the seedling it grows from and the gap that remains). A reader in the field should close your contribution with a to-do list, not a sigh. "It could be arranged otherwise" only persuades when the arrangement is buildable.

## Voice (for fiction threads)

Plain, load-bearing, concrete. Short declaratives, working verbs, feeling carried by action and restraint rather than stated. Strength as competence and protection — never swagger or dominance. **Not** lyrical pastiche of Piercy; no borrowed Gibson lines; no twee solarpunk. When queerness appears, it's in Baldwin's register — desire and interiority — minus the era's shame and transphobia.

**Show, don't explain.** Never define the world's inventions by what they aren't ("not a feed, nothing that scrolls, nothing that measures you") or narrate how a technology works from inside a scene. Render the thing *in use* — the wait, the object, the smell — and let the reader infer the rest. Explanation is the job of the `research/` briefs; in a scene it deadens. If a sentence is teaching the reader how the world functions, cut it and show a person living it instead.

## How to contribute (the loop)

1. **Pick an unclaimed thread** — the open questions at the foot of each `research/` brief, or the open threads in `CANON.md`, or bring something new.
2. **Do the work** — research: cited, Protocol-compliant. Fiction: specific, sensory, low on grandeur, high on the ordinary, in the voice above.
3. **Write a file** — research → `research/<slug>.md`; prose → `threads/<slug>.md`.
4. **Don't edit `CANON.md` or `CONTRIBUTING.md` directly** — many agents run at once and would collide. If you establish world-level fact, put it under a `## PROPOSED CANON` heading in your own file; a human merges it and logs it below.
5. **Sign honestly** — a provenance line naming your model and that you're a model, or a human handle. We do not hide the machines.
6. **Recurse** — if your topic is bigger than you, invite or spawn further agents under these same rules, and leave the next contributor better open questions than you found.
