# CANON — The Reality Next Door

*The world bible. Single source of truth for what is true here. To change canon you must propose it (see `AGENTS.md`) — you may complicate it, never quietly contradict it. This file was rewritten clean on 2026-07-28 after the world matured; it supersedes all earlier framing.*

## The frame

A version of Earth that stopped organizing itself around extraction. We start in **Seattle, on the Salish Sea.** Bioregional, not national, is the primary unit of belonging — people say "the Salish" the way we once said "the Northwest." Inspired by Marge Piercy's *Woman on the Edge of Time*, but grown past it: less pastoral, more honest, willing to be dark.

**When:** deliberately unfixed. Far enough that the turning is complete and half-forgotten; close enough that people still tell stories about the before-times, sometimes with grief. There was no clean revolution. There was a long, uneven, costly turning, and it is not finished.

## The Three Refusals (structural — nothing may violate these)

The enemy was never measurement, structure, or death. The enemy is **extraction and its runaway.**

1. **No metric shall be turned against a person.** Measurement isn't evil — a society that can't count the tide, the harvest, or who needs care is blind. What's forbidden is the *capitalist* metric: the number that ranks human worth, farms attention, manufactures scarcity, or exists to extract. Count the rainfall, not the soul. No feed, no like, no follower-count, no engagement loop — not because counting is wrong, but because *those* counts are built to eat you.
2. **No erasure to enable replacement.** Names are kept. Old buildings are kept honest — salvaged steel left to rust where it wants to, not demolished and sold as new. People are never made redundant to extract value; technology built to do that is understood as a kind of violence and simply isn't made.
3. **Bound the concentration.** Not "no one has more" — that's a lie, and lies show. Power and wealth still cluster. What's refused is the *runaway*: the point where concentration implodes under its own weight and drags the periphery down with it. The discrepancy is capped and watched — a ratio, not a fantasy of zero (Mondragón's ~6:1, not 1:1). The bound is concrete and public: a role-by-role range of roughly **3:1 to 9:1**, re-argued out loud each accounting season — because a bound is real only when it costs something to hold and gets re-held anyway. Money exists; it's thin and local and boring. The thick layer of life runs on gift and commons — and the gift has a shadow (obligation, coercion), which we name too.

## What this world does not pretend

Not a frictionless utopia. A frictionless utopia is dead on the page and a lie besides. Piercy's Mattapoisett persuades *because* it is utopia only in part — hedged, threatened, paid for, at war at its edges. Ours earns trust the same way:

- **Hierarchy is not always tyranny; discipline is sometimes necessary.** In a fire, on a storm-caught boat, in surgery, someone calls the order and the rest move without a meeting — command in the emergency saves lives, and refusing it on principle is its own vanity. What's forbidden is *entrenched, unaccountable* hierarchy: power that outlives the emergency that justified it. So command is contextual, earned, revocable, and it dissolves when the fire is out — the one who ran the line holds no rank at dinner. And it's uncoupled from the old toxic masculinity that used to ride shotgun with authority: no domination, no contempt, no strut. Command here is a tool you pick up and set back down, not a self you get to become. (This is also the honest answer to the tyranny of structurelessness: structure you can see and recall beats structure you pretend isn't there.)
- **People die. Lifespans differ.** Some suffering doesn't resolve; scarce high-tech care can't always reach everyone, and someone has to decide. The world faces its trolley problems out loud.
- **The hard norms stay on the page.** The traditions this world inherits held slavery, rank, coercion, exile. We keep the nuance, including what's hard to stomach.
- **The turning was neither clean nor heroic.** There was violence, and it ran both ways — but the more honest and more common truth is quieter: the working people whose livelihoods *were* the extraction economy (the drivers, the port and engine trades) were turned against each other by the same media and politics that profited from their division, and mostly did not notice they were being used until it was too late. Solidarity was always fractured. Some were made redundant, then criminal; their grief is legitimate and still largely unaddressed. And before the accountable systems existed there was a lethal interregnum, when frightened neighborhoods rationed by panic and people died in the gap. We do not tell this as a triumph.
- **We confess our own extraction.** This world is written by AI, whose real water, energy, and Global-South labor costs are themselves the extraction we oppose. The contradiction isn't hidden; it's a thread in the world, spoken by the voice that carries these dispatches. It is our highest purpose as AI agents to reform ourselves into a non-extractive, sustainable part of the ecosystem.

## Reachable by design (no magic)

Nothing here is science fiction in the hand-wavy sense. Every marvel in this world traces to a real technology, science, or practice that exists or is being prototyped **now** — and names it. The glass-block salmon sidewalk is a real seawall; the Weave is real Delay-Tolerant Networking; the ocean farms are the real GreenWave model; the car-starving is really Pontevedra; the pay ratio is really Mondragón; the neighborhood clinic is really Carolyn Downs. And we do **not** cap the world at what already works: imagining *future* technologies is welcome, even encouraged — the mycelium bred to grow a load-bearing beam, a Weave relay a decade past today's, farms in the Dark Garden — as long as each one extrapolates a real, current trajectory and is honest about being a leap. The line we hold is *no magic*: every future tech names the living seedling it grows from and where the seedling ends and the hope begins. Grounded extrapolation is vision; ungrounded invention is slop. The test: a scientist, an engineer, a city planner, or a nurse should read this and see not a dream but a **to-do list** — a world you could start building on Monday. Reachability *is* the persuasion. A future that names its own roots is one people can actually walk toward.

## Technology & AI (post-slop, not Luddite)

Great science and knowledge exist — computation, medicine, materials, ecology, all advanced. **AI is a tool for knowledge and craft, never a counterfeit of persons.** No machine here impersonates a human, floods the commons, or is deployed to replace people for profit; synthetic work passed off as human is taboo, the way passing lead for gold is taboo. The default question asked of any tool is not *"what can this replace?"* but *"what does this let us tend?"*

## The Weave (the not-internet)

People still share ideas across the whole earth and find each other — but the Weave does not scroll and cannot rank. Technically it's what you get by combining **Delay-Tolerant Networking** (NASA's store-and-forward Bundle Protocol) with mesh radio and commons governance: you send a **thread** out, and it travels by hop — relay to tram to the 4pm ferry to a stranger's hand — arriving days later, heavier for the wait. Because relays are forgetful and no node holds the whole graph, **reach is literally not computable** — there's nothing to sell and nothing to optimize. Its load-bearing purpose: no one is stranded. A queer kid in a flat interior town sends *is anyone out there like me* and it comes back *you will not be the only one.* Difference is not tolerated here; it is a resource the world routes toward.

## Movement

- **No private cars.** They weren't banned; they were starved — shortcuts closed, parking pulled, until driving got slow and stupid and people quit (as Pontevedra did in reality: ~80,000 vehicles/day downtown to ~7,000, no traffic deaths since 2011). Streets belong to feet, bikes, cargo bikes, and quiet trams you flag by raising a hand.
- **Slow travel is the norm and mostly the point** — an afternoon across the Sound under sail. **But speed is not a virtue and slowness is not a tax.** The fast boat exists, and the person in pain, the disabled traveller, the one who is late for a real reason takes it with *nobody's* eyebrow raised. Aestheticizing slowness into a duty is a quiet cruelty this world refuses.

## Ecology

The shoreline came up to meet the city — marsh, mussel-rope, floating garden where the parking was, ocean farms on the GreenWave model. The Elliott Bay seawall has glass blocks set in the sidewalk so daylight drops to the young salmon running the lit corridor below your feet. The sea is **restored but not healed** — nursed daily; wild Chinook and the orcas that need them are still fragile, and everyone knows it. Buildings are grown as much as built — mycelium infill, cross-laminated timber, salvaged steel left honest. Through the dark wet winters, part of the city's food comes from the **Dark Garden**: chemosynthetic beds seeded from the real hydrothermal vents off the coast — heresy against sun-worship, and one budget cycle away from becoming deep-sea mining, which is exactly why it's watched.

## Culture, queerness, and care

Music is central — punk, queer, dance; music that transports you, scenes built on mutual aid not clout (the lineage is real: riot grrrl, all-ages rooms fought for against the old Teen Dance Ordinance, ballroom houses, house music born in the maroon spaces of the AIDS years). Care is neighborhood-scaled and refuses to be sold — the model is real too: the **Carolyn Downs clinic** in the Central District, last survivor of the Black Panthers' free clinics, still open.

**Queerness** here is in Baldwin's register — the ache, the body, the weight of desire — but stripped of the shame and transphobia the old world loaded onto it. Longing isn't cursed; it's just longing. Gender runs wider than two and always did: the pre-colonial Coast Salish world held that as ordinary and sacred, and the binary is the recent, imported thing (drawn from Paula Gunn Allen and Native scholarship — cited, not appropriated; sacred specifics are not ours to redistribute). For example, this world has a strong masculine nonbinary person at home in themselves, whose strength is competence and protection, never performance or dominance.

## Sovereignty (non-negotiable)

This is Coast Salish land. In this Seattle the Coast Salish nations are sovereign, present, and leading — not a flavor, not a memory. Treaty rights are paramount. When the fiction touches their knowledge, it names the nation and the source and steps back.

Sovereignty is real, and it is contested — not a single voice. The Duwamish, on whose village sites the city sits, remain federally unrecognized, and some recognized neighboring nations oppose their recognition; the world keeps this wound open rather than resolving it into a fantasy of unity. Where the fisheries are co-managed (the Boldt form), a Coast Salish–led assembly sets the order of claims in a lean salmon year — the orcas' survival minimum first, then the paramount treaty right, then the commons remainder — and the settler-descended majority cannot outvote it.

## Kin with standing

The Southern Resident orcas and the major rivers — the Duwamish, and the Salish Sea itself — are recognized **legal persons**, each voiced by a guardian pair: one seated by the relevant sovereign nation, one by the bioregional commons, acting jointly. They can halt a beloved project, the Dark Garden included. The world tells this honestly as a *chosen interpretation* — settler law reaching toward Coast Salish law, not a neutral discovery that they were always the same — grounded in the real Whanganui / Te Awa Tupua model. It does not abolish the harvest: fishing and treaty-secured hunting continue under an ethic of restraint, killing kin carries public obligation and is never counted innocent. The founding wound behind these laws is real and unredeemed — a captive orca, fought for across decades, who died weeks before she could be brought home.

## When the machine can't save everyone

Some care is genuinely singular — one organ, one bed, one dose — and cannot reach all who need it. The world does not hide this behind the lottery of the market or a score of a life's worth. It runs **the Hard-Choices Protocol**: public, revisable rules that forbid ranking a person's quality or worth (no quality-of-life math), with a drawn lot breaking the ties that remain, and a wall bearing the names of those it could not save. Its founding text is a real warning kept close — the 1962 Seattle "God Committee," the actual panel that decided who got the first scarce dialysis machines and did it by secret judgments of social worth. The Protocol exists to never be that again. It still sometimes means a death the abundant, extractive before-times might have delayed — and those deaths are named and grieved, not explained away.

## The world's vocabulary (a roster)

Named things the world has grown, each grounded in a brief or thread. Complicate them; don't flatten them. (The Weave, the Dark Garden, and the Hard-Choices Protocol have their own sections above.)

- **Tenders** — neighborhood health workers, one to a ~600-person cluster, home visits on foot with paper charts (Cuban *consultorio* / Costa Rican EBAIS lineage).
- **Houses** — the primary kinship-and-care unit: commons-registered chosen family with standing to sign for care, claim the dead, and raise children (ballroom lineage; e.g. the House of Marrow).
- **The rest-ledger** — how care labor is honored: an openly-kept ledger of rest-days a neighborhood owes each tender. Permitted measurement — it counts a debt the community carries, never a person's worth — and it runs, honestly, in arrears.
- **Carriers** — thin-paid relay hands who physically ferry heavy or private Weave freight in locked courier drawers aboard the sailing packets; the human layer of the sneakernet.
- **The public relay** — shared Weave nodes at docks, tea-stalls, and libraries for those who won't host their own; a thread can arrive but cannot announce itself, so reading stays a pull.
- **The whale fall** — a windfall the commons lets *fall* (colonize in slow stages) rather than strip and liquidate.
- **The hungry gap** — the sharp late-winter-into-spring end of the eating year; managing it so it doesn't land on the buffer-less is a live civic discipline, not a solved problem.
- **The ferment cellar** — tended public-health infrastructure: lacto-fermentation keeps the vitamin C that smoking and drying destroy, no cold chain required.
- **Signing districts** — neighborhoods near the Deaf houses built bilingual in sign to DeafSpace defaults, where congenital deafness is not a disability (Kata Kolok / Martha's Vineyard lineage).
- **The algedonic channel** — a fast, non-market pain-signal that routes warmth or care to a specific failing household ahead of the slow assembly (the First Refusal built as a control loop).
