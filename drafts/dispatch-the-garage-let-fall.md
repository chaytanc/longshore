**The old Marion Street garage, level five, an afternoon in the ninth month —**

Look at the stain first. On the concrete deck where a car once stood there is a dark oval the size of a car, soaked into the slab across thirty years of a machine dripping the slow black oil of its own running [PLAUSIBLE · draft]. The stall number is still painted at its head — 514, white, chalky, most of the way gone. The oval is a body. It is the ghost of a private thing that was kept up here, one to a person, in a tower built for nothing on earth but the storing of them.

Nobody scrubbed it. Nobody sold the tower for scrap the week the driving stopped, though the rebar in it would have made somebody a season's money [CANON · draft — Third Refusal, bound the concentration]. The commons did the other thing. It let the garage *fall*.

That is the word, and it comes from the whale. When a great whale dies and sinks it is not stripped in a frenzy and gone; it is colonized in slow, overlapping stages, each cohort laying the substrate for the next, and the oil locked in the bone feeds a sunless chemical web that can run for decades [DEMONSTRATED · draft]. The commons took the word and made a verb of it. You do not eat the whale. You let the whale become a reef.

So: level one is warm and dry and wired, and a bike-repair commons works it now, and a ferment cellar took the back bays where the ramp holds off the cold [CANON · draft]. Level two grows things under lamps. Above that the deck is opening to weather on purpose — moss coming in on the north rail, a mycelium-and-timber skin going up in panels where any knowledgeable hand will tell you the mushroom is infill and never, ever a beam [DEMONSTRATED · draft]. The salvaged steel is left to rust where it wants to [CANON · draft]. Level five, where I am standing, is still mostly bone. It is decades off from being anything. That is the plan. It is also the problem.

Because a whale fall is still a concentration, and the first scavenger eats first. Somebody got level one — warm, dry, wired — and somebody else is promised the commons that is decades out, and that promise is an easy one to make to a person who has to wait the decades for it [PLAUSIBLE · draft].

And there is the other body in this. The hands that poured these ramps and ran the engine trades were made redundant, then made criminal, and their grief was never addressed [CANON · draft]. One of them stood on this deck at the last accounting season and said the plain thing: strip it, sell the steel, split it now, my kids are cold this winter. The commons outvoted him and called it stewardship. He is not wrong that stewardship can be a warm word for making the living wait.

The stain is still a stain. 514 is still going. Nobody up here will tell you the man was answered.
