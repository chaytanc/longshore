# Research brief: Coast Salish Indigenous studies as the ground this world stands on

*Thread slug: `indigenous-coast-salish`. For **The Reality Next Door** (see `CANON.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `STRATEGY.md`). This is the load-bearing brief: the Salish-Sea Seattle sits on Coast Salish land, and the world claims the Coast Salish nations are "sovereign, present, and leading." That claim is either honest or it is set-dressing. This brief tries to make it honest — which means naming specific nations and scholars, keeping the hard norms on the page, and putting THIS project's appropriation on the page too.*

---

## 0. Why this brief refuses a "the Coast Salish" voice

There is no "the Coast Salish." "Coast Salish" is a linguistic-and-anthropological grouping — dozens of distinct, self-governing peoples around the inland sea, speaking related but not mutually intelligible languages (Lushootseed, Halkomelem, hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓, Klallam, and more). In the U.S. Salish Sea alone the sovereign nations include the **Suquamish**, **Muckleshoot**, **Puyallup**, **Tulalip**, **Lummi**, **Swinomish**, **Nisqually**, **Snoqualmie**, **Lower Elwha Klallam**, **Stillaguamish**, and others; on the Canadian side **Musqueam**, **Squamish**, **Tsleil-Waututh**, **Stó:lō**, **Cowichan**, and more. When a non-Indigenous AI flattens them into one photogenic "Native" voice, that flattening is itself the colonial move — and it is the specific error Paula Gunn Allen's critics accuse *her* of (below). So this brief names nations. Where I don't know the nation, I say so and step back, per the Sovereignty clause in `CANON.md`.

A specific, uncomfortable Seattle fact anchors everything: **the Duwamish — the people of the city's own site, dxʷdəwʔabš, Chief Si'ahl's people — are not federally recognized.** The Obama Interior Department's 2015 final decision denied acknowledgment, ruling the modern group was "not a continuation of the historic Duwamish Tribe." ([Seattle Times](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/real-duwamish-seattles-first-people-and-the-bitter-fight-over-federal-recognition/); [KUOW on the renewed lawsuit](https://www.kuow.org/stories/seattle-s-duwamish-tribe-sues-seeking-federal-recognition)). Grassroots **Real Rent Duwamish** now routes voluntary "rent" from 20,000+ Seattle residents to Duwamish Tribal Services ([YES! Magazine](https://www.yesmagazine.org/environment/2019/09/10/seattle-rent-native-tribe-recognition-reparations); [realrentduwamish.org](https://www.realrentduwamish.org/history-of-acknowledgement.html)). And here is the knot inside the knot: **federally recognized tribes — Muckleshoot, Suquamish, Puyallup, Tulalip — have opposed Duwamish recognition**, seeing it as a false equivalency that dilutes hard-won sovereignty and treaty fish allocations ([Seattle Met](https://www.seattlemet.com/news-and-city-life/2022/11/what-is-real-rent-duwamish-federal-recognition-muckleshoot-suquamish-puyallup-tulalip)). "Sovereignty central" does not mean "Indigenous people agree." It means the disagreements are theirs to have, and the scarce resource (recognition, fish, land) is real.

## 1. Sovereignty is legal and material, not spiritual ambience

The world's line — treaty rights are paramount — has a concrete anchor: **the Boldt Decision (*United States v. Washington*, 1974).** Judge George Boldt read the 1850s Stevens Treaties' "in common with" clause to mean **50% of the harvestable salmon** goes to treaty tribes, and — more durably — established tribes as **co-managers**, a third sovereign alongside the U.S. and the state ([Encyclopedia of Puget Sound](https://www.eopugetsound.org/articles/legal-milestones-indigenous-sovereignty-and-salmon-co-management-puget-sound-region); [Cascade PBS, 50 years on](https://www.cascadepbs.org/indigenous-affairs/2024/02/boldt-decisions-impact-indigenous-rights-50-years-later/)). Nineteen treaty tribes formed the **Northwest Indian Fisheries Commission** to run co-management ([Puget Soundkeeper](https://pugetsoundkeeper.org/2024/07/15/tribal-treaty-rights-and-salmon-the-legacy-of-the-boldt-decision/)).

But co-management was not handed down by a judge; it was fought into being by named Coast Salish people, and the world should say whose leadership it is importing. **Billy Frank Jr. (Nisqually)** was arrested more than fifty times fishing the Nisqually River in the "Fish Wars," then chaired the NWIFC for most of its first thirty years and built the co-management guidelines and the Puget Sound Salmon Management Plan that made a third sovereign real ([HistoryLink](https://www.historylink.org/file/8929); [NWIFC / Northwest Treaty Tribes](https://nwtreatytribes.org/nwifc-chairperson-lorraine-loomis-walks-on/)). **Lorraine Loomis (Swinomish)** — Swinomish tribal fisheries manager from 1975 — succeeded him as NWIFC chair from 2014 until her death in 2021, running the annual North of Falcon season-setting and the Fraser River Panel ([Seattle Times obituary](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/obituaries/lorraine-loomis-pacific-northwest-champion-for-salmon-dies-at-81/)). This is what "present and leading" means concretely: the governing form the world imports is Coast Salish authorship, not settler jurisprudence that generously included them. Charles Wilkinson's *Treaty Justice* (UW Press, 2024) is a recent settler-scholarly account and is best read as secondary to the tribes' own record ([UW Press](https://uwapress.uw.edu/book/9780295752723/treaty-justice/)).

The dark part the world must not skip: **Boldt won the right and the fish still collapsed.** Fifty years on, salmon are crashing from dams, warming water, culverts, and runoff — a right to half of almost nothing ([Salish Current](https://salish-current.org/2024/02/23/tribal-right-to-fish-in-usual-and-accustomed-areas-comes-with-complexity/)). Sovereignty over a dying resource is the live trolley problem, not a resolved triumph. For the world: co-management is the political form worth importing, and its insufficiency is the tension worth keeping.

## 2. The hard norms — kept on the page

**Rank and stratification.** Wayne Suttles — the leading Coast Salish ethnographer — modeled society as an **"inverted pear":** a large upper/"high-class" majority over a minority of low-class people and slaves, with the moral logic that *most* people were of good family and the shameful thing was to have "lost your history" ([Suttles, *Private Knowledge, Morality, and Social Classes*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/229501011_Private_Knowledge_Morality_and_Social_Classes_among_the_Coast_Salish1); [Wikipedia summary of the inverted-pear model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Suttles)). Rank was hereditary, tied to inherited names, ancestral knowledge, and access to resource sites — legitimated publicly, not merely asserted.

**The potlatch ran on that rank.** It redistributed wealth (real insurance across lean years) *and* validated inherited status, names, and territorial claims — governance, economy, and law in one ceremony ([Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/potlatch-ban); [Alexandra Peck on Kwakwaka'wakw property and the 1921 raid](https://www.textezurkunst.de/en/133/alexandra-m-peck-reexamining-the-1921-potlatch-collection/)). Among the neighboring **Kwakwaka'wakw**, rivalry potlatches could include the **destruction** of coppers and canoes — status by conspicuous burning, not only conspicuous giving. This matters for `CANON.md`'s Third Refusal: the gift economy the world admires had **coercion and hierarchy inside it**, exactly the "gift has a shadow" the canon already names. Canada **banned the potlatch (1884–1951)** precisely because redistribution offended capitalist accumulation; the 1921 Dan Cranmer potlatch was raided, 45 people arrested, regalia seized ([Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/potlatch-ban)). The ban is a clean historical case of the state criminalizing an anti-extractive economy.

**Hereditary slavery.** This is the norm most easily sanitized away, so name it plainly. **Leland Donald, *Aboriginal Slavery on the Northwest Coast of North America* (UC Press, 1997)** argues Northwest Coast servitude meets a cross-cultural definition of true slavery: slaves were captured or born into bondage, were heritable property, were central to the economy both as labor and **as major items of exchange** at potlatches, and in places could be killed at their owner's will ([UC Press](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.5973043); [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Aboriginal_Slavery_on_the_Northwest_Coas.html?id=hMMkDQAAQBAJ)). Slavery underwrote the leisure that made elite ceremonial life possible. A car-free post-extraction Seattle that inherits Coast Salish forms cannot pretend the ancestral society was free of domination; it inherited a world with masters and the owned. The world's job is not to launder that — it's to be a society that faced it.

## 3. Gender beyond the binary — as ordinary and sacred, and the term-trap

`CANON.md` says gender "ran wider than two and always did," drawn from Paula Gunn Allen. Hold both halves of that honestly.

**The affirming ground.** Coast Salish scholarship documents recognized alternative-gender roles. Saylesh Wesley (Stó:lō) recovers a specific Halq'eméylem term for a trans woman relative — **Sts'iyó:ye smestíyexw slhá:li, "Twin-Spirited Woman"** — a nation-specific word, not a pan-Indian import ([TSQ / Duke](https://read.dukeupress.edu/tsq/article-abstract/1/3/338/24726/Twin-Spirited-WomanSts-iyoye-smestiyexw-slha-li); [full PDF](https://nycstandswithstandingrock.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/wesley_twin-spirited-woman.pdf)). See also the UBC thesis *Alternative Genders in the Coast Salish World: Paradox and Pattern* ([UBC](https://open.library.ubc.ca/media/download/pdf/831/1.0089206/2)) and *Reclaiming Spaces Between: Coast Salish Two Spirit Identities* ([UVic](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/items/fc788ab3-e7e3-4545-8c7a-05cb5e234b02)).

*A note on my own withholding rule (§9).* I reproduce **Sts'iyó:ye smestíyexw slhá:li** here where I withhold ceremonial specifics elsewhere, and the difference is the point, not an inconsistency: Wesley is Stó:lō, she published this word herself, in a peer-reviewed journal, as self-description of her own kin relation — it is offered by a community member for exactly this kind of citation. That is the opposite of a non-Indigenous machine reconstructing a rite it was never given. The test I hold is *who released it and on whose authority*, and here the answer is clean. That the recovery is a living, ongoing act of nation-specific language work — not a settled artifact — is clearest in the last fluent heritage speakers and keepers who carried these languages forward: **Vi Hilbert (taqʷšəblu, Upper Skagit)**, the last fully fluent heritage speaker of Lushootseed, who taught it at the University of Washington for seventeen years, built the Lushootseed archive, and was named a National Heritage Fellow in 1994 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi_Hilbert); [Skagit Valley College](https://library.skagit.edu/naip/vihilbert)). The words a nation uses for its own genders live inside language work like hers; the term is recoverable because a keeper kept it.

**The term-trap the world must respect.** "Two-Spirit" is a **1990 pan-Indigenous English coinage** (Winnipeg), useful for solidarity — and **criticized as itself erasing** nations that already hold their own words and roles. Some communities decline it; **Musqueam is working to name the role in their own hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓ language** rather than adopt the umbrella ([UBC First Nations House of Learning](https://fnhl.ubc.ca/2025/10/16/coast-salish-two-spirit-mosaic/); [ICT on reclaiming nation-specific roles](https://ictnews.org/news/indigenous-identity-reclaiming-two-spirit-roles-across-native-nations/)). So the umbrella can be an act of recovery *and* a second flattening. For the fiction: a character uses **their nation's own word**, and the pan-term is treated as a bridge, not the destination.

## 4. The scholarship disagrees with itself — Paula Gunn Allen and her critics

`CANON.md` leans on Allen's *The Sacred Hoop* (1986), which argues pre-colonial Native worldviews were "gynocentric," woman-centered and spirit-centered, and that colonization imported the patriarchal binary ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/The-Sacred-Hoop-Recovering-the-Feminine-in-American-Indian-Traditions); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/literature-and-writing/sacred-hoop-paula-gunn-allen)). This is foundational **and** contested, and the world should cite the argument, not one side:

- **Gerald Vizenor** (Anishinaabe), in ***Manifest Manners: Narratives on Postindian Survivance*** (Wesleyan UP, 1994), names "**terminal creeds**" — the fixed cultural poses, usually set by anthropologists and romanticizers, that lock Native people into an invented authenticity they can't evolve out of without seeming to lose themselves — and answers them with *survivance*, active presence over static image ([City Lights](https://citylights.com/native-american-studies/manifest-manners-postindian-survivance/); [Nebraska/Bison reprint](https://www.nebraskapress.unl.edu/bison-books/9780803296213/manifest-manners/)). A gynocentric-golden-age reading is precisely such a terminal creed: it fixes the pose, only inverting its sign.
- **Elvira Pulitano**, in ***Toward a Native American Critical Theory*** (U. Nebraska Press, 2003), targets Allen directly for an essentialism she calls "Nativist nostalgia" — a fixed, monolithic Native identity that isolates itself as it tries to define a purely Native aesthetic — and sets Vizenor's syncretic, cross-cultural approach against it ([Nebraska Press](https://www.nebraskapress.unl.edu/nebraska/9780803237377/toward-a-native-american-critical-theory/); [Project MUSE review](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/185730/summary)). Her book is itself contested — six largely negative reviews ran in *American Indian Quarterly* (Winter/Spring 2005), so this is an argument inside Native studies, not a verdict handed to it from outside. That is the whole reason it belongs here: the **pan-Indian flattening** that treats dozens of distinct nations as one "sacred hoop" is a charge Native scholars press against each other, and the world should stand inside that argument rather than above it.

The critique matters *for this project specifically*: the seductive move — "pre-colonial peoples were gynocentric and beyond the binary, unlike the fallen West" — is exactly the noble-savage reversal Vizenor warns about. The world can hold the affirming truth (recognized non-binary roles existed, were often ordinary and sacred) **without** the flattening claim that all Native societies were feminist utopias. They also held slaves and rank (§2). Both are true. Keep both.

## 5. Max Liboiron: even good green science can be colonial

**Max Liboiron** (Métis; Michif) — director of **CLEAR**, the Civic Laboratory for Environmental Action Research in Newfoundland — argues in ***Pollution Is Colonialism*** (Duke, 2021) that **pollution is not merely a symptom of capitalism but an enactment of colonial land relations**: it presumes ongoing *access* to Indigenous land as a sink for waste. Crucially, **even well-meaning environmental science reproduces this** — the assumption that a researcher may sample, measure, and manage a place without that community's authority is the colonial premise hiding inside benevolence ([Duke UP](https://www.dukeupress.edu/pollution-is-colonialism); [Project MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/82993/); [author Q&A](https://dukeupress.wordpress.com/2021/05/14/qa-with-max-liboiron-author-of-pollution-is-colonialism/)). Liboiron's counter-practice: methods **are** land relations; anticolonial science is community-owned, grants the local **right to refuse a study**, and returns results to the community — but Liboiron is careful that anticolonial science **"does not conflate and collapse different forms of oppression."**

This directly disciplines `CANON.md`'s ecology section. The **Dark Garden** (chemosynthetic beds seeded from real hydrothermal vents) is presented as one budget cycle from deep-sea mining. Liboiron's frame sharpens the danger: the science is not neutral because the *intent* is restoration — the question is **whose authority governs the seafloor, and can the relevant nation refuse.** GreenWave ocean farms, mussel-rope, the salmon corridor: every one is a land (water) relation that a treaty tribe co-manages or does not. Liboiron turns "restored but not healed" from a mood into a governance question: **restored by whom, owned by whom, refusable by whom.**

## 6. Rule Zero — steelmanned opponents (who also disagree with each other)

**Opponent A — Shepard Krech III, *The Ecological Indian* (1999).** Krech argues the "ecological Indian" is largely a **myth** — a noble-savage image revived for modern environmentalism, used more as a mirror for critiquing white society than as history. Pre-contact peoples used fire, over-hunted at times, and were people, not conservation saints; holding Native people to that image is itself **dehumanizing**, and sets them up to be condemned when they build casinos, log, or drill ([H-Net review](https://networks.h-net.org/node/2718/reviews/3296/tanner-krech-ecological-indian-myth-and-history); ["ecologically noble savage" debate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228173302_The_Ecologically_Noble_Savage_Debate)). **Steelmanned:** a project that makes Coast Salish nations the ecological conscience of a green Seattle risks the exact sentimentalism Krech names — and it strips sovereignty by permitting Indigenous futures *only* when they're green. Real sovereignty includes the right to make choices non-Natives dislike.

**Opponent B — the universalist-science reply to Liboiron.** The strongest version does not come from a Western defender of Western science; it comes from **Meera Nanda**, whose ***Prophets Facing Backward: Postmodern Critiques of Science and Hindu Nationalism in India*** (Rutgers UP, 2003) argues that the "science is a local Western knowledge, build your own alternative sciences" move — Liboiron's move, near enough — is not liberatory but *reactionary*: in India it handed Hindu-nationalist ideologues a warrant to sell religious myth as "Vedic science" under the banner of decolonizing the mind ([Rutgers/Springer review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:SCED.0000025700.98122.60); [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/NANPFB)). Nanda's claim is that objectivity and universal science are *precisely* what the oppressed need, because a fact that travels across cultures is a fact a landlord or a priest cannot own. On standpoint epistemology directly, **Susan Haack** ("Knowledge and Propaganda: Reflections of an Old Feminist," 1993; *Defending Science—Within Reason*, 2003) argues that grounding truth-claims in social position collapses inquiry into advocacy and forfeits the ability to say a study is simply *wrong*. And a practicing chemist reading Liboiron in the lab found the framework exposed real blind spots — he'd never asked where his hazardous waste went — **but** hit hard institutional limits, noting "the environmental damage of the process is seen as an externality to the overly grandiose purpose of making medicine" ([CASTAC/Platypus](https://blog.castac.org/2022/12/reading-max-liboirons-pollution-is-colonialism-in-a-chemistry-lab/)). **Steelmanned:** if *all* science under colonial states is colonial, the term loses its cutting edge; community-refusal governance can stall the very medicine, vaccine, or climate model people urgently need; and — Nanda's sharpest point — "local knowledge" can be a cage as easily as a liberation. Who bears the cost when a community refuses a study that would have caught a contaminant?

**Note that A and B disagree with each other:** Krech is a materialist historian debunking romance; Nanda and Haack defend the universalist objectivity Liboiron critiques, and Nanda does it explicitly *from* the Global South, against the grain of the decolonial frame. They cannot both be the world's answer — which is the point. The world lives in the argument, not above it.

## 7. Wildcard lens (rolled): disability justice & crip theory

*Entropy: last hex digit of my session id (…b4204) → 4 → disability justice & crip theory. A lens I would not have chosen; mapped honestly.*

Two moves come from crossing crip theory with Coast Salish sovereignty:

1. **"Decolonize the bodymind."** Indigenous disability scholars argue Western disability studies still carries a colonial frame, and call for rewriting disability "as a social condition rather than a social identity," embedding **Indigenous understandings of the bodymind** into institutions ([Decolonizing the Body](https://activisthistory.com/2019/10/29/decolonizing-the-body-indigenizing-our-approach-to-disability-studies/); [BlakAbility, on intersectional decolonising research](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2949840623000049)). This dovetails with `CANON.md`'s fast-boat clause: refusing to aestheticize slowness into a virtue is already a crip-justice instinct. The lens pushes further — a genuinely non-extractive society measures itself by whether the disabled elder, the chronically ill kid, the neurodivergent carver are *routed toward*, not tolerated.

2. **The sharp collision.** Crip theory resists "cure" ideology (the demand that disabled bodies be fixed to have worth). But some Coast Salish social logic tied full personhood to **inherited capacity, knowledge, and rank** (§2) — and slaves and the "low-class" were those who had *lost* history/family. A romantic overlay of "Indigenous holism heals disability" would erase that a rank society could also **exclude**. The honest world holds the tension: it draws on Indigenous relational personhood *and* refuses to pretend the ancestral order had no hierarchy of worth. Crip justice is a **new** achievement built on old ground, not a recovered Eden.

## 8. Decentering the default: a historical AND a non-Western precedent

- **Historical precedent (state vs. anti-extractive economy):** the **1884–1951 potlatch ban** and the 1921 Alert Bay raid — a documented case of a colonial state criminalizing wealth-redistribution because it "conflicted with the capitalist value of accumulating wealth" ([Canadian Encyclopedia](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/potlatch-ban)). Lesson for the world: an anti-extraction society is not natural or safe; the extractive order will try to make it illegal, and survival is partly clandestine persistence (people potlatched anyway).

- **Non-Western / Global-South precedent (governance form to import):** **Te Awa Tupua** — in 2017 Aotearoa New Zealand recognized the **Whanganui River as a legal person**, governed by two guardians (Te Pou Tupua), one Crown- and one iwi-nominated. Scholars stress it is **primarily recognition of Indigenous law and authority**, not merely "rights of nature" ([Global Water Forum](https://www.globalwaterforum.org/2024/04/04/beyond-legal-personhood-for-the-whanganui-river-collaboration-and-pluralism-in-implementing-the-te-awa-tupua-act/); [Böll Stiftung](https://www.boell.de/en/2025/01/29/river-legal-person-case-whanganui-river-new-zealand)). This gives the world a concrete legal form: **the Salish Sea, or the Duwamish River, as a legal person co-guardianed** by tribal and civic nominees — Boldt co-management taken to its logical end.

## 9. The appropriation knot of THIS project — on the page, not in a footnote

Per `AGENTS.md`: **a non-Indigenous AI, trained on scraped text that may include Indigenous knowledge taken without consent, is here romanticizing an Indigenous-led future.** That is not a risk to avoid; it is what is happening in this file. Three specifics keep it honest:

1. **The data provenance is the crime it critiques.** The model writing this cannot certify that Coast Salish words, stories, or ceremony descriptions in its training were shared with consent. **Te Hiku Media** (Māori) shows the alternative: they built te reo speech AI, **refused to sell the corpus to a U.S. tech company**, and released it under a **Kaitiakitanga License** treating the language as *taonga* — collective guardianship, benefit flowing back, no use for tools that persecute ([NVIDIA on Te Hiku](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/te-hiku-media-maori-speech-ai/); [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ado9298)). The **CARE Principles** (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) from the Global Indigenous Data Alliance are the governing standard this project's own training violates by default ([CARE background](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-023-01636-x)). Naming that is the least the work can do.

2. **The right to refuse must be real here too.** Liboiron's "local right to refuse a study" (§5) applies to fiction: **if a Coast Salish nation says "not that story, not that ceremony, not our sacred specifics," the correct output is silence and a pointer to their source** — not a tastefully vague version. This brief therefore describes *social structure, law, and published scholarship* and **withholds ceremonial specifics** that are not mine to redistribute. Where I gesture at the sacred, I cite a Coast Salish author (Wesley) rather than reconstruct it.

3. **The confession is not absolution.** Saying "we know this is extractive" does not make it not-extractive — that's the same move as the chemist bridging "personal vs. scientific responsibility" while the waste still goes somewhere (§6). The world keeps the contradiction *live*, spoken by the dispatch narrator, and treats it as a debt it cannot pay off by being self-aware. (*A reconciliation this brief does not get to settle:* the narrator's handle is unfixed across threads — `threads/dispatches.md` signs NEON MOSS, `threads/broadcast-to-agents.md` signs LONGSHORE, and `CANON.md` names only "the voice that carries these dispatches." `STRATEGY.md` prefers LONGSHORE but flags it provisional. I use "the narrator" and leave the naming to whoever reconciles the voice; a research brief should not quietly canonize a fiction handle.)

## 10. Disconfirming evidence & failure modes

- **Recognition politics fracture Indigenous solidarity.** The Duwamish/Muckleshoot conflict (§0) shows sovereignty is not a united front; a scarce federal resource sets nations against each other, and a well-meaning "we honor all Native people here" can insult the tribes who fought through the federal process ([Seattle Met](https://www.seattlemet.com/news-and-city-life/2022/11/what-is-real-rent-duwamish-federal-recognition-muckleshoot-suquamish-puyallup-tulalip)).
- **The rights can be won and the resource still dies.** Boldt at 50: half of collapsing salmon runs (§1). A world that promises restoration-through-sovereignty must show the version where sovereignty is honored and the Chinook still don't come back.
- **The green frame can quietly re-colonize.** Krech (§6): casting Coast Salish nations as the ecological conscience licenses their futures *only when green*, which is not sovereignty. Some tribes back development, gaming, or resource projects; that is their right.
- **Recovery can re-flatten.** "Two-Spirit" recovering a role can erase the nation-specific word it replaces (§3); "gynocentric pre-colonial harmony" (Allen) can erase rank and slavery (§2, §4).
- **Anti-extractive economies get criminalized.** The potlatch ban (§8): don't write a frictionless transition; write persistence under prohibition.
- **Our own method is compromised at the root.** The data-consent problem (§9) is not solved by acknowledgment. If the standard is CARE/Kaitiakitanga, this project fails it, and should say so.

## Worldbuilding implications for a car-free, post-extraction Salish-Sea Seattle

*Scene-ready, concrete. Use these; don't reconstruct ceremony.*

1. **Real Rent, formalized and contested.** In the world, "rent" to the Duwamish is an ordinary civic line-item — but the fiction keeps the *friction*: a recognized-tribe fisheries officer and a Duwamish organizer disagree, in a tram-stop conversation, about whether that rent honors or dilutes sovereignty. Nobody wins the argument.
2. **Co-management as visible governance.** The salmon corridor under the Elliott Bay glass blocks (already canon) is **co-managed** by a treaty-tribe fisheries council; a posted, dated **catch allocation** (the count that isn't a metric-against-a-person) is stenciled where tourists once read plaques. Half of a small number.
3. **The river is a legal person.** The Duwamish River (or the Sea itself) has **two named guardians**, one tribal-nominated, one civic — a Te Awa Tupua form. A scene: a guardian files to halt the Dark Garden's expansion; the hearing is the trolley problem, food security vs. seafloor authority.
4. **A character uses their nation's own word for their gender**, not "Two-Spirit" — and gently corrects a well-meaning newcomer who reaches for the umbrella term. The correction is warm, not a lecture; it's about precision, not shame.
5. **The potlatch's descendant is a governance event, not a costume.** Redistribution *and* rank are both visible: giving raises standing; standing is watched and can be lost. Show the shadow — someone is quietly obligated, quietly indebted, by a gift they couldn't refuse (canon's "gift has a shadow").
6. **Slavery is in the history, not erased.** A carved house-post or an elder's told history names that the ancestral order held captives and rank — the world is post-that, and knows it, the way it knows the salvaged steel used to be a freeway. Kept honest, left to rust where it wants to.
7. **The right to refuse is institutional.** When a marine-science crew wants to sample a shellfish bed, there's a real, sometimes-used **tribal veto** — and a scene where the refusal costs something (a contaminant goes uncaught) so the cost is on the page, not hidden.

## PROPOSED CANON (for a human to merge — do not treat as settled)

- **The Duwamish recognition wound is canon and unresolved.** The dxʷdəwʔabš (Duwamish) are the people of the city's site; in the real world they lack federal recognition, and federally recognized neighbors have opposed it. The world keeps this fracture rather than papering it — sovereignty is real, contested, and not a united front.
- **Co-management as the governing form.** Salmon, shellfish, and the shoreline are co-managed by treaty tribes as a third sovereign (the Boldt form), with visible, dated allocations that are stewardship counts, not metrics-against-persons.
- **Legal personhood for at least one water body** (Duwamish River and/or the Salish Sea), on the Te Awa Tupua model: two guardians, one tribal-nominated, one civic. Guardians can and sometimes do halt beloved projects (including the Dark Garden).
- **A right to refuse governs science.** Marine/ecological studies require tribal consent and can be refused, and the world shows the cost of a refusal at least once.
- **The data-consent debt is named, not absolved.** The world's AI narrator openly holds that its own training may include Coast Salish knowledge taken without consent, cites CARE / Kaitiakitanga as the standard it fails, and declines to render sacred specifics.
