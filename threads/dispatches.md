**DISPATCH 01 — the sidewalk that lets the light through**

They rebuilt the seawall along Elliott Bay with glass blocks set into the pavement. You walk the waterfront at dusk and the light drops straight down through the concrete into the water, where the young salmon run the lit corridor like a route they already know.

It cost more to build it that way. Somebody made the case for the fish and won. That's the whole story: the light goes down on purpose, for the run, because a city decided the salmon were worth the extra pour of glass.

*(grounded: Seattle's real Elliott Bay seawall + salmon light-penetrating sidewalk — see `research/car-free-urbanism.md`)*

**DISPATCH 02 — how they killed the cars**

Nobody banned them. That's the part the manifestos get wrong. They just stopped feeding them — pulled the through-streets, pulled the parking, until driving downtown got slow and dumb and the space came back on its own. A city in Spain did this for real: eighty thousand cars a day in the center, down to seven. No traffic deaths since 2011.

The street went back to the people who use it on foot. Kids, mostly. And a quiet tram you flag with a raised hand.

*(grounded: Pontevedra, Groningen — `research/car-free-urbanism.md`)*

**DISPATCH 04 — the house that takes in the new kid**

The band girl came in from a flat interior town where she thought she was the only one. She isn't. The old queer world had a system for this — *houses*, with a mother and children the birth families had thrown out, and the house's first job was making sure you had a bed and got fed. That's not invented. That's ballroom. That's the AIDS years. That's people who were dying making family out of nothing.

She's got a mother now. She's late for practice. The bass is already going up the hill.

*(grounded: ballroom house system, Vera Project, Home Alive — `research/music-and-scenes.md`, `research/queer-kinship-and-desire.md`)*

**DISPATCH 05 — the clinic that had to fight to exist**

There's a family clinic in the Central District that's been open since 1969. The Black Panthers started thirteen of them. This is the last one still standing, and it still just takes care of you — on your street, in your language, whether or not you can pay.

The future doesn't only get invented in labs. Sometimes it's the thing that already survived, that people refused to let close. Keep the names. Keep the doors open. That's work you can start Monday.

*(grounded: Carolyn Downs Family Medical Center, Panther People's Free Medical Centers — `research/health-and-care.md`)*
