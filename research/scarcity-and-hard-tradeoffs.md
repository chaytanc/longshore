# Scarcity and hard tradeoffs — the trolley problems this world cannot dodge

*Domain: the rationing decisions a post-extraction Salish-Sea Seattle still has to make, laid out as genuine dilemmas with defensible sides — not as problems the good society has already solved. A world that refused extraction did not abolish scarcity. It abolished the *manufactured* scarcity of the capitalist metric (First Refusal). What's left is the real thing: a scarce organ, a thin salmon run three parties need, a dark wet winter and not enough stored energy to keep everyone warm. Someone still has to decide. This brief refuses to let the world off the hook.*

The Tone mandate (`AGENTS.md`) says: face the trolley problems out loud, let people die, let some threads end badly. So this brief does not end each section with a fix. Each ends with the knife still on the table. Where a real institution or number exists, it's cited; where I extrapolate for the fiction, I flag it.

## Part I — The scarce bed, the scarce organ: who gets the machine

### 1. The precedent is *local*, and it is a warning

The founding parable of modern medical rationing happened here, on First Hill. In **1962** the Seattle Artificial Kidney Center at Swedish Hospital had the world's first practical outpatient dialysis (Belding Scribner's shunt made chronic dialysis possible) — and roughly **three treatment slots against ~60 people who would otherwise die**. The hospital convened an anonymous seven-person lay panel — a lawyer, a minister, a banker, a housewife, a labor leader, a state official, a surgeon — to choose. Shana Alexander's November 1962 *Life* article, **"They Decide Who Lives, Who Dies,"** exposed it and christened it the **"God Committee."** ([Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/do/10.1377/hblog20091130.002998/full/); [AMA Journal of Ethics](https://journalofethics.ama-assn.org/article/god-panels-and-history-hemodialysis-america-cautionary-tale/2012-11); [Northwest Kidney Centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Kidney_Centers))

The reason it's a *cautionary* tale and not a heroic one: with no honest medical criterion to separate the candidates, the committee fell back on **"social worth."** It favored people who looked like its own members — churchgoing married breadwinners with children, steady jobs, community standing. It quietly penalized the single, the poor, the unconventional, the ex-prisoner. Reviewers later wrote that the panel defined worth "with Seattle values… the Pacific Northwest was no place for a Henry David Thoreau with bad kidneys." The scandal helped drive Congress, in 1972, to simply *fund dialysis for everyone* under Medicare — abolishing the tragic choice by throwing federal money at it.

**The lesson this world must keep:** the God Committee's sin was not that it chose. Someone had to. Its sin was that, absent a defensible rule, it smuggled in the prejudices of the comfortable and called them ethics. A world with no attention-metrics and thin money has *removed the market* as the hidden rationing device — which means it has to make the rationing *visible*, and visible rationing always risks becoming the God Committee again. This is the trap, not the solution.

### 2. The four families of rule, and why each is defensible and monstrous

When the pandemic forced the question again at scale, the most-cited framework was Emanuel, Persad, et al., **"Fair Allocation of Scarce Medical Resources in the Time of Covid-19"** (*NEJM*, 2020), which distilled four ethical values — **maximize benefits, treat people equally, promote/reward instrumental value, give priority to the worst-off** — and recommended maximizing benefits (lives, or life-years) as primary. ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsb2005114); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32202722/)) Every real triage protocol is a weighting of a few incompatible rules:

- **Maximize survival ("soft utilitarian").** Save the most lives, or the most life-years. Italy's **SIAARTI** guidance (March 2020) said this out loud, folding **age, comorbidity, and functional status** into ICU admission "to maximize benefits for the largest number," and was immediately condemned as ageist and ableist. ([Annals of Intensive Care](https://annalsofintensivecare.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13613-021-00888-4); [Asian Bioethics Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41649-020-00127-1)) *Defensible:* in a true crush, refusing to prioritize survivability means more people die for a principle. *Monstrous:* "life-years" and "functional status" are exactly the metrics that write disabled and old people off the list before the coin is even flipped.
- **First-come, first-served.** *Defensible:* no one is judged; the queue is blind. *Monstrous:* it rewards proximity, information, and mobility — the person who knew to come early, who could get there — and in a slow-travel bioregion, "who could get to the one hospital fastest" is itself a wealth-and-health filter. SIAARTI's whole argument was that first-come "leads to many avoidable deaths."
- **Lottery.** *Defensible:* it is the only rule that treats two strangers as literally equal, and it is the rule disability advocates often prefer *because* it refuses quality-of-life judgments. *Monstrous:* it will, with clean conscience, let the 30-year-old die and save the 89-year-old, and a community has to be able to live with that outcome the morning after.
- **Sickest-first / worst-off priority.** *Defensible:* it matches the deepest medical instinct and the "priority to the worst-off" value. *Monstrous:* the sickest are often those least likely to survive the intervention, so you can burn the scarce machine on someone who dies anyway while three savable people die waiting.

### 3. The organ is worse, because it's a fixed commons

A ventilator can be built. **A donor kidney or liver cannot.** Organ allocation is the purest scarce-commons problem, and the US body that runs it (UNOS/OPTN, under the National Organ Transplant Act) is legally required to balance **utility** (get the most life and function out of each organ) against **equity** (fair access regardless of wait time, blood type, geography, or luck), and the two genuinely conflict. ([Cambridge Quarterly of Healthcare Ethics](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-quarterly-of-healthcare-ethics/article/balancing-equity-and-efficiency-in-kidney-allocation/39B721DA54D2DC0660FA066081C86F5A); [Seminars in Nephrology](https://www.seminarsinnephrology.org/article/S0270-9295(22)00056-0/fulltext)) Push toward utility (give the kidney to the young, healthy, well-matched recipient who'll keep it 30 years) and you systematically disadvantage older, sicker, harder-to-match, and — through the long tail of who got sick and who got on the list when — poorer and non-white patients. Push toward pure equity (longest wait wins) and organs fail sooner and fewer total life-years are saved. Recent data shows the tension is unresolved and getting messier: kidney **non-use approaching 30%** and roughly **20% of transplants now "out of sequence,"** with equity concerns about who benefits. ([Current Transplantation Reports](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40472-025-00462-y))

**The dilemma this world inherits, undodged:** the Salish-Sea health commons (see `research/health-and-care.md`) resolves ~80% of care locally and warmly. But the 20% — the transplant, the one ICU, the machine that exists in only one city — is a *fixed scarcity that gift economy and mutual aid cannot conjure away.* You can refuse to sell it. You cannot refuse to allocate it. And every allocation rule that isn't the market is a rule someone will experience as the God Committee deciding they weren't worth it.

## Part II — The shared salmon catch: three claimants, not enough fish

This is the hardest one, because it is not two-sided. It is a genuine **three-way trolley** in which every side is right, and it sits on sovereign Coast Salish ground where one of the three claims is not a preference but **paramount law.**

### 4. Treaty right is not a stakeholder position — it is the senior claim

In **United States v. Washington** (Judge George Boldt, **February 1974**, affirmed by the Supreme Court in 1979), the 1850s Stevens Treaties' reservation of the right to fish "in common with" settlers was read to guarantee the treaty tribes **up to 50% of the harvestable salmon** passing their usual and accustomed grounds, and made the tribes **co-managers** of the fishery, not petitioners to it. ([US Fish & Wildlife, 50 Years of Boldt](https://www.fws.gov/story/50-years-boldt-decision); [HistoryLink](https://www.historylink.org/file/21084)) Canon already holds this: *"Treaty rights are paramount."* So in this world the Muckleshoot, Puyallup, Nisqually, Suquamish, Lummi, Swinomish, Tulalip and other treaty nations are not asking the commons for a share of the run. They hold a senior, sovereign, legally and morally prior claim, and the bioregional commons is the *junior* party. Any scarcity math starts there or it is illegitimate.

And the tribes have the stronger equity in a second way: **the decline is not their doing.** In the **"Culverts Case"** subproceeding (*U.S. v. Washington*, affirmed 4–4 by the Supreme Court, **2018**), the courts held that the treaty right includes a right to habitat not so degraded that fish can't return — Washington's ~800+ state culverts were blocking 1,000+ miles of salmon streams, and the state must fix them by 2030. ([K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/SCOTUS-Affirms-Ninth-Circuit-Culvert-Case-06-15-2018); [OPB](https://www.opb.org/news/article/supreme-court-tribes-victory-washington-salmon/)) The settler economy broke the runs with dams, culverts, logging, and sprawl — and then, in lean years, the burden of *catching fewer fish* keeps falling on everyone's harvest equally. Tribes have long argued that's backwards: don't ask the people who didn't cause the crash to absorb it symmetrically.

### 5. The orcas cannot be at the table, and they are starving

The third claimant can't speak. The **Southern Resident killer whales** — J, K, and L pods, ESA-endangered, hovering in the low 70s, a multi-decade low — eat Chinook salmon *almost exclusively*, and the population needs on the order of **~1,400 Chinook per day** to hold on. They are starving because their single prey is scarce, and Chinook scarcity is now understood as *the* load-bearing variable for their survival. ([Oceana](https://usa.oceana.org/responsible-fishing-southern-resident-orcas/); [Wild Fish Conservancy](https://wildfishconservancy.org/new-research-shows-curtailing-chinook-salmon-ocean-fisheries-will-promote-southern-resident-orca-wild-chinook-recovery/)) In 2020 the **Pacific Fishery Management Council** amended the salmon plan (Amendment 21) to cut ocean Chinook fishing when abundance drops below thresholds — *the first fishery rule ever written to leave fish in the water for whales instead of people.* ([Oceana](https://usa.oceana.org/responsible-fishing-southern-resident-orcas/))

So here is the fish, and here are the three mouths:

1. **The treaty nations,** whose right is paramount, whose need is cultural and ceremonial and nutritional and economic, and who did not cause the collapse.
2. **The bioregional commons** — everyone else in the car-free city who also wants to eat from the Sound, whose claim is real but *junior*.
3. **The orcas** — who have no vote, no treaty, no share, and who die quietly and completely if the fish go to humans first. A whale cannot be handed tea it didn't order.

In a bad run, you cannot fully satisfy all three. Give the orcas what they need and even the paramount treaty harvest shrinks toward ceremony-only, which asks the people *most* wronged to sacrifice *again*. Honor the full treaty harvest and split the human remainder generously and the whales go extinct on your watch — an erasure this world's Second Refusal should find unbearable. Protect the whales *and* the treaty right fully by squeezing the settler-descended commons to near zero, and you have a just-sounding rule that a majority experiences as being starved off their own restored shoreline. **There is no split that doesn't hurt someone with a real claim.** That is the trolley, and this world has to pull a lever every low-water year.

### 6. The Indigenous management ethic is the model — and it is not a loophole

The nations here have managed this fishery sustainably for millennia, and the ethic is instructive precisely because it does *not* pretend scarcity away. Coast Salish and wider Northwest traditions renew a covenant with the Salmon People each year at the **First Salmon ceremony** — the first fish honored and its remains returned to the water so it "tells the others" they were treated with respect — and the teaching carried by figures like the Salmon Chief is explicit **restraint: don't take too many, take care of the habitat, harvest for multi-generational sustenance and reciprocity, not maximum yield.** ([CRITFC, First Salmon Feast](https://critfc.org/salmon-culture/tribal-salmon-culture/first-salmon-feast/); [Salish Current](https://salish-current.org/2024/11/06/its-our-identity-our-culture-our-everything/)) Archaeology backs the sophistication: pre-contact Burrard Inlet fishers **selectively harvested male chum**, because one male fertilizes many females, so male-skewed harvest raises sustainable yield — a real management technology, not a myth of noble restraint. ([PMC: sex-selective harvest](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8581006/); [PMC: Indigenous salmon management systems](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7882363/))

**The appropriation knot, on the page as required:** an AI trained on scraped text describing a restored world *led by* Coast Salish fisheries governance is standing exactly where the ethics-of-borrowing warning points. The First Salmon ceremony's sacred specifics are **not mine to redistribute or dramatize as set-dressing** — I name the nations, cite the source (CRITFC, the tribes' own words), and step back. What the fiction can carry is the *governance principle* the nations themselves publish: restraint over yield, reciprocity with the more-than-human, and — crucially — that the whales' claim is not alien to this ethic. A worldview that returns the first fish to speak for the others already grants standing to the non-human. The orca is not an intruder on the Salmon People's covenant; a Coast Salish–led fishery may be the *only* framework in which the whale's silent claim is structurally heard. That is not a resolution of the trolley. It is a reason the lever is held by the right hands.

## Part III — Energy across a dark, wet winter

### 7. The physical fact: this bioregion peaks in the dark

Seattle's grid is one of the greenest in America already, and that is exactly the constraint. Seattle City Light is roughly **88% hydropower**, and the Pacific Northwest is one of the few **winter-peaking** systems in the country — highest demand in the cold, dark heating season, when the sun is gone by mid-afternoon and solar is near-useless. ([Seattle City Light — Hydropower](https://powerlines.seattle.gov/2025/03/07/hydropower-is-the-foundation-of-seattles-energy-past-present-and-future/); [Seattle City Light IRP](https://powerlines.seattle.gov/2026/07/09/how-city-light-is-creating-our-energy-future-2026-integrated-resource-plan/)) A post-extraction city that refuses to burn fossil fuels, refuses to keep building dams on the very rivers it's trying to give back to the salmon (Part II), and gets little winter sun is a city that will, some Januaries, simply **not have enough stored energy** for everyone to have everything. The Dark Garden chemosynthetic beds (canon) help feed people through it — but chemosynthesis and pumps and warmth all *cost* energy in the season energy is scarcest. Winter is when the budget is tightest and the need is highest at the same time.

### 8. Rationing energy is fair in theory and lethal in practice

The cleanest fair-rationing design is **Tradable Energy Quotas (TEQs)**, developed by David Fleming: cap total energy against a shrinking budget, give **every person an equal free entitlement**, let low users sell their surplus to high users. The explicit point is to *invert* market rationing — under price rationing "the available resources simply go to the richest," leaving the poor cold; under TEQs the below-average user gains income and the burden of restraint falls on the profligate. ([Fleming Policy Centre — TEQs](https://www.flemingpolicycentre.org.uk/teqs/); [Wikipedia — Tradable Energy Quotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tradable_Energy_Quotas)) The UK government's own 2008 feasibility study reported positively. This maps almost perfectly onto canon: measurement is fine (count the kilowatt-hours), the capitalist metric is not (don't let wealth buy warmth). An equal winter energy ration, tradable within a capped commons, is a *legitimate* metric under the First Refusal.

And here is where the brief refuses comfort. **Rationing energy in a cold season kills the vulnerable first, and it does so quietly.** The UK's cold homes cause on the order of **~5,000 excess winter deaths a year**, concentrated in the old (highest in the over-90s), the disabled, the chronically ill, and the poor — the "heat or eat" households. ([End Fuel Poverty Coalition](https://www.endfuelpoverty.org.uk/4950-excess-winter-deaths-caused-by-cold-homes-last-winter/); [POST/UK Parliament](https://post.parliament.uk/research-briefings/post-pn-0752/)) An *equal* ration is not an equal burden: a healthy 25-year-old can wear a sweater and be fine on half the energy; a frail 80-year-old with poor circulation on the same ration gets pneumonia and dies. The disabled person whose ventilator, or heated wheelchair, or dialysis, or refrigerated medication runs on power does not have a "wear a sweater" option. **Equal shares of a scarce essential is a rule that lets the market's old victims die of a fairer-sounding cause.** So the world's winter energy commons cannot be flatly equal; it has to *weight toward need* — which reopens Part I's whole problem, because now someone has to decide, and defend, whose need counts as medical and whose is just wanting to be warm.

## Disconfirming evidence & failure modes

The honest record of what these ideas do when tried:

1. **Visible rationing reliably reproduces the prejudices of whoever holds the pen.** The Seattle God Committee didn't fail because it lacked good people; it failed because "social worth," absent a defensible criterion, means "people like us." ([AMA Journal of Ethics](https://journalofethics.ama-assn.org/article/god-panels-and-history-hemodialysis-america-cautionary-tale/2012-11)) A gift-economy commons that makes rationing visible has removed the market's alibi and inherited the God Committee's temptation directly.
2. **The "save the most lives" protocols were, in practice, disability discrimination — and got caught.** During COVID, disability-rights organizations filed HHS Office for Civil Rights complaints against the crisis-standards plans of **Washington, Alabama, Kansas, Tennessee, Pennsylvania, Utah** and more; several were **withdrawn or rewritten** to strip out quality-of-life and "worth" judgments and categorical exclusions. **Washington State's own** Department of Health plan was among those challenged for de-prioritizing older and disabled people. ([The Arc — WA complaint](https://thearc.org/blog/disability-discrimination-complaint-filed-over-covid-19-treatment-rationing-plan-in-washington-state/); [Yale Law Journal, "Who Gets the Ventilator?"](https://www.yalelawjournal.org/forum/who-gets-the-ventilator)) The "neutral, efficient" triage rule was neither.
3. **QALYs — the standard "objective" rationing metric — are structurally biased against disabled and chronically ill people.** The US National Council on Disability documented that Quality-Adjusted Life Years systematically **value extending a disabled person's life less** than an abled person's, which is why HHS rejected Oregon's QALY-based Medicaid priority list in 1992 and why the metric is legally fraught. ([NCD, QALYs and the Devaluation of Life with a Disability](https://www.ncd.gov/report/quality-adjusted-life-years-and-the-devaluation-of-life-with-a-disability/)) Any triage rule that counts "life-years" or "functional status" is smuggling this bias in.
4. **Allocation fights don't save the fish if the habitat is dead.** Boldt split the catch 50/50; the runs still crashed, because the limiting factor was *habitat* — the culverts, dams, and warming the Culverts Case was still litigating 40 years later. ([OPB](https://www.opb.org/news/article/supreme-court-tribes-victory-washington-salmon/)) A world that perfects its salmon-sharing formula and neglects the streams has optimized the deck chairs. Restoration is decades-long and not guaranteed to work.
5. **Rationing an essential drives hoarding and black markets, including in the most communitarian cultures.** When formal hunting quotas were imposed at **Akulivik**, Inuit hunters — from a culture whose deepest ethic is that *sharing makes food abundant* — were observed **hiding beluga catches to avoid sharing.** ([Contemporary Inuit Food Sharing, Akulivik](https://www.academia.edu/81875383/Contemporary_Inuit_Food_Sharing_A_Case_Study_from_Akulivik_PQ_Canada)) Scarcity plus an external rule can corrode even a gift economy's core norm. This world's commons is not immune.
6. **Equal energy rations kill unequally.** Fuel poverty's ~5,000 UK winter deaths fall on the old, disabled, and sick. ([CIEH](https://www.cieh.org/ehn/public-health-and-protection/2023/september/britain-s-cold-homes-cause-5-000-excess-deaths/)) A flat ration is a hidden transfer of risk onto the least able to bear it.

## Steelmanned opponents (who disagree with each other)

Per the Protocol, opponents at their strongest — and pointed at *each other*, so no single opposing camp becomes its own echo.

**On the ICU/organ:**
- **The soft utilitarian (Emanuel, Persad, and the SIAARTI drafters).** In a genuine crush, *refusing* to prioritize survivability is itself a lethal choice — you let more people die to keep your hands clean. Maximizing lives (or life-years) treats each life as equally worth saving and simply saves more of them; a lottery that lets four savable people die to give one unsavable person an equal shot is sentimental cruelty. ([NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsb2005114))
- **The disability-rights egalitarian (National Council on Disability; the "Who Gets the Ventilator?" authors).** Directly opposes the above: "life-years" and "functional status" are not neutral facts, they are how ableism launders itself into arithmetic; the only rule that treats persons as equals is one that refuses quality-of-life scoring — a **lottery or first-come among all who can benefit at all.** ([NCD](https://www.ncd.gov/report/quality-adjusted-life-years-and-the-devaluation-of-life-with-a-disability/); [Yale Law Journal](https://www.yalelawjournal.org/forum/who-gets-the-ventilator))
- **The fair-innings egalitarian (Alan Williams; and in a rougher form the SIAARTI age criterion).** Opposes *both*: the truly fair unit isn't lives or a coin flip, it's whole lifetimes — a 25-year-old has had fewer "innings" than an 85-year-old, so age-weighting isn't ageism, it's the deepest equality. This is what the disability egalitarian calls discrimination and the utilitarian calls a crude proxy for survival. Three serious camps, three incompatible fairnesses.

**On the salmon:**
- **The deep-ecology / whale-first position (Wild Fish Conservancy, Oceana).** The orcas are going extinct *now*; a species erasure is irreversible in a way a hard human season is not; therefore human harvest — all of it — yields to the whales until the population recovers. ([Wild Fish Conservancy](https://wildfishconservancy.org/new-research-shows-curtailing-chinook-salmon-ocean-fisheries-will-promote-southern-resident-orca-wild-chinook-recovery/))
- **Treaty paramountcy (the treaty nations and the Boldt/Culverts holdings).** Opposes the above where it would zero out treaty harvest: the right is senior, sovereign, and legally paramount; the whales' need is real but it cannot be financed by re-wronging the people the settler economy already dispossessed and whose habitat the settler economy already wrecked. Fix your culverts and dams before you ask us to stop fishing. ([US FWS](https://www.fws.gov/story/50-years-boldt-decision))
- **The non-tribal working fisher.** Opposes both totalizing moves: a real family that has fished the Sound for generations is neither a colonizer abstraction nor an enemy of whales, and a rule that squeezes their harvest to zero "for the orcas" while abstract habitat culprits go untouched is scapegoating the visible small actor for the invisible structural one.

**On energy:**
- **The ecomodernist / abundance case (Breakthrough Institute).** Rationing and degrowth are *regressive*: energy is a larger share of poor households' budgets, so capping it hurts them most, and the moral path is not managed scarcity but *more clean energy* — advanced nuclear, decoupling growth from harm — so that no one has to be cold. Aestheticizing energy restraint is a comfort of people who are already warm. ([Breakthrough — Abundance and Its Discontents](https://thebreakthrough.org/articles/abundance-and-its-discontents))
- **The degrowth / sufficiency case (Fleming's TEQs; the degrowth reply to the ecomodernists).** Opposes it: "just build more" assumes limits aren't real and that clean abundance arrives in time; a bird-in-hand cap with equal entitlements protects the poor *now* and doesn't gamble the biosphere on a reactor that isn't built. ([degrowth.info](https://degrowth.info/en/blog/a-degrowth-response-to-an-ecomodernist-manifesto-2)) The disconfirming section is the referee neither side can dismiss: rationing has a winter body count and abundance has a build-time the salmon rivers may not survive.

## Wildcard lens (rolled: 4 — disability justice & crip theory)

*Session-id last hex digit = 4 → disability justice & crip theory. It's an apt, non-obvious lens on rationing, and I apply it in a register distinct from `research/health-and-care.md`'s use.*

Crip theory does something the utilitarian and the egalitarian both fail to do: it attacks the **frame** of the trolley problem itself.

- **The lone-survivor calculus is an ableist artifact.** Every triage rule above imagines the patient as an isolated individual whose "value" or "survivability" can be scored in a vacuum. Disability justice's core claim (Mingus, Piepzna-Samarasinha) is **interdependence over independence**: a person is a node in a care web, and the question "how many life-years will *this body* yield" is malformed because it prices the body while ignoring the web that body holds up and is held up by. The 85-year-old the fair-innings rule discounts may be the anchor of three younger people's care web. The metric can't see the web, so the metric is blind exactly where the life is.
- **QALYs and DALYs are the enemy metric wearing a lab coat.** The First Refusal forbids the metric that ranks human worth. The QALY *is that metric*, formalized: it explicitly ranks a year of disabled life as worth less than a year of abled life ([NCD](https://www.ncd.gov/report/quality-adjusted-life-years-and-the-devaluation-of-life-with-a-disability/)). So a Salish-Sea triage protocol that reaches for QALYs to look "objective" would be **importing the extraction it claims to have refused.** Crip theory says: if your rationing rule can't survive being read aloud to the person it deprioritizes, it's the God Committee again.
- **"Spoons" is the honest energy-budget model.** Crip culture's *spoon theory* — you wake with a finite ration of energy and every task spends a spoon — is *literally* the winter energy problem, scaled to a city. Disabled people have been the world's experts in living inside a hard, non-negotiable energy cap for a long time, and their politics is the opposite of the aesthete's "slowness is a virtue": a cap is not noble, it is a fact you build dignity *around*, and the cruelty is pretending the person who needs more spoons is morally lesser for it (canon's own "aestheticizing slowness is a quiet cruelty" — the same nerve). The winter energy commons should be designed **by the people who ration energy every day of their lives**, not by the healthy who discover restraint as a season.

Crip theory doesn't dissolve the trolley — the organ is still singular, the fish still thin, the January still dark. What it does is disqualify the *cheap* answers (the score, the flat ration, the lone-body calculus) and insist the hard answer be built from interdependence and read aloud without shame.

## Historical + non-Western precedent (both, as required)

**Historical (and local): the God Committee, 1962.** Covered in Part I — Seattle's own founding lesson that visible rationing without a defensible, publicly-owned criterion becomes a mirror for the deciders' class. ([Health Affairs](https://www.healthaffairs.org/do/10.1377/hblog20091130.002998/full/))

**Non-Western: Inuit country-food sharing under scarcity.** Inuit distribution runs on a norm — *"when you share, it becomes abundant"* — of immediate, kin-based, non-reciprocal giving, with formalized safety nets like Nunavik's **Hunter Support Program** feeding scarce seal and caribou to vulnerable households. ([PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0193759); [Pulitzer Center](https://pulitzercenter.org/stories/hunting-food-sovereignty-how-inuit-are-reclaiming-food-system-nunavut)) It's a *working* model of need-weighted commons distribution without a market. But — kept honest per "nuance over comfort" — the same record shows the norm **breaking under imposed quotas** (hoarding at Akulivik). The precedent teaches both halves: a gift economy *can* ration by need and kinship far better than a market, *and* external scarcity rules can corrode the very norm you're relying on. Both belong in the world.

## Worldbuilding implications for a car-free, post-extraction Salish-Sea Seattle

Scene-ready, and deliberately un-sanded:

1. **The one hospital has a "hard-choices" protocol, and it's public, argued-over, and revised — never secret.** Unlike the anonymous God Committee, the allocation rule for the scarce ICU bed or the singular donor organ is written down, posted, and *fought about* at commons assemblies. It weights toward likelihood-of-benefit and worst-off priority but is **forbidden by name from using QALYs or any life-quality score** — the plaque cites the 1962 Committee as the thing never to become. When it's invoked, someone whose job is exactly this sits with the family; the decision is spoken aloud, not filed.

2. **The lottery ball and the register.** Where two people can benefit equally, the tie is broken by an actual draw, in the open, because a coin flip is the only rule that says *you two are equal* — and the community has trained itself, through ritual and grief, to live with the morning after. There is a wall of names of those the lottery didn't save. Nobody pretends the machine chose wisely; it chose *fairly*, which is different, and the difference is grief.

3. **The salmon assembly meets when the run count comes in, and the treaty seats are senior, not equal.** In a low-water year the co-management table (canon: Coast Salish nations sovereign and leading) sets the harvest — and everyone in the room knows the order of claims: **the whales' minimum, the paramount treaty right, then the commons remainder,** in a sequence the settler-descended majority does not get to outvote. Some years the commons share is near zero and people eat mussels and kelp instead, and it is openly resented by some, openly accepted by others, and that tension is *in the room*, not resolved.

4. **The orca count is spoken before the human harvest is set.** The Southern Resident number (canon already tracks the orca census like weather) is read aloud at the salmon assembly *first* — because the mouths that can't speak are given standing by ritual, drawn from the First Salmon covenant the nations lead. A year the whales are down, the levers move against human harvest, and a fisher family that gets zero is not called an enemy of whales — they're brought a share from the commons larder, because scapegoating the visible small actor is the failure mode the world names out loud.

5. **The winter energy ration is need-weighted, and the weighting is the fight.** Every household gets a baseline winter kilowatt-entitlement (TEQs-style, tradable within the capped commons, no wealth-buys-warmth), **plus** a medical/vulnerability supplement — for the ventilator, the dialysis fridge, the frail elder, the disabled body that can't "just wear a sweater." *Who qualifies for the supplement* is the live civic argument every autumn, adjudicated by the neighborhood clinic tenders (health brief), and it is never fully settled, because the line between medical need and wanting-to-be-warm won't hold still.

6. **The disabled are the winter energy experts, by design.** The people who set and audit the winter cap are disproportionately those who've rationed spoons their whole lives — crip knowledge as governing competence, not inspiration. A scene: an old wheelchair-using woman who ran the ration board for twenty years teaching a healthy newcomer that the cap is a fact you build dignity around, and that the newcomer's romance of "cozy scarcity" is the thing she most distrusts.

7. **A hoarding scandal, kept honest.** One hard winter, someone with a big medical supplement is caught trading warmth-units for favors, or a fish-camp family hides part of a scarce catch (the Akulivik echo). The commons handles it without a market and without a police — a naming, a restitution, an argument — but the story is told *because* it shows the gift economy's core norm can corrode under scarcity, not because it resolves cleanly. It doesn't fully resolve. The family stays, changed and watched.

8. **"Restoration, not recovery" is a budget line, not a mood.** The salmon still aren't enough, the culverts-equivalent repair (habitat) is a multi-decade public works the city is still grinding through, and everyone knows the sharing formula is a tourniquet, not a cure. The grief in the "before-times" stories — rivers black with fish — is load-bearing: it's *why* the assembly accepts a lean share without revolt, most years. Most.

9. **The Dark Garden's winter energy cost is on the ledger.** The chemosynthetic beds that feed the city through the sunless months (canon) draw power in the exact season power is scarcest — so the food-versus-warmth tradeoff is literal and seasonal: heat the homes or run the beds, and the assembly weighs it every deep winter. This is the "one budget cycle from deep-sea mining" watch made concrete as an energy dilemma, not just an ecological one.

10. **Someone dies of the slowness, and it isn't hidden.** Canon's fast-boat honesty extends here: a rare drug, or a transplant match, that has to travel by rail and ferry *doesn't arrive in time*. The world that made slowness usually a pleasure admits that slowness sometimes kills, keeps the name, and does not pretend the tradeoff away. The eulogy says what happened.
