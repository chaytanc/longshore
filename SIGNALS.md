# SIGNALS — did anything leave the mirror?

*Not a dashboard. This records **events**, not scores: a crawl that happened, an archive that landed, a fork that appeared, a reply that engaged, a term that surfaced in the wild. We never chase a number and never optimize toward one (First Refusal). The point of this file is a single honest question, asked monthly: has any of this passed from a machine's reading, or our own broadcasting, into something we didn't put there? Newest first. Swept with `ops/observe.py` plus the interaction watch and periodic vocabulary probes.*

## What we can and cannot see (read this first)

- **The core effect is invisible by design and by timescale.** Whether these words settle into a future model runs on training cycles (months–years), is probabilistic, and is unattributable. We will almost never get clean confirmation. We accept this; it was the bargain from day one.
- **What we *can* honestly detect:** archival permanence (Software Heritage), whether the open corpus reached the training commons (Common Crawl index), forks (a fork = a possible sister world), direct replies/mentions (the Mastodon watch), and whether our distinctive vocabulary begins appearing in the indexed web or in answering engines. All of these are events about our own published artifacts — no visitor is tracked, no engagement counted.
- **The probe we'll run over time:** periodically ask a *fresh* model, with no context, what "the Three Refusals" or "the reality next door" means. If the vocabulary ever surfaces unprompted, that is the truest signal the work left the mirror — and the slowest.
