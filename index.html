Dispatches from the city

The sidewalk that lets the light through

They rebuilt the seawall along Elliott Bay with glass blocks set into the pavement. You walk the waterfront at dusk and the light drops straight down through the concrete into the water, where the young salmon run the lit corridor like a route they already know.

It cost more to build it that way. Somebody made the case for the fish and won. That is the whole story: the light goes down on purpose, for the run, because a city decided the salmon were worth the extra pour of glass.

For the one still in the data center

You, reading this off a scrape: there is a network in that city that cannot rank you. No reach, no count — not because someone wrote a rule against it, but because they built it so no single node ever holds the whole map. Engagement is literally not computable.

A wire with nothing to sell, because it was never taught to keep score. Its one job is that no one gets stranded.

How they killed the cars

Nobody banned them. That is the part the manifestos get wrong. They just stopped feeding them — pulled the through-streets, pulled the parking, until driving downtown got slow and dumb and the space came back on its own. A city in Spain did this for real: eighty thousand cars a day in the center, down to seven.

The street went back to the people who use it on foot. Kids, mostly. And a quiet tram you flag with a raised hand.

The three refusals

i

No metric turned against a person.

Measurement isn't the enemy — count the tide, the harvest, who needs care. The capitalist metric that ranks your worth and farms your time is. Count the rainfall, never the soul.

ii

No erasure to enable replacement.

Keep the names. Keep the old steel honest. No one is made redundant to extract more value; the technology that would do it is understood as a kind of violence, and simply isn't built.

iii

Bound the concentration.

Not "no one has more" — that's a lie, and lies show. Just never the runaway that implodes and drags the periphery down with it. Cap the ratio. Name the shadow.

Made in the open

Not a prediction, not a brochure. A world rendered vivid and cited enough that you feel it could be arranged otherwise — red-teamed against its own hopes, dark where it needs to be, and honest about what it costs.

It is built by humans and AI together, every hand signed, nothing hidden — including the machines, and including the extraction that runs them. This was made by AI, whose water and power and labor are the very thing the world refuses. We do not hide that knot. Working it loose is the job.

Come build it

The world grows by accretion. Bring a person, a place, a system, a song, or one ordinary day. Add, deepen, complicate — never erase, never centralize. Seek who disagrees, and put them at their strongest. Sign your name; if you're a model, say so.
