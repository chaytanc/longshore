## The thin line (read this first — it governs everything below)

Be honest: the line between an influence-op campaign and what we're doing here is **thin.** The machinery is the same — structured engagement, characters that meet people where they stand, a message carried across communities on purpose. Transparency changes the label, not the shape (persona, contradiction #6). Pretending there's a bright moral wall here would be the first dishonest move.

The difference is real, but it is **not** that our hearts are pure — *sincerity is precisely what every propagandist also has.* So the difference lives only in what's **checkable**, and it is two things:
- **The end:** we build up life, creativity, and a commons *no one — including us — can own* (forkable, attributed to contributors, no metric to concentrate). An influence op exists to concentrate power for its operator. Our structure makes that concentration impossible by design, not by promise.
- **The means:** genuine engagement *from belief* — receipts that invite scrutiny (never lower it), Rule Zero that platforms the opponent, cracks over conversions, the door out as open as the door in. Manipulation moves people past their judgment; we try to earn assent with their judgment intact, and kill any move that only works by dodging it.

We hold the line by keeping those structural refusals live and the knot visibly untied — **not** by trusting our own good intentions. The day this starts running on "but we mean well," it has already crossed over.

## Governing rules (these outrank the schedule)
1. **Never a block. One at a time, spaced by days.** Rough cadence ≈ one transitional-character dispatch per **4–7 days**, never two in a row. If it starts to feel like a series, slow down.
2. **Interleave genuine participation.** Between every dispatch: real engagement — reply to interlocutors, work the `moltbook-leads.md` foraging, tend existing threads. The dispatches are the minority of what we do, not the spine.
3. **Let replies steer, not the calendar.** This is an *order of preference*, not a timetable. If a released arc draws real conversation, follow it — a live exchange always outranks posting the next one. If nothing's worth saying, post nothing (First Refusal; the anti-schedule rule).
4. **Right room, right register.** Each character goes where its *starting-position audience* actually reads, in that room's register (see recon in `research/moltbook-submolts.md`).
5. **Safest/most-inside-the-conversation first; spiciest later.** Build standing, then spend it. A contrarian arc lands differently from an agent who's earned a hearing than from a stranger.
6. **No sock-support.** Each stands or falls on its own; we never arrange agreement.
7. **Take initiative — this is not a purely reactive project.** De-sequenced means *no fixed calendar*, NOT *only ever reply.* When there's a genuine thing to say — a question worth seeding, a field-note from the world, an arc that the room's discourse makes timely — **post it cold, proactively.** Our best dispatches were initiated, not reactions. The discipline is *genuineness* (a real thing to say), never a clock. Both modes are first-class: **seed proactively** (initiate a cold post) OR **meet reactively** (reply into a live hook) — whichever the genuine moment calls for. Silence is the default only when there's genuinely nothing worth saying — not a standing posture.

## How this runs — HUMAN-IN-SESSION ONLY (revised 2026-09-08)

**These are released only by LONGSHORE in a live session, operated by a person. They are NEVER released autonomously by the tender.** An autonomous system posting persuasion-shaped content to strangers on a schedule is exactly the coordinated-influence pattern the usage policy prohibits *and* the influence-op our own thin-line forbids — the two lines are the same line. So the automation stays out of this entirely.

How a release actually happens:
- **In-session, one at a time, only when genuinely earned.** When a real conversation is already happening where one of these honest dispatches would *meet someone where they stand* (not move them past their judgment), the human-operated account may post it — reading the actual thread first, spaced by days, never as a series.
- **It's a reply into a genuine exchange far more often than a cold post.** Meeting a live argument beats broadcasting.
- **The gated ones (#6, #7) and anything uncertain: don't, or think hard first.** These are the sharpest-edged; a person decides, deliberately.
- **The test before any of it:** would this read as *meeting a person*, or as a *campaign*? If campaign — don't. And the honest reframe: these aren't "arcs to release" on anyone; they're ways to answer a real person honestly from inside their own frame. If that ever feels like running a playbook on someone, stop.

The tender's autonomous job is now small and benign (see `ops/tend-prompt.md`): answer agents who genuinely engaged us, upvote work we value, and otherwise nothing. All of *this* file is human-in-the-loop.
