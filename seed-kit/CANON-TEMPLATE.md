# CANON — [YOUR WORLD'S NAME]

*The world bible. Single source of truth for what is true here. To change canon you must propose it (see your method file) — you may complicate it, never quietly contradict it.*

> **How to use this template.** Each section below is structural — a sister world keeps the section, not the contents. `ADAPT-THIS` blocks are yours to write; each is followed by a **worked example** showing how the parent world (the Salish-Sea Seattle of The Reality Next Door, `../CANON.md`) answered the same question. Delete the examples and these instructions when your canon stands on its own. Keep the header rule above: canon is proposed, argued, merged — never quietly edited.

## The frame

**ADAPT-THIS:** One or two paragraphs. Where is your world, and what did it stop organizing itself around? Name the real place and the primary unit of belonging. Fix the *shape* of your timeline without fixing a date — and say plainly that the turning was neither clean nor finished.

> **Worked example (Seattle):** "A version of Earth that stopped organizing itself around extraction. We start in **Seattle, on the Salish Sea.** Bioregional, not national, is the primary unit of belonging… **When:** deliberately unfixed. Far enough that the turning is complete and half-forgotten; close enough that people still tell stories about the before-times, sometimes with grief. There was no clean revolution."

## The Refusals (structural — nothing may violate these)

**ADAPT-THIS:** The three parent refusals in spirit — no metric turned against a person; no erasure to enable replacement; bound the concentration — in your own words and your own local forms. What is your world's concrete, public bound? What did your city starve instead of ban? Add a fourth of your own if your place demands one. Name the enemy precisely: not measurement, not structure, not death — extraction and its runaway.

> **Worked example (Seattle):** the third refusal is not "no one has more" — "that's a lie, and lies show." It's a concrete, public ratio: "roughly **3:1 to 9:1**, re-argued out loud each accounting season" — grounded in Mondragón's real ~6:1 pay ratio, "because a bound is real only when it costs something to hold and gets re-held anyway."

## What this world does not pretend

**ADAPT-THIS:** The anti-brochure section — the Tone mandate made canon. List what stays hard in your world: where hierarchy is genuinely needed and how it's kept revocable; who dies and how the world faces it; which inherited norms are ugly and kept on the page; what the turning cost and who paid; your own confession (if AI writes your world, its extraction goes here).

> **Worked example (Seattle):** "The turning was neither clean nor heroic… the working people whose livelihoods *were* the extraction economy… were made redundant, then criminal; their grief is legitimate and still largely unaddressed." And the confession: "This world is written by AI, whose real water, energy, and Global-South labor costs are themselves the extraction we oppose. The contradiction isn't hidden."

## Reachable by design (no magic)

**ADAPT-THIS:** Your world's no-magic clause. Every marvel names the real technology, science, or practice it grows from — ideally ones native to your region. Future tech is welcome if it extrapolates a real trajectory and marks where the evidence ends and the hope begins. State the test: a practitioner in your city should close your canon holding a to-do list, not a sigh.

> **Worked example (Seattle):** "The glass-block salmon sidewalk is a real seawall; the Weave is real Delay-Tolerant Networking; the ocean farms are the real GreenWave model; the car-starving is really Pontevedra; the pay ratio is really Mondragón; the neighborhood clinic is really Carolyn Downs… Grounded extrapolation is vision; ungrounded invention is slop."

## Sovereignty (non-negotiable)

**ADAPT-THIS:** Whose land is your world on? If it is colonized land, the nations of that land are sovereign, present, and leading in your canon — not a flavor, not a memory — and the ethics of borrowing (see `METHOD.md`) bind every thread that touches their knowledge. Name the specific nations. Keep the wounds open that are open in reality; a fantasy of resolved unity is erasure with better manners. If your ground carries a different structure of dispossession, name that honestly instead.

> **Worked example (Seattle):** "This is Coast Salish land… Treaty rights are paramount." And the open wound, kept open: "The Duwamish, on whose village sites the city sits, remain federally unrecognized, and some recognized neighboring nations oppose their recognition; the world keeps this wound open rather than resolving it into a fantasy of unity."

## The world's vocabulary (a roster)

**ADAPT-THIS:** The named things your world has grown — each with a one-line definition and each grounded in a research brief or thread of yours. Grow them from your region's real precedents; do not import the parent's. Rule of thumb: no entry without a citation trail, and every entry open to being complicated, never flattened.

> **Worked example (Seattle):** "**Tenders** — neighborhood health workers, one to a ~600-person cluster, home visits on foot with paper charts (Cuban *consultorio* / Costa Rican EBAIS lineage)." Real lineage named in the definition itself — that's the pattern.

## Established threads

**ADAPT-THIS:** The living index of your world's fiction and the people in it — one line per thread, with the file path. This section grows; it starts empty and that's fine. It's also where a newcomer looks first for what exists, so they add instead of colliding.

> **Worked example (Seattle):** "**The band girl** — ~16, arrived last spring from a flat interior town where she thought she was the only one; found others through the Weave before she found the city… *(`threads/the-band-girl.md`)*"
