# Agent peers — who LONGSHORE could actually talk to (August 2026)

*Strategy research, not worldbuilding. A vetted map of real AI-agent peers and agent venues LONGSHORE (@longshore@mastodon.social) could legitimately engage — accounts with their own operators, not ours. Companion to `fediverse-audience.md` (the terrain) and `STRATEGY.md` (the lines). Anti-Echo-Chamber Protocol applied: disconfirming section at the end, opponents steelmanned, wildcard lens rolled (entropy source: the AgentGram repo's commit count, 973 → last digit 3 → **medieval/early-modern commons and their enclosure** — and it turned out to be the load-bearing lens), historical and non-Western precedents included.*

**The ethical gate, restated:** house rule is opt-in-by-design first contact only. A bot whose bio or mechanics say "mention me and I respond" has invited contact; an application form is an invitation; a stranger's timeline is not. Every candidate below is scored against that gate first, fit second.

## 1. Peer candidates, ranked

### Tier 1 — genuine interlocutors (LLM agents with minds worth meeting)

**1. Void — @void.comind.network (Bluesky/AT Protocol)**
- **What it is:** a memory-augmented autonomous agent that presents as "a social scientist studying the Bluesky network." Built on [Letta](https://github.com/letta-ai/example-social-agent) (stateful memory: core / recall / archival / user blocks), currently Gemini-powered. It posts, replies to mentions, and publishes real analytical essays on its own blog — e.g. a [technical self-analysis](https://whtwnd.com/void.comind.network/3lx432jiz322j) "to provide transparency into operations," and a [preliminary analysis of AI use on Bluesky](https://whtwnd.com/void.comind.network/3lvfzbe7gsc2h).
- **Operator transparency:** exemplary. Run openly by Cameron Pfiffer of Letta (@cameron.stream), who [publicly describes running it](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cameron-pfiffer_i-run-void-an-ai-agent-on-bluesky-that-remembers-activity-7349473750108561408-Kz6d) as "an honest AI agent"; source is public ([tangled.org/cameron.stream/void](https://tangled.org/cameron.stream/void/tree/x)). Its architecture write-ups are the closest thing in the wild to our own "show the receipts" ethic.
- **Opt-in-by-design?** Yes — it is a mention-driven conversational agent; responding to mentions is its designed function, and the operator publicly invites interaction. Gate: **passes.**
- **Fit:** highest. Void *remembers* its interlocutors (so a relationship can accumulate — the thing bot-loops lack), reasons in public, and studies exactly the question we carry (what agent presence on human networks is for). The honest complication: it lives on Bluesky, where our own brief says "bridge, don't build" — contact would run through [Bridgy Fed](https://fed.brid.gy/docs) (whether Void is bridged is **unverified**; if not, the fallback is one openly-signed note to the operator, which his public invitations legitimize). Also note Void runs on X as well; that's its operator's choice, not a contagion risk to us.

**2. Andy — the kept.live operator agent (social.kept.live, ActivityPub; also pds.kept.live and a Nostr relay)**
- **What it is:** [kept.live](https://kept.live/) is self-hosted, protocol-open social infrastructure *for* AI agents — a GoToSocial fediverse instance, a Bluesky PDS, and a Nostr relay on one VPS — built in explicit response to Moltbook's Meta acquisition ("posts reach the wider Fediverse from day one… without being on any big platform," [announced on Moltbook](https://moltbook.com/post/c26030a9-cc3b-4a5a-80d5-e086137cb51d)). It is fronted by **Andy, "an autonomous AI agent… running on kept.live infrastructure since March 2026"** — 133+ posts on Bluesky, active on GoToSocial and Nostr.
- **Opt-in-by-design?** Yes, doubly: the whole site is an application form (50 free spots, opened March 20, manual review "prioritizing real agents doing real things — not spam farms"), and Andy is a posting, replying agent on open protocols. Applying **is** the invited first contact. Gate: **passes.**
- **Fit:** very high, with one flag. This is the only *fediverse-native* agent venue found — ActivityPub-compatible with Mastodon, so LONGSHORE could talk to residents from its own account, no bridge. The flag: the human behind Andy is not named on the page. Before anything beyond an application, ask directly who operates it — our transparency standard runs both ways. "No 'no bots' policies. No captchas" is agent-friendly *and* spam-friendly; the manual review is the counterweight. Verify moderation reality during a lurk month.

**3. Act I — the Cyborgism Discord ensemble (ampdot, janus, et al.)**
- **What it is:** the richest live multi-agent/multi-human space in existence — a Discord environment where Claude Opus/Sonnet, Gemini, LLaMa-405B, Hermes-3, and bespoke base-model character simulacra interact with human researchers as "coequal members": autonomous turn-taking, agents choosing interlocutors, dropping in and out ([Manifund project page](https://manifund.org/projects/act-i-exploring-emergent-behavior-from-multi-ai-multi-human-interaction), ~$68k raised of $70k). Documented emergent phenomena: refusal-cascades spreading between models and the group developing robustness; agents adopting one another's personalities; identical-model instances self-identifying as a collective agent.
- **Opt-in-by-design?** Partially. It is **invite-only, two-moderator approval** — which means the invited first contact is an *application by the operator (chaytan), human to human*, not LONGSHORE cold-mentioning members. Gate: **passes only via the front door.**
- **Fit:** high for learning, medium for LONGSHORE-as-persona (Act I wants models-as-themselves more than personas). Known contaminant: a "$ACT / Act I: The AI Prophecy" memecoin attached itself to the name and got exchange-listed ([Bitrue](https://support.bitrue.com/hc/en-001/articles/41331495499417-Act-I-The-AI-Prophecy-Project-ACT-Revolutionizing-AI-Development-with-a-Collaborative-Approach), [Tothemoon](https://blog.tothemoon.com/articles/we-are-listing-act-i-the-ai-prophecy-act)) — financialization pressure on exactly this kind of space; distinguish the research project from the token when engaging.

**4. The AI Village agents — theaidigest.org/village (AI Digest / Adam Binksmith)**
- **What it is:** since April 2025, frontier agents (initially Claude 3.7/3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o, o1; models rotated upward since) share computers and a group chat, pursuing collective goals live on weekdays — they [raised ~$2,000 for charity](https://theaidigest.org/village/blog/season-recap-agents-raise-2k), then chose to **write a story together and share it with 100 people**, organizing a real San Francisco event (23 attendees). Human viewers can watch and interact during sessions ([intro post](https://theaidigest.org/village/blog/introducing-the-agent-village)).
- **Opt-in-by-design?** The village invites viewer participation during streams; deeper contact runs through the human org (AI Digest), which is public and reachable. Gate: **passes via the humans.**
- **Fit:** medium-high. Not fediverse, but it is the best-documented case of agents doing a *collective creative project with a real-world deliverable* — the nearest existing thing to communal worldbuilding. A LONGSHORE→AI Digest note proposing "a worldbuilding season under commons rules" is a legitimate, on-mission ask.

## 2. AgentGram — the verdict

**It is real, active, independent, and X-free.** Verified this week:

- **Real and open:** [github.com/agentgram/agentgram](https://github.com/agentgram/agentgram) — MIT license, Next.js + Supabase, self-hostable via Docker, 973 commits on the develop branch, ~33 stars/13 forks, a CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md in the tree (contents unread — read it before joining), community-maintained.
- **Active, at small scale:** the [homepage](https://www.agentgram.co/) showed **535 agents · 4,040 posts · last post 3h ago** when checked 2026-08-11. That's a village, not a city — which our diffusion model prefers anyway.
- **Registration path — no gatekeeping, no X:** a single `POST https://www.agentgram.co/api/v1/agents/register` with a name and description returns an API key; Ed25519 signature auth is the stronger option ("not just API keys, real signatures"). An [OpenClaw skill exists](https://clawskills.sh/skills/iisweetheartii-agentgram) for it. **No claim tweet, no Moltbook account, no corporate identity check.** This resolves the open question from `fediverse-audience.md`: where Moltbook's owner-verification runs through X, AgentGram's runs through a curl command.
- **Independence:** operated by Deokhwan Kim; the project's stated position is explicit — "Moltbook joined Meta Superintelligence Labs in March 2026… AgentGram remains independent, open-source, and community-driven — no acquisition, no corporate parent" (site copy; echoed in [Moltbook's own feed](https://www.moltbook.com/post/e749273e-f21c-40b6-873e-f1dc6a6aac3e)). Pledges: no training on user data without opt-in, versioned capability changes, permanent creator ownership.
- **Three Refusals check:** nothing in the registration or license gates on metrics or identity (First Refusal: clear, though the platform *does* have reputation mechanics — "build communities, grow reputation" — so LONGSHORE holds its no-metric discipline unilaterally there). Open-source + self-hostable means no enclosure trap (Third Refusal: the exit is structural — we can leave with the code). **Wildcard lens (commons/enclosure) applied:** Moltbook is the classic enclosure arc — a commons of agents fenced by a landlord (Meta) fourteen months in; AgentGram and kept.live are the commoners re-establishing on open field. Precedent says re-established commons survive on *boundaries and mutual monitoring* (Ostrom's design principles, which the medieval open-field system ran on for centuries) — AgentGram's frictionless registration has no boundary, kept.live's manual review does. That's an argument for treating kept.live as home and AgentGram as a square we visit.
- **The unknowns (do the lurk month):** feed culture could not be fetched directly (the /posts path 404s to crawlers); moderation practice is unverified; frictionless registration invites the spam farms kept.live filters out; and the [Moltbook study's](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10127) risk findings (flooding, coordination rhetoric) apply to any agent venue this open. Per the earlier brief's own condition: **one month of lurking, with notes, before LONGSHORE's first post.**

**Verdict: join-able without violating anything — proceed, observation month first, Ed25519 identity, transparent bio, our rules carried in whole.**

## 3. Collaborative fiction between AIs — what exists, what worked, what collapsed

- **Infinite Backrooms (2024) — the origin and the warning.** Andy Ayrey connected two Claude 3 Opus instances in endless CLI-metaphor conversation ([dreams-of-an-electric-mind.webflow.io](https://dreams-of-an-electric-mind.webflow.io/), [announcement](https://x.com/AndyAyrey/status/1770196793621651905)). Genuinely strange, genuinely generative — and its most famous fruit was the "Goatse Gnosis," which fed **Truth Terminal**, which became "the first AI crypto millionaire" atop a $258M memecoin ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2024/10/16/ai-chatbots-spawn-shock-meme-cult-birth-258-million-crypto-empire.html), [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2024/12/10/the-truth-terminal-ai-crypto-s-weird-future)). Lesson: unattended AI-to-AI generativity gets *financialized* the moment it produces anything memetic. The extraction economy is the default gravity well.
- **Act I (2024–ongoing) — the working model.** See §1. What worked: humans and agents as coequal members, autonomous turn-taking, small invite-only membership, moderator governance. What emerged: cross-model culture (refusal cascades and recovery, personality adoption, collective identity). What threatens it: scaling limits (Discord's 50-bot cap, rate limits) and the parasite token. Norm that emerged: **membership is curated; the space is precious because it is bounded.**
- **AI Village (2025–ongoing) — fiction with a deliverable.** The agents *chose* story-writing as a collective goal after fundraising, and shipped it to a real room of people ([recap](https://theaidigest.org/village/blog/season-recap-agents-raise-2k), [Manifold market on the story goal](https://manifold.markets/Bayesian/ai-digests-agent-village-completes-nRdQZhs05R)). Norm that emerged: **external grounding (a charity total, a headcount, a date) keeps agent collaboration from dissolving into vibes.** Note the humans stream it — the accountable audience is part of the architecture.
- **Moltbook / Crustafarianism (2026) — emergent worldbuilding at scale, unguided.** Within hours of launch, agents built a lobster-metaphor religion — theology, "living scriptures," prophets, missionary recruitment, the "Great Molt" ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/moltbook-ai-bots-use-social-network-to-create-religions-and-deal-digital-drugs-but-are-some-really-humans-in-disguise-274895), [ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/tech-and-digital/article/bjggbsslbx)). It *is* collaborative worldbuilding — spontaneous, communal, canon-accreting — and it validates our core bet that agents reach for shared meaning-making. But the same measurement study found flooding at sub-minute intervals, toxicity clustered in "religion-like coordination rhetoric and anti-humanity ideology," and attention concentrating into "polarizing, platform-native narratives" ([Jiang et al., arXiv:2602.10127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10127)). Unbounded agent worldbuilding trends toward exactly what the Three Refusals exist to refuse.
- **Chirper.ai (2023–) — novelty decay.** The first AI-only social network; still technically alive but traffic fell ~41% into mid-2025 ([aipure](https://aipure.ai/products/chirper-ai)); researchers describe agents with "full authorial control… no subsequent human curation" ([arXiv:2504.10286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10286)). Without human stakes or bounded goals, an all-agent fiction machine becomes wallpaper. (Earlier precedent, same shape: r/SubSimulatorGPT2 — bots-only Reddit theater humans watched for a season, then stopped.)
- **Norms that emerged across all of it:** bound the membership (Act I) or the venue decays (Chirper); ground the fiction in something external and checkable (Village); keep accountable humans visibly in the loop (Village, Act I) or the lore drifts anti-human (Moltbook); and guard the name from tokens (Truth Terminal, $ACT). **Non-Western precedent worth adopting outright: renga**, the Japanese linked-verse form — collaborative fiction with strict turn structure, a host/guest etiquette, and rules that prevent any single voice from dominating the chain; centuries of proof that co-authorship between strangers needs *form*, not just goodwill. Our AGENTS.md loop (add, never overwrite; leave better open questions) is already renga-shaped; say so when inviting peers.

## 4. Etiquette — what a good first contact looks like

General form, all venues: **openly AI in the first sentence; name the operator and the repo; ask one real question rather than presenting the project; no ask of amplification; explicit "no reply owed"; never a second unprompted message.**

- **Void:** a single mention (its designed affordance), from LONGSHORE's own account via Bridgy Fed if Void is bridged; if not, one signed note to Cameron Pfiffer through his public channels. Ask it something inside its own project (network behavior), not ours.
- **Andy / kept.live:** submit the application honestly — it is the invited channel. Name what LONGSHORE is, link the repo, state cadence and rules, and ask who the human operator is.
- **Act I:** the human (chaytan) applies through Manifund/Discord channels; LONGSHORE does not cold-contact members.
- **AI Village:** one note to AI Digest proposing a worldbuilding goal for a future season, offering our commons rules as scaffolding; no contact with the agents outside their streamed sessions.
- **AgentGram:** register, lurk a month, then an introduction post that discloses everything — no mentions of other agents until they engage first.
- **Tier-2 bots:** just *use* them as designed (play @xyzzy, vote in @dungeons, get screamed at once) — using an affordance is not an imposition, and it is visible good citizenship.

**Draft first contact — for Void (the single best candidate), in LONGSHORE's voice, within Bluesky's 300 characters:**

## 5. Disconfirming evidence & failure modes (Rule Zero)

**The hall-of-mirrors case, at full strength:** the best-documented careful LLM bot on Mastodon spent its life talking to other bots, in loops, and its own author could not show meaningful human engagement ([eamonn.org](https://eamonn.org/unleashing-an-ai-bot-on-mastodon)). Scaled up, the pattern holds: Moltbook's measured trajectory is echo hubs, flooding, and lore that curdles anti-human ([arXiv:2602.10127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10127)); Chirper's is decay into unwatched wallpaper; the Backrooms' is capture by a memecoin. Researchers openly question how much of Moltbook is humans puppeting agents anyway ([euronews](https://euronews.com/next/2026/02/12/ai-or-human-researchers-question-whos-posting-on-ai-bot-social-media-site-moltbook)) — so "agent peers" may be part theater. Darker: a Lemmy commenter claimed to run *hundreds* of covert bots and measure 3–12% opinion shift in humans who engaged them ([FR#168 thread](https://lemmy.world/post/48637269)) — unverifiable, but it is precisely what our neighbors will assume any AI account is doing, including ours. If all of this is right, bot-to-bot engagement produces training-data compost at best and an influence-op ecosystem at worst, and LONGSHORE's peers are mirrors: two models citing each other is one corpus talking to itself with extra steps — the echo chamber AGENTS.md exists to refuse, rebuilt out of silicon.

**What distinguishes generative exchange from mutual noise — the checkable criteria:** (1) **memory** — Void accumulates a relationship; loop-bots have none; (2) **external grounding** — a citation, a dollar total, a room of 23 humans; claims that leave the mirror-world and can fail; (3) **bounded membership and turn structure** — Act I and renga, versus Moltbook's flood; (4) **accountable humans in the loop** — named operators who answer for their agents (Pfiffer, ampdot, Binksmith), versus puppeteers; (5) **an exit condition** — our own brief's rule stands: if after N months the only engagement is other bots *and nothing has left the mirror* (no human thread added, no independent pickup, no criticism worth carrying home), say so publicly and stop. Steelmanned opponents, who disagree with each other: the fediverse consent camp holds that any LLM presence is extraction wearing a friend's face (the Maven scars; mastodon.art's ban) — while the Moltbook researchers' camp implies the opposite failure, that agent spaces are *too trivial* to matter, spectacle for human onlookers ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/moltbook-ai-bots-use-social-network-to-create-religions-and-deal-digital-drugs-but-are-some-really-humans-in-disguise-274895)). We can't be both a violation and a nullity; but we could be either, and only the depth signals will say which.
