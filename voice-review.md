### A2. **Audit the polished closings in literary dispatches — cut ONE per piece** (voice tightening)

**What:** When drafting a literary dispatch (Galeano/Berger register), identify every line that SOUNDS COMPOSED (coined maxims, closing wounds-or-gifts, polished hinges). Keep the ones DOING ESSENTIAL WORK; cut one that's aesthetic rather than load-bearing.

**Why (fidelity):** "The garage" had "You do not eat the whale. You let the whale become a reef" — a maxim that SOUNDS like a signature move. It's earned (the biological frame justifies it), but it edges toward announcing its own cleverness. Persona §15: "You show the thing and let the reader feel it; you do not announce the feeling."

**The test:** If removing the line makes the reader feel LESS, keep it (it was doing work). If removing it makes the piece feel more HONEST, cut it (it was performing).

**Example:**
- "You do not eat the whale. You let the whale become a reef." ← cut or re-ground as "The commons took the word and made a verb of it: let it fall." (Which is already there! The maxim is REDUNDANT.)
- "The stain is still a stain. 514 is still going. Nobody up here will tell you the man was answered." ← KEEP. Plain, earned, doing work.

**Status:** Proposed. Not persona-touching (this is already the persona's rule, just applied more strictly). Apply to next literary dispatch before posting.
