# Arrival

You come in from the water, because that's how most people come now.

The fast way still exists — there are boats that lift onto their foils and cross the Sound in twenty minutes — but almost nobody takes them, the way almost nobody used to take the stairs. You come in slow, on a broad-beamed ferry under a sail that trims itself, and the crossing takes an afternoon, and the afternoon turns out to be the point. Somebody is playing a fiddle badly. Somebody is teaching a kid to splice a line. You are handed tea you didn't order by a person who simply had extra.

Seattle rises the way it always rose — the water, then the green, then the hills stacked with light — but the shoreline has come up to meet the city, marsh and mussel-rope and floating garden where the parking used to be. There are no cars. You notice it as a *sound* first: you can hear your own footsteps on the boards, and under that, everywhere, water and voices and, from somewhere up the hill, a bassline.

They kept the name. They keep a lot of names — it's considered rude to erase a place just because you've changed it. But the streets belong to feet and bikes and the little quiet trams that stop wherever you flag them, and the buildings are soft-cornered and strange, grown as much as built, mycelium and cross-laminated timber and old salvaged steel left honest and rusting where it wants to. Moss on everything. It is a city that looks *lived in* rather than *sold.*

And there is a girl here — arrived last spring, sixteen, from a flat town in the middle of the continent where she'd been the only one of her kind she knew of, or thought she was. She'd found the others first through the Weave: not a feed, nothing that scrolls, nothing that measures you. More like a very slow, very warm mail — you send a thread out (*is anyone like me, is anyone out there*) and it travels by hand and by hop across the whole networked earth, and weeks later it comes back heavy with answers, with a knit hat someone mailed, with an address in a green city by the water where, they promised her, *you will not be the only one.* She was not. She is, in fact, currently late for band practice.
