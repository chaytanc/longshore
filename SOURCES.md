# SOURCES — The Reality Next Door

*A consolidated, deduplicated, annotated bibliography for the worldbuilding project **The Reality Next Door** (see `CANON.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `STRATEGY.md`).*

**What this is.** Every real citation and link harvested from the research briefs in `research/` and the cited sources in `threads/`, deduplicated by URL and grouped by theme. Each entry gives the source (author / title / outlet where known), the URL, and the brief(s) it grounds. Where two or more links pointed at the same underlying work they are kept as separate rows only when the URLs genuinely differ; identical URLs cited from multiple briefs are merged into one row that names all of them.

**How to read it.** This is a **living document**, compiled *from* the briefs, not a replacement for them — the argument, the steelmanned opponents, the disconfirming evidence, and the exact claims each source supports live in the briefs themselves. Follow the `grounds:` tag back to the brief for context. Because the world is built under the Anti-Echo-Chamber Protocol, this list deliberately includes sources that **disagree with the project's premises** (degrowth critics, prison-abolition skeptics, the "deathism" critique, greenwashing exposés, the iron law of oligarchy, memetics critiques, and more) — they are load-bearing, not decoration.

**Provenance of the sources themselves.** Quality is uneven *by design and by honesty*: peer-reviewed papers and primary standards sit next to news features, encyclopedia entries, advocacy orgs, and a few educational-survey pages. Several briefs flag their own weakest citations (self-reported company figures, association membership counts, survey sites); those caveats live in the briefs and are not re-litigated here.

**Scope.** ~526 unique sources, drawn from 18 research briefs and the `threads/` fiction (which mostly cites back into the briefs; the exception is the deafness / hair-cell-regeneration science introduced in `threads/the-band-girl.md`, filed under Care & health).

**A note on the theme buckets.** Several briefs are cross-cutting (`red-team`, `wildcards`, `scarcity-and-hard-tradeoffs`, `audience-model`, `landscape-2026`). Each source is filed under a single best-fit theme, but the `grounds:` tag always lists **every** brief it appears in, so nothing is lost by the grouping. Themes: Networks & the Weave · Movement & urbanism · Food & the sea · Care & health · Justice · Indigenous studies & sovereignty · Governance & economy · Culture & music · Death & grief · Ecology.
