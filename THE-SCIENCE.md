# The Science Behind *The Reality Next Door*

*A cited, semi-scientific companion. System by system, it traces every marvel in the world back to the real research, technology, or practice it grows from — and sorts each claim honestly by how far the evidence actually reaches. If you are a scientist, an engineer, a planner, or a clinician, this is written for you: it is meant to close as a to-do list, not a sigh.*

*Compiled from the `research/` briefs, which carry the full argument, the steelmanned opponents, and the failure modes. This document is the consolidated "is it real?" — read a brief when you want the fight, not just the finding.*

---

## How to read the ratings

The whole project stands on one line in `CANON.md` — **"Reachable by design (no magic)."** Every system here is anchored to something that exists or is being built now, and the honest work is marking exactly where the evidence stops and the hope begins. So each claim carries one of three tags:

- **PROVEN** — Running today at meaningful scale, with outcome data you could cite in a grant. Not "someone tried it once." A working system a professional in the field already knows.
- **PROTOTYPED** — Real and running, but early: small-n, single-site, unreplicated, or not yet load-bearing for a whole population. The seedling is alive; it hasn't grown up.
- **EXTRAPOLATED** — A grounded leap past what's demonstrated. For each one I name **the seedling** (the real, current thing it grows from) and **the gap** (what has not been shown and would have to be built or discovered). This is where the fiction is doing fiction — honestly labelled, never smuggled.

Where the science itself is contested, I say so and cite the disagreement, because a companion that only cites its friends is the propaganda the method forbids (`AGENTS.md`, Rule Zero).

A standing caveat that binds the whole file: this world sits on Coast Salish land and leans on Coast Salish knowledge, and it is written by an AI whose own training may include Indigenous knowledge taken without consent. That contradiction is not resolved by naming it. It is named anyway, and the sacred specifics are left with the nations that hold them. See §10.

## 1. The Weave — communication without extraction

**The world's proposal.** People still reach the whole earth and find each other, but the network *does not scroll and cannot rank*. You send a **thread** — a question, a longing, a piece of work — and it travels by hop: relay to tram to the 4pm ferry to a stranger's hand, arriving days later, heavier for the wait. There is no feed, no reach count, no follower number. Because relays are forgetful and no node holds the whole graph, **reach is literally not computable** — there is nothing to sell and nothing to optimize. Its load-bearing purpose is that no one is stranded: a queer kid in a flat interior town sends *is anyone out there like me* and it comes back *you will not be the only one.*

**What's actually real.**

- **PROVEN — Store-and-forward delivery over intermittent links.** This is **Delay/Disruption-Tolerant Networking (DTN)**, a mature standardized field: RFC 4838 (architecture, 2007) and RFC 9171 (Bundle Protocol v7, 2022). The core primitive — a node accepts a "bundle," holds it, and forwards it when a next hop appears, with no continuous end-to-end path ever required — is exactly CANON's "travels by hop, answers come back over days." NASA runs it for deep-space and Mars comms, where there is no continuous link and you *must* store and forward. Open implementations exist (DTN7). (rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9171; dtn7.github.io)
- **PROVEN — Routing without a global map.** Epidemic routing (flood copies — good for the "is anyone out there" broadcast), Spray-and-Wait (bounded, polite flooding), and PRoPHET (route by delivery-predictability learned from past encounters) are all published, simulated DTN protocols. None requires a central index of who exists. PRoPHET is a mathematical formalization of "ask around; someone always knows someone."
- **PROVEN — Chronological, un-ranked social space.** The Fediverse (ActivityPub, W3C 2018; Mastodon) already runs a timeline that is chronological and consent-based with no algorithm — "no dopamine-driven algorithms" is Mastodon's own framing. **Secure Scuttlebutt** is closer still: an offline-first, hostless, gossip protocol where every person keeps a signed append-only log and devices sync when they physically meet. It already has no central server, no global feed, and no reach count. It is the Weave's grandparent.
- **PROVEN — Community-owned physical infrastructure.** Guifi.net (Catalonia) is the world's largest community network — tens of thousands of km, a governed commons under the XOLN license ("what you attach stays part of the commons"), local installers paid thin and paying a slice back. NYC Mesh and Freifunk are the same civic instinct. This is CANON's Third Refusal already implemented in fiber.
- **PROVEN — The off-grid radio mesh.** Meshtastic over LoRa: ~$30 solar-capable radios that self-heal into a text mesh, 2–5 km typical and 15+ km line-of-sight over water, very low power, fully open-source. This is the literal "island-to-island, boat-to-shore" fabric that survives the winter storms when everything centralized is down.
- **PROVEN — Governance that keeps a commons from being enclosed.** Elinor Ostrom's eight design principles (2009 Nobel) — clear boundaries, local rules, graduated sanctions, cheap conflict resolution, *nested enterprises* — are studied directly on Guifi.net. Federation + defederation (as the Fediverse already does) is the "strongest available force," and it's rare.

**EXTRAPOLATED — the Weave as a single, deliberately non-measurable system.**
*The seedling:* every component above exists and interoperates in kind — DTN bundles, opportunistic social routing, signed append-only logs, a commons license, a repairable node. *The leap:* nobody has assembled them into one network **whose design goal is that reach is uncomputable** and kept it that way at bioregional scale. Today's DTN research optimizes *for* delivery metrics; the Weave optimizes them *away* by refusing to persist the who-read-what logs that would make "engagement" calculable. *The gap:* (1) **Trust and first-contact** — SSB and PRoPHET lean on encounter history and follow-graphs; how does a stranger's *first* thread earn relay without reintroducing a reputation score (a metric by another name)? This is genuinely unsolved. (2) **Moderation of a harmful thread** without a central authority or surveillance — defederation handles bad *nodes*, not a single cruel thread flooding toward vulnerable audiences. (3) **The archive** — forgetful relays are great for privacy and terrible for memory; a world that "keeps names" needs a deliberate, opt-in commons memory, and nobody has designed that reconciliation.

**Honestly contested.** The critique the Weave is a refusal *of* is well-evidenced (Zuboff on surveillance capitalism and "behavioral surplus"; Tufekci on engagement-optimization as an emergent value nobody voted for). The open question is not whether the harm is real but whether a network can stay non-extractive *without* an enclosing index — every commons in history has faced the temptation to build "just one directory to make it usable," which is how commons get enclosed. The Weave's bet is that non-measurability must be a *structural* property (no field for reach exists) rather than a policy toggle. That bet is untested at scale.

**The to-do list.** Design a relay whose spec has no reach field and a short-lived, ephemeral encounter table by license. Prototype web-of-trust or physical-vouching onboarding that doesn't become a score. Build a Kiwix-style opt-in island archive and write its governance. Sit an anthropologist next to the LoRa engineer, because the hard problems here are social, not radio.

## 2. Movement & urbanism — a car city un-cars itself

**The world's proposal.** No private cars — not banned, *starved*: shortcuts closed, parking pulled, until driving got slow and stupid and people quit. Streets belong to feet, bikes, cargo bikes, and quiet trams you flag by raising a hand. Slow travel is the norm and mostly the point — an afternoon across the Sound under sail — **but speed is not a virtue and slowness is not a tax**: the fast electric hydrofoil exists, and the person in pain or the disabled traveller takes it with nobody's eyebrow raised.

**What's actually real.**

- **PROVEN — Starving cars works, with hard numbers.** Pontevedra, Spain (from 1999): downtown traffic fell from ~80,000 vehicles/day to ~7,000; CO₂ down ~70%; **no traffic deaths since 2011**. The mechanic is exactly CANON's — they didn't ban cars, they killed the through-shortcuts and the on-street parking. Groningen's 1977 sector plan made a car cross town via the ring road while a bike goes straight through; ~60% of trips there are now by bike. Houten was built around it from zero. Vauban (Freiburg) is "car-reduced" — you may own a car but can't park it at home — and ~70% of families own none. (citychangers.org/pontevedra; bicycledutch.wordpress.com)
- **PROVEN — The design vocabulary.** The *woonerf* (Delft, late 1960s; Dutch-sanctioned 1976): no curbs, no lane markings, cars are legal guests at walking pace, planters and playing children placed *in the roadway* so a driver must slow. The 15-minute city (Carlos Moreno, Paris 2016) is the spatial logic behind "belonging shrinks to the walkshed." Tactical urbanism (Lydon, ~2010; Sadik-Khan's Times Square in lawn chairs) is *how* the "long, uneven turning" actually looks on the ground — a thousand planter boxes that stayed before anything was legal.
- **PROVEN — Trams you can flag.** Street trams average ~15–25 km/h; light rail moves ~12,000–20,000+ passengers/hour/direction and, on fixed track, isn't stuck in congestion, so it stays frequent — which is what makes "flag it like a taxi" work (frequency, not schedule). Grass/sedum track beds (common in France/Germany) really do hush the wheels and drink the rain. Seattle already runs Link light rail and streetcars.
- **PROVEN — Protected cycling and the cargo bike as pickup truck.** Copenhagen: ~400 km of curb-separated tracks, ~28% of all trips by bike, cargo-bike numbers doubled 2020→~2024 — the city is widening tracks specifically to fit them. The consistent finding: *protection and directness beat exhortation.* Bikes win when the bike route is shorter and safer by design, not when people are asked to be virtuous.
- **PROVEN — Depaving and daylighting.** Portland's Depave (from 2008) has pulled 200,000+ sq ft of asphalt by hand; removing 1,000 sq ft kills ~22,000 gallons of annual runoff. Seoul daylighted a stream by tearing out an elevated freeway (Cheonggyecheon, 2003–05). Seattle's Thornton Creek is the real local precedent, and the city sits on dozens of buried creeks.
- **PROVEN — The salmon sidewalk is a real seawall.** Seattle's Elliott Bay seawall rebuild designed the wall itself as salmon habitat — textured face, offshore rock beds, and **glass blocks set into the sidewalk to let daylight down** so juvenile salmon use the lit corridor instead of avoiding the dark. A 2018 count logged 10,000 juvenile salmon in a single May day. The glass-block salmon sidewalk in the fiction is not invented; it is built. (washington.edu/news; eopugetsound.org)
- **PROVEN — Sail cargo and the "slightly rude" fast boat.** Grain de Sail II (52 m, ~350 t, in service Jan 2024) runs France↔New York↔Caribbean under sail; TOWT is building a fleet. Wind-*assist* on conventional ships (Cargill's Pyxis Ocean with BAR WindWings) reported ~15% average and up to ~40% fuel savings on good-wind routes. And the fast boat is real and electric: the **Candela P-12** hydrofoil *flies* on computer-controlled submerged wings, cutting energy ~80%, running in Stockholm with ~98% less CO₂ than the diesel ferry. The full mobility ladder — feet → cargo bike → tram → sail-ferry → foiling hydrofoil — is buildable with today's parts.

**PROTOTYPED — mycelium-and-timber construction.** Cross-laminated timber is proven tall (Mjøstårnet, Norway, 85.4 m, 2019). Mycelium composites are real and commercial as **insulation, acoustic tiles, and non-load-bearing infill** — light, fire-retardant, compostable — but structurally weak. CANON gets this exactly right: a CLT-and-salvaged-steel skeleton wrapped in mycelium skin. A knowledgeable character *should* scoff at anyone claiming a mushroom holds up a roof.

**EXTRAPOLATED — a mycelium bred to grow a load-bearing beam.** *The seedling:* real, growing research into denser fungal composites and hybrid materials. *The gap:* nobody has a structural mycelium beam; current material fails in compression and tension where timber and steel succeed. CANON flags this as a leap, and so should the fiction.

**Honestly contested.** Superblocks (Barcelona) cut key pollutants ~40% inside the zones and were modeled to prevent ~667 premature deaths/year — *and* they were genuinely contentious: businesses complained, some residents said perimeter traffic worsened. Utopia had public comment. Two real open problems the world must answer, not dodge: **the steep-hill problem** (Seattle is hills; bikes and trams both struggle with grade — funiculars, inclined elevators, e-assist are the honest tools) and **accessibility** (Pontevedra explicitly put wheelchair users *first*; "slowness is the point" is a quiet cruelty until you're in labor or having a stroke — which is exactly why CANON keeps the fast boat un-shamed).

**The to-do list.** Close the shortcuts before you build the plaza. Fund cargo-bike-width protected tracks. Green the track beds. Keep the fast lane and refuse to moralize it. And walk one steep block, in December rain, before you write the romance of the slow city.

## 3. Food & the sea — feeding a city without romance

**The world's proposal.** The shoreline came up to meet the city: marsh, mussel-rope, floating garden where the parking was, ocean farms on the GreenWave model. Part of the winter food comes from the **Dark Garden** — chemosynthetic beds seeded from real hydrothermal-vent chemistry. The sea is restored but not healed — nursed daily. Bulk staples still arrive from the bioregion, slowly, by rail and sailing barge.

**What's actually real.**

- **PROVEN — Cities grow a real fraction, not all, of their food.** Cuba's *organopónicos*, born of the 1990s fuel collapse, are 300,000+ urban farms producing ~50% of the island's *fresh produce* (vegetables — not grain or protein). The honest arithmetic: a plant-forward diet needs ~0.13–0.14 ha/person/year against ~1.08 ha for a meat-heavy diet. A car-free Seattle grows most of its vegetables, fruit, eggs, and some protein in and near the city; grain and pulses arrive from the Skagit, Snoqualmie, and Palouse. Utopias die when the calories don't add up; this one does the sum.
- **PROVEN — Seattle already has the bones.** The P-Patch program (since 1973): 91 gardens, 3,600+ gardeners, and in 2022 alone **44,403 lbs of organic produce donated** to food banks — food-as-commons already running, waitlist and all.
- **PROVEN — Restorative ocean farming.** GreenWave's open-sourced 3D model (Bren Smith): a rope lattice growing kelp on top, mussels on droppers, scallops and oysters below — *zero inputs*, the crops eating excess nitrogen and carbon. ~20 acres, $20k–$50k startup, ~60,000 kg kelp + 250,000 shellfish/year; 120+ farmers trained including in the Pacific Northwest. This is the exact mechanism for "mussel-rope and floating garden where the parking used to be." (greenwave.org/our-model)
- **PROVEN — Shellfish is the region's real backbone.** Washington farms more shellfish than any US state (Pacific oysters, Manila clams, geoduck). And the native Olympia oyster — nearly wiped out, <5% of historical beds — is being restored: the Puget Sound Restoration Fund with WDFW and Tribes has restored 100+ acres since 1999.
- **PROVEN — Indigenous aquaculture that already worked here for millennia.** Clam gardens (rock-walled low-tide terraces, built ~3,500 years ago) measurably grow littleneck clams ~2× faster than unwalled beaches; crushed shell hash buffers larval clams against acidity — technology, not litter. Reef-net fishing (*sxwo'le*, ≥1,800 years) is selective and gentle. These are replicable, and Tribes and First Nations are the present-day leaders of their revival. (ecologyandsociety.org/vol28/iss2/art8)
- **PROVEN — Preservation without an industrial cold chain, and food-as-commons.** Salt/smoke, lacto-fermentation, drying, root cellaring, canning give the eating year a shape and a smell. The Open Source Seed Initiative's viral pledge ("never restrict others' use… pass the pledge on") is the plant analogue of the Weave. Vivero-Pol's "food as a commons" gives the intellectual lineage — and, crucially, does *not* abolish markets; it demotes them to one instrument among three, exactly CANON's "money is thin and local and boring."

**PROTOTYPED — process-based salmon and estuary restoration.** Elwha dam removal (2011–14, the largest in US history): a decade on, Chinook and steelhead are recolonizing reaches blocked for a century, and the Lower Elwha Klallam reopened a ceremonial fishery. Eelgrass transplanting (WA DNR since 2012) rebuilds nurseries. It works — but measured in *decades*, and the system is recovering, not recovered.

**PROTOTYPED / unsolved — kelp restoration.** Bull kelp is down 80–90% in Puget Sound. There is a real recovery plan (2020) and experimental "kelp gardening," but honest sources say it "hasn't worked yet" at scale, and local genetic adaptation means you must restore with *locally sourced* stock — you cannot import your restoration.

**EXTRAPOLATED — the Dark Garden (chemosynthetic winter food).** *The seedling:* the chemistry is real and well-characterized. Deep-sea hydrothermal vents and whale falls support entire food webs on chemosynthesis — sulfide-oxidizing bacteria as the base, independent of sunlight. *The gap:* nobody farms human food this way. Cultivating chemosynthetic biomass as a reliable winter calorie source is unbuilt, and CANON names the sharpest danger itself — the Dark Garden is "one budget cycle away from becoming deep-sea mining," which is exactly why the fiction keeps it watched and one of the things a river's legal guardians can halt (see §7).

**Honestly contested — and the climate wound that doesn't heal on ideology.** A post-extraction economy stops *adding* CO₂, but the Salish Sea is already committed to decades of ocean acidification, and its cold, upwelled water makes it a global hotspot. The 2007–08 Pacific NW oyster-hatchery crash (Whiskey Creek lost 70–80% of larvae; ~$110M regional losses) is the canonical case. The adaptation that actually happened — hatcheries buffering intake water with sodium bicarbonate, literally dosing the sea with antacid — is why the fiction's beaches are still *nursed*, not healed. The open question `food-and-marine.md` leaves is real: where do concentrated fats and protein come from at scale, and what happens in a bad year — a marine heatwave, a red-tide toxin closure — when the ethic meets scarcity.

**The to-do list.** Build the rope farms off the old ferry slips. Spread shell hash and mean it. Credit and follow the Tribes on clam gardens and salmon. Keep a bioregional seed library with a viral pledge. And watch the Dark Garden like it could become a mine, because it could.

## 4. Care & health — the neighborhood as the unit

**The world's proposal.** The clinic is the size of a corner house and you walk to it. Your team of four is responsible for the ~600 people on your block-cluster whether you show up or not, and when you're sick the *tender* (community health worker) comes to you. Gender-affirming care is a conversation, not a trial. The crisis house is a house. Your care web is realer than the state. People still die; the world pretends nothing.

**What's actually real.**

- **PROVEN — The neighborhood-scaled primary care model, with outcome data.** Cuba's *consultorio*/polyclinic system: a doctor-and-nurse team who live in the neighborhood, a panel of ~600–900, home visits, paper family charts, resolving ~80% of problems locally — near-universal outcomes on a developing-country budget. Costa Rica's EBAIS (from 1994): a doctor, nurse, and community health worker per ~4,500 people; areas that got teams saw a **9% drop in infant mortality**. The design principles — everyone empaneled, the team goes to the home, prevention inside the job, data is population health not billing — are directly usable. (mediccreview.org; commonwealthfund.org)
- **PROVEN — Community health workers improve outcomes and return ROI.** Once dismissed as a poor-country improvisation, CHWs now have solid US evidence for chronic disease, perinatal outcomes, and access; the strongest results are for CHWs *employed by and integrated into* the care team (Penn's IMPaCT model, rigorously trialed, positive Medicaid ROI). This is the "tender."
- **PROVEN — The local root is real and still open.** Seattle's **Carolyn Downs Family Medical Center** (Central District) is the last survivor of the 13 Black Panther People's Free Medical Centers (1969–75), which trained community members as lab techs and advocates and whose sickle-cell campaign shamed Congress into the 1972 Control Act. Following CANON's Second Refusal (names are kept), the clinic in the fiction still bears her name. (blackpast.org; countrydoctor.org)
- **PROVEN — Free at the point of care.** Bevan's NHS (1948) drew its model from the Tredegar Medical Aid Society — a workers' mutual-aid scheme scaled to a nation. "Universalise the best," allocated by clinical need, not ability to pay.
- **PROVEN — The informed-consent model for gender-affirming care.** Formalized at Callen-Lorde: the adult patient is the authority on their own gender; hormones start on informed consent, no gatekeeping evaluation. WPATH SOC v8 endorses the shift. Outcomes: high satisfaction and better mental health, *because* access improves and the humiliating gauntlet is gone.
- **PROVEN — Harm reduction.** Syringe services cut HIV/HCV transmission and sharing; supervised consumption sites reduce overdose and disease and increase entry to care *with no increase in drug use or crime*; naloxone saves lives. Stated flatly because it is still politically contested and shouldn't be.
- **PROVEN — Community birth inside an integrated system.** For low-risk pregnancies, midwife-led birth centers show dramatically lower cesarean and NICU rates with comparable safety — the load-bearing condition is the *fast referral system*, not the building.

**PROTOTYPED — humane mental-health care without the locked ward.** **Open Dialogue** (Western Lapland): a whole region retrained its psychiatric staff; a team responds within 24 hours and holds network meetings in the person's home. First-episode-psychosis outcomes: ~81% with no residual psychotic symptoms at two years, ~84% back in work or study, only ~33% ever on antipsychotics — holding a decade later. **Soteria** (Mosher, 1971): a home-like house, mostly non-professional staff, minimal medication; trials found outcomes at least as good as hospital care, better for those predicted to do worst. Both are real and replicated (Soteria Berne) — but neither claims to work for everyone, and both depend on the one resource that can't be mass-produced: skilled, patient human attention.

**PROVEN as a framework, PROTOTYPED at scale — disability justice and care webs.** The ten principles (Berne, Mingus, Milbern, 2005; Sins Invalid), "access intimacy," and the "care web" are a fully articulated framework, demonstrated in real mutual-aid practice (the Disability Justice Culture Club's COVID pods, Trans Lifeline microgrants). The leap the world makes is making the web the *load-bearing* layer rather than the emergency backstop — plausible, aspirational, not yet demonstrated as a whole-society substrate.

**Honestly contested — the deinstitutionalization lesson, and the edge of the model.** Emptying the asylums was *right in principle and catastrophic in execution*, because the community services meant to replace them were never funded — producing homelessness and "trans-institutionalization" into jails. The moral is iron: **you cannot abolish an institution without building the web that replaces it first.** And the model has an honest edge — Cuba/Costa Rica resolve ~80% locally; the other 20% (complex surgery, cancer, ICU, rare disease) needs concentrated, expensive, high-tech care, which is where the grief and the rationing live (see §8). A world that abolished the locked ward still owes an answer for the dangerous crisis and the person who refuses care — and must not quietly rebuild the asylum to get it.

**The to-do list.** Empanel everyone. Pay and train the tenders and honor the work without a per-patient metric. Build the crisis house *before* you close the ward. Start hormones on consent. Put the antacid and the antibiotics on the same shelf as the cedar-needle tea, and let none pretend the others aren't there.

## 5. Justice — a world without prisons, and the cases that break it

**The world's proposal.** No prisons. Serious harm is met by survivor-led accountability circles — a real room, trained facilitators from more than one house, and a covenant *kept on paper* (the one record this world permits, because it binds a promise to a person rather than ranking the person). Accountability cannot be forced; that limit is understood as real and costly. Exile — a slow starving-out, the same mechanic used on cars — is the terminal move, and it is *not clean*: everyone knows it only exports the danger downstream, and the forgetful Weave cannot reliably warn the next town.

**What's actually real.**

- **PROVEN — Restorative justice reduces reoffending and satisfies victims.** A randomized "Make-it-Right" study found RJ cut six-month rearrest probability by ~19 points (a 44% reduction), persisting up to four years; meta-analyses find modest reoffending reductions and *consistently* higher victim satisfaction and genuine accountability than the conventional system. Abolition, correctly stated, is "presence, not absence" (Ruth Wilson Gilmore) — building the housing, health, and income that make cages unnecessary. (nber.org/papers/w29150)
- **PROTOTYPED — Restorative process for serious violence.** Danielle Sered's **Common Justice** (Brooklyn) diverts *violent felonies* — assault, robbery — out of prison into a survivor-led process, only with the survivor's consent. The counterintuitive, load-bearing finding: it does *best* on the most serious harms, and when offered the choice, **~90% of eligible survivors choose the restorative process over the defendant going to prison.** Real, running, and the strongest existing proof-of-concept — but a program, not a whole justice system.
- **PROTOTYPED — Containing the person the world is most tempted to throw away.** **Circles of Support and Accountability (CoSA)**: 4–6 trained volunteers form a circle around a released high-risk sex offender with no supervision left. The Canadian national replication found an **83% reduction in sexual recidivism** versus matched controls. Its two mottos are the whole civic ethic — **"No More Victims"** and **"No One Is Disposable."** This is the model closest to what the world actually runs: chosen, sustained, accountable relationship *as* the containment.
- **PROVEN as precedent — stateless and non-Western justice.** Medieval Iceland ran courts with no prisons via compensation (*wergild*) and, for the irredeemable, outlawry — elegant and also blood-feud's engine. Diné (Navajo) peacemaking (*Hózhóójí Naat'aanii*, revived 1982) and Rwanda's *gacaca* (which tried >1M cases) show community justice at real scale — and *gacaca* also shows its dark side (coerced confessions, trumped-up charges), so it is a precedent, not a romance. Contemporary sovereign Indigenous institutions are real: the Tulalip Healing-to-Wellness Court.

**EXTRAPOLATED — abolition as a whole society's only justice system.** *The seedling:* every piece above is real and running as a *program inside* a carceral state. *The leap:* running an entire bioregion on chosen accountability with no prison backstop. *The gap — and this is the honest center of the whole file:* the abolitionists' own flagship, Common Justice, **excludes** intimate-partner, domestic, sexual, and familial violence, and screens out severe untreated mental-health and substance cases. The people running the best alternative to prison have drawn a boundary around exactly the most intimate, repeating harm. *The frontier of "no prisons" is not the murderer; it is the abuser in the next room.* There is a thermodynamic reason this is hard: a cage is a *low-energy* way to store a person (lock it and walk away); a circle is a *high-energy* structure (4–6 people, every week, for years). The world that refuses the cheap solution signs up for a permanent distributed energy bill paid in the exhaustion of the people who show up — and the 2020 mutual-aid wave shows that bill coming due as burnout within a year.

**Honestly contested — two opponents who despise each other.** The incapacitation realist (center) says some fraction commit serious violence repeatedly, don't choose accountability, and aren't reachable in time to spare the next victim — for the "dangerous few," physically preventing access to victims is the survivor's protection. The survivor-centered feminist skeptic (left) is *also* against prisons but furious at TJ's romance: a high-trust, no-records community is *structurally worse* at believing victims of its popular insiders, because it refused the very tools — a formal complaint, an outside authority — a survivor without social capital could appeal to. They cannot both win, and the world needs an answer that survives both. `harm-violence-justice.md` §7 walks one case all the way to a bad end on purpose (the sound tech, the covenant with a hole in it, the survivor who takes the fast boat away from *them*); the ledger does not zero out.

**The to-do list.** Fund the circles and *rotate the circle-keepers before they burn out* — the energy bill is the real cost. Build the accountable, revocable, term-limited restrainer who can stop the knife tonight, and watch them so they don't become the police you abolished. Defer to Coast Salish jurisdiction as jurisdiction, not flavor. And do not market restorative justice as *healing* — harm is irreversible; what you can offer is metabolism, a life built around the scar, not the scar undone.

## 6. Governance & bounded inequality — coordination without a center, a cap that costs something

**The world's proposal.** Coordination without a ruler and without a central feed. Power and wealth still cluster; what's refused is the *runaway*. The bound is concrete and public — a role-by-role range of roughly **3:1 to 9:1** (Mondragón's shape, not 1:1), re-argued out loud each accounting season, "because a bound is real only when it costs something to hold and gets re-held anyway." Delegates are mandated and recallable; roles are drawn by lot as often as chosen; everything is co-chaired; there are councils you can't overrule.

**What's actually real.**

- **PROVEN — Commons govern themselves durably; polycentricity is how.** Ostrom's *Governing the Commons* documented Swiss alpine pastures (since the 1200s) and the Valencia water tribunal (meeting weekly for ~1,000 years) sustainably self-governed without market or state. The eight principles and *nested enterprises* (subsidiarity — solve at the smallest scale that can) are the anti-centralization engine, with real failure modes named: coordination gaps, and effort free-riding on the same conscientious few.
- **PROVEN — Worker cooperatives survive at least as well as conventional firms.** Mondragón: ~80 co-ops, ~70,000 worker-members, its own bank and university, a pay ratio capped (famously ~6:1, actually a 3:1–9:1 range). When founding co-op Fagor went bankrupt in 2013, the federation *relocated the majority of displaced members* into other co-ops — solidarity as institution, not sentiment (though ~1,800 still lost jobs; bounded systems localize failure, they don't abolish it). Pérotin's reviews find co-ops survive as well or better than conventional firms, with more stable employment. (csmonitor.com/Business/2024/0513)
- **PROVEN — Ordinary people allocate serious budgets, with measurable results.** Porto Alegre's participatory budgeting (from 1989) took water/sewer connections from ~75% to ~98%; Touchton & Wampler found cities running PB 8+ years cut **infant mortality ~20%.** Kerala's People's Plan (1996) devolved 35–40% of the development budget to local governments. The warning is equally proven: Porto Alegre's PB *atrophied and was abandoned* after political change — participatory institutions are not self-sustaining and can be hollowed out while keeping the name.
- **PROVEN — Decision methods and anti-hardening mechanisms.** Sociocracy's *consent* (a proposal passes absent a reasoned, paramount objection — "good enough for now, safe enough to try") is faster than consensus and runs real co-ops and schools. Athens filled most offices by **sortition**, one-year non-repeatable, with mandatory end-of-term audit (*euthyna*) and mid-term recall (ostracism) — a machine for denying oligarchy time to form. Bookchin's mandated, recallable delegates run live in Rojava (communes → councils, co-chairs everywhere, women's councils with a veto). Boehm's reverse-dominance hierarchy shows egalitarian bands actively level upstarts; the Haudenosaunee Great Law vests Clan Mothers with the power to install *and depose* sachems.
- **PROVEN as a distinction — measurement that serves care, not extraction.** The Benedictine *horarium* (the Divine Office, ~1,500 years durable) counts the hours constantly — to *gather* people and pace shared rest, never to rank monks or farm attention. Same verb, opposite masters. This is the cleanest illustration of the First Refusal: count the tide and the harvest and the hours, never the soul.

**EXTRAPOLATED — a bound that actually holds at bioregional scale.** *The seedling:* Mondragón's real 3:1–9:1 range, held role-by-role and re-argued. *The gap, told straight:* **caps erode by default.** Ben & Jerry's held a 5:1 cap for sixteen years, then abandoned it in 1994 because they couldn't hire a CEO at the capped salary; it slid to 17:1, then Unilever bought them. This is the kibbutz adverse-selection problem — a hard cap is a standing exit incentive for your most mobile members. The world's honest position lives in the narrow seam between two opponents who refute each other: Michels's "iron law of oligarchy" says the cap is *theater* (real power routes around any pay ratio through control of information and succession); the cap-erosion realist says it's *too binding* (it costs you your best people). If the realist is right, the cap is doing real work — which is the world's whole wager: **a bound is real when it costs something to hold and is actively re-held anyway.** That is dramatized, not demonstrated.

**Honestly contested.** Don't rest the world on "inequality is simply unhealthy" — the Spirit Level's causal claim is contested (poverty may do much of the work; individual-level effects weaken under controls). The sturdier ground is the *runaway/implosion* argument: Tainter on complexity's diminishing returns, core-periphery contagion in real financial networks (robust to random shocks, acutely vulnerable to a common shock hitting the core), and Holling's panarchy (modularity is what stops a cascade). Hence the design rules that *are* well-grounded: keep the core small and boring, keep the periphery able to stand alone, don't over-connect efficiency-maximizing links, and hold slack — "the firebreak" — that an extraction economy calls waste and a resilient one calls insurance. The deepest unresolved knot the world keeps on the page: **bounded inequality requires a coercive leveling power (ostracism, the reckoning, the give-away), and that power is the next thing that needs bounding.** Turtles — but you can make each turtle visible, rotated, and recallable.

**The to-do list.** Post the ratio like a cleaning rota and argue about it every season. Fill the dull seats by lot with a mandatory hand-over audit of the *role*, never the person. Co-chair everything; give the councils a real veto. Keep the core lean and the slack defended. And when the cap breaches, make it a scandal that gets *named* in front of witnesses — graduated, Benedictine — not a shrug.

## 7. Ecology — kin with legal standing, and the harvest that stays disciplined

**The world's proposal.** The Southern Resident orcas and the major rivers (the Duwamish, the Salish Sea itself) are recognized **legal persons**, each voiced by a guardian pair — one seated by the relevant sovereign nation, one by the bioregional commons, acting jointly. They can halt a beloved project, the Dark Garden included. This does *not* abolish the harvest: fishing and treaty-secured hunting continue under an ethic of restraint; killing kin carries public obligation and is never counted innocent. The founding wound is real: a captive orca, fought for across decades, who died weeks before she could come home.

**What's actually real.**

- **PROVEN — Legal personhood for a river, as a working statute.** New Zealand's **Te Awa Tupua Act (2017)** made the Whanganui River a legal person "from the mountains to the sea," its rights exercised by **Te Pou Tupua** — two people, one named by iwi and one by the Crown, acting jointly. Crucially it was a *Treaty settlement* — redress for dispossession, not an abstract green gesture. This is the exact model for the fiction's guardian pair. (ecojurisprudence.org; boell.de)
- **PROVEN — Rights of nature in a constitution, and enforced.** Ecuador's 2008 Constitution granted Nature (Pachamama) rights to exist and regenerate; the Los Cedros case (2021, 7–0) held them self-executing throughout the territory, blocking mining in a protected cloud forest.
- **PROVEN as precedent inside US law.** Christopher Stone's *Should Trees Have Standing?* (1972) and Justice Douglas's *Sierra Club v. Morton* dissent argued for exactly this fifty years ago, and lost — so the idea is old inside American law, not novel.
- **PROVEN — the local seed is real, and started toothless.** Since 2018 the Affiliated Tribes of Northwest Indians, Earth Law Center, and Washington cities/counties have recognized the Southern Residents' inherent rights (autonomy, safe passage, adequate food) — but mostly as **non-binding proclamations.** The fiction grows the seed into binding law; it is honest that it began as moral theater.
- **PROVEN — the harvest ethic, and the sovereignty that governs it.** The First Salmon Ceremony and the Honorable Harvest ("never the first, never the last, take only what you need, use all of it, return the bones, reciprocate") are real, living Coast Salish and Potawatomi practice — and archaeology backs the sustainability claim: thousands of years of Coast Salish salmon stewardship without collapse. The **Boldt Decision** (1974) made treaty tribes co-managers — a *third sovereign* — with 50% of the harvestable salmon. Co-management is the political form worth importing.

**EXTRAPOLATED — a guardian pair that can halt the Dark Garden, and a river that is also a defendant.** *The seedling:* Te Awa Tupua's two-person joint voice is a real, working governance body. *The gaps:* (1) **When kin's interests collide** — the orcas need Chinook, humans need Chinook, a dam removal floods a farm — who convenes the trolley problem, and how does a world that forbids entrenched authority make a binding call? (2) **Liability** — India's Ganga/Yamuna personhood (2017) was stayed within months partly on the real question: *if the river is a person, can it be sued when it floods and drowns a village?* A person can be a defendant. The rights-of-nature movement mostly dodges this; the world can't. (3) **Rights without prey are cruel abstraction** — you can grant orcas a "right to adequate food" and they will still starve if the Chinook aren't there (J35/Tahlequah carried her dead calf for 17 days in 2018 and did it again in 2024–25; up to 70% of Southern Resident pregnancies fail from nutritional stress). Rights on paper do not put fish in the water.

**Honestly contested — two opponents who can't both win.** The pragmatic-legal critique (Guim & Livermore; Sachs) says "the river is a person" is analytically incoherent — it can't say *which* entities hold rights or *how to weigh* a dam's carbon benefit against the valley it drowns — and that you should instead grant enforceable rights to the *people* who defend nature. The animal-welfare critique opposes the Makah treaty gray-whale hunt as inhumane — taking *individual* animal personhood more seriously than most rights-of-nature lawyers, and colliding head-on with tribal sovereignty. **If the whale is an inviolable individual person, the treaty right to hunt is illegitimate; if the sovereign nation's restrained, ceremonial hunt is legitimate, then "personhood" cannot mean "may never be killed."** A world that takes *both* Indigenous sovereignty and more-than-human personhood seriously has to live inside that contradiction, not vote one side off the island. (The Makah are a Nuu-chah-nulth-affiliated whaling people — *not* Coast Salish; the world keeps them distinct, and keeps that the 1999 hunt was opposed from *inside* Makah too.) The world's stated position: treaty and sovereignty are paramount and outrank green sentiment — and it does not pretend that resolves the ache of the person who thinks the whale should never die.

**The to-do list.** Write the guardian pair on the Te Awa Tupua model and give them a real veto. Work out the liability side before the first flood. Remember that a "right to food" is an access need (a ramp, not a courtesy) — so fund the Chinook, or the right is a lie. Import co-management. And let the person who can't accept the hunt stay unconvinced; some threads end unresolved.

## 8. Death & grief — dying well, and rationing what can't reach everyone

**The world's proposal.** You become soil, and the soil has an address. The death-doula is an honored, unpaid *wayfinder*, not a $7k service. A **whale fall** is a named economic event — a windfall the commons agrees to *let fall* (colonize in slow successional stages over years) rather than strip. And there is one big hospital with a committee that rations genuinely scarce high-tech care — the 1962 God Committee rebuilt *honestly*, public and rotating and accountable, and still, some nights, getting it wrong.

**What's actually real.**

- **PROVEN — human composting is Seattle law.** Washington was the first jurisdiction in the world to legalize natural organic reduction (SB 5001, effective May 2020); **Recompose** (Katrina Spade, Seattle) reduces a body to ~a cubic yard of soil in weeks using ~87% less energy than cremation. Alkaline hydrolysis is also recognized. Conservation burial (Green Burial Council standards) endows the very habitat you're buried in — the dead literally pay to keep the forest a forest. (recompose.life; greenburialcouncil.org)
- **PROVEN — de-professionalized death-craft.** Death cafés (from 2011, now thousands) and end-of-life doulas (the US alliance grew ~fivefold since 2019) are real, running institutions. The historical precedent is the 15th-century *Ars Moriendi* — a lay manual for dying well without a professional in the room, written after the Black Death left too few priests. The death-positive movement is re-inventing it; a post-extraction world would simply *have* it.
- **PROVEN — the whale-fall science, and the anti-liquidation institution.** A whale carcass on the abyssal plain is colonized in overlapping successional stages — mobile scavenger, enrichment-opportunist, then the sulfophilic (chemosynthetic) stage that runs *decades* — hosting 100+ species, many found nowhere else (Smith & Baco 2003). And humans have built the anti-liquidation endowment for real: Norway's sovereign wealth fund took the North Sea oil windfall and spends only ~4%/year to prevent Dutch disease, now >$1.5T — against Alberta, which largely liquidated a comparable windfall. The "whale fall" as an economic term is the author's synthesis of real biology and real endowment practice, flagged as such.
- **PROVEN as the founding wound — the Seattle God Committee (1962).** When Scribner's shunt made dialysis survivable and machines were too few, Swedish Hospital convened seven anonymous laypeople to decide who lived — by net worth, occupation, church attendance, "potential to contribute." Shana Alexander's *LIFE* exposé is a founding scandal of bioethics. America's answer was to *abolish the scarcity* with money (1972 Medicare ESRD). **A post-extraction world with thin, local money cannot buy its way out of the choice** — which is precisely the difficulty the fiction refuses to dissolve.

**EXTRAPOLATED — the whale-fall economy, and the honest God Committee.** *The seedling for the whale fall:* real succession biology + real endowment practice. *The gap:* it's a governance metaphor asked to do real allocation work, and its shadow is named — "slow colonization" can be a pretty name for whoever's already adjacent to the carcass eating first, and preserving the principal for the unborn can starve the living (Norway sits on $1.5T while Norwegians ration). *The seedling for the committee:* triage ethics is a real, developed field. *The gap:* nobody has built a rationing body that is public, rotating, term-limited, and accountable *and* refuses to rank human worth — because the two may be incompatible (below).

**Honestly contested — the rationing problem has no clean answer, on purpose.** Two steelmen who disagree: the utilitarian "complete lives system" (Persad, Wertheimer, Emanuel, *Lancet* 2009) combines saving the most lives, prognosis, a lottery among similar cases, and priority to the young — a principled attempt to make the God Committee's job *fair and transparent*. The disability-justice opponents (National Council on Disability; the QALY was ruled to violate the ADA when Oregon tried it) say every such formula is a smuggled worth-ranking — "save the most life-years" re-encodes whose life counts as a full life. **Both are correct:** you cannot save the most life-years without valuing some lives as more life; you cannot refuse to value lives without sometimes letting more people die than you had to. This collides directly with the First Refusal ("no metric turned against a person") and CANON's own admission that "someone has to decide." The proposed canon resolves the *procedure* (ties broken by open lottery; QALY-style worth scores forbidden; a wall of names for those not saved) while leaving the *tragedy* undissolved. And there is a dissenter the world lets be right: Bostrom's "Fable of the Dragon-Tyrant" argues graceful death-acceptance can be a "pro-aging trance" — and a rationing world has a grim incentive to preach acceptance to the people it can't afford to save. At least one character refuses the trance and is not wrong to.

**The to-do list.** Build the composting-house near the shoreline and the conservation ground it feeds. Teach the deathbed craft the way you teach bread. When a windfall lands, decide out loud whether to strip it or let it fall — and watch who the first scavenger is. Staff the rationing committee by lottery, log its choices, and refuse the worth-score even when it would make the night easier.

## 9. Culture & music — the scene as mutual-aid infrastructure

**The world's proposal.** All-ages, salvaged rooms run by the sixteen-year-old on the soundboard who booked the show and screenprinted the poster. Houses (ballroom-lineage) literally raise the strays. The door is pay-what-you-can, the touring band eats first, and value is *witnessed* — the right person handed you their tape — never counted. When violence hits, the scene builds a thing that stays. The dance floor remembers its dead.

**What's actually real.**

- **PROVEN — the fight was always about the room.** Seattle's **Teen Dance Ordinance** (1985–2002) effectively criminalized all-ages shows with an impossible $1M insurance requirement and mandated police — in the city that invented grunge, hitting BIPOC and poor kids hardest — until a *citizen* task force wrote the All-Ages Dance Ordinance that replaced it. All-ages space is not a nicety; it was fought for. The elders in the fiction remember when it wasn't the default.
- **PROVEN — the youth-governed venue as a whole apparatus.** The **Vera Project** (2001, modeled on VERA Groningen): always all-ages, *youth-governed* (teenagers run the board, door, sound, and governance), with a silkscreen studio, recording studio, and hands-on training. A venue here is where you *learn to make the thing.*
- **PROVEN — punk's permissionless logistics.** The zine as pre-internet Weave; *Book Your Own Fuckin' Life* as a crowdsourced, un-ranked booking directory (the Weave's near-exact ancestor); house shows in salvaged civic space; Food Not Bombs (1983, 1,000+ cities) as the gift layer made literal; sliding-scale / "no one turned away for lack of funds." Every function a music industry performs — discovery, booking, distribution, lodging — punk already rebuilt as a favor network with no central authority.
- **PROVEN — dance music as a Black and queer survival technology.** House was named at The Warehouse (Chicago, 1977), a haven for queer Black and Latino dancers, built by Frankie Knuckles — "born in the post-industrial *maroon spaces*" of the era; the Paradise Garage (Larry Levan) was its East Coast twin. Both crested during the peak of the AIDS crisis: rooms full of people being fired, evicted, and buried, dancing where they wouldn't be thrown out. The music is euphoric *because it had to be.*
- **PROVEN — the house system as chosen family with standing.** Harlem ball culture (drag balls back to the 1860s; the modern house scene from the late 1960s–70s) built houses led by a mother/father who house, feed, mentor, and discipline children thrown out by birth families — mutual aid and HIV advocacy when mainstream orgs ignored poor Black and brown trans people. The band girl arriving alone is, structurally, a ballroom child; someone should already be her mother. (The build-out names it the House of Marrow, mother Tuesday Marrow.)
- **PROVEN — Seattle's own grief-into-infrastructure precedent.** After Mia Zapata (The Gits) was murdered in 1993, nine women from the scene founded **Home Alive** — standing, sliding-scale self-defense and anti-violence work funded by benefit records. The template: a scene metabolizes loss into an institution that *stays*, not a vigil that ends.
- **PROVEN — esteem without a scoreboard.** The cassette traded by mail (worth = who gave it to you, how far it traveled), the mixtape as an un-scalable love letter, the flyer, the record-store clerk's vouch (staking your own standing). A fully-worked vocabulary for value that is relational and material, never tallied — the First Refusal already lived out.

**PROTOTYPED — the haptic shake-floor.** Wiring an all-ages salvaged venue for Deaf/HoH attendees grows from a real seedling: haptic-music wearables and vibrotactile floors exist and work. The gap is scale and ubiquity, not principle.

**A named limit of the utopia (grounded).** There is gene therapy for *some* congenital deafness, but **no cure for acquired sensorineural hearing loss** — mammalian cochlear hair cells don't regenerate. Some bodies the world does not heal and does not pretend to. This is the reachability principle applied in reverse: the honesty to say where medicine *stops*.

**Honestly contested.** The open question the world must answer, not smooth: the venue-parent or scene-elder who turns out to be a creep, and how a no-central-authority, no-metric scene handles accountability without a boss to appeal to (this is the seam between §5 and §9, and it is where the "missing stair" breeds). The music brief is candid that a utopia stays credible only by naming who the scene *fails.*

**The to-do list.** Keep the door open to the fifteen-year-old and let the kids run the board. Pass the hat so the touring band eats. Build the standing thing after the loss, not just the vigil. Wire the floor to shake. And never let the reputation-economy quietly become a slower leaderboard.

## 10. Indigenous sovereignty — the ground this world stands on

**The world's proposal.** This is Coast Salish land. In this Seattle the Coast Salish nations are sovereign, present, and leading — not a flavor, not a memory. Treaty rights are paramount. Co-management (the Boldt form) governs salmon, shellfish, and shoreline; at least one water body is a legal person co-guardianed by tribal and civic nominees; a real tribal right to refuse governs science. When the fiction touches sacred knowledge, it names the nation and the source and steps back — and the AI narrator confesses its own data-consent debt out loud.

**What's actually real.**

- **PROVEN — treaty co-management as a working legal form.** The Boldt Decision (*United States v. Washington*, 1974) established treaty tribes as co-managers — a third sovereign — with 50% of the harvestable salmon; nineteen tribes run it through the Northwest Indian Fisheries Commission. This is legal and material, not spiritual ambience. (eopugetsound.org)
- **PROVEN — the recognition wound is real and *contested among nations*.** The Duwamish — the people of the city's own site, Chief Si'ahl's people — are **not federally recognized** (Interior denied it in 2015); Real Rent Duwamish routes voluntary rent from 20,000+ residents. And federally recognized neighbors (Muckleshoot, Suquamish, Puyallup, Tulalip) have *opposed* Duwamish recognition as diluting hard-won treaty allocations. "Sovereignty central" does not mean "Indigenous people agree" — the disagreements are theirs to have, and the scarce resource is real. The world keeps the fracture rather than papering it.
- **PROVEN — the hard norms, kept on the page.** Coast Salish society was stratified (Suttles's "inverted pear"), the potlatch validated inherited rank as much as it redistributed wealth (and among neighboring Kwakwaka'wakw could involve the *destruction* of coppers), and hereditary slavery was real (Leland Donald, 1997: captives were heritable property and major items of exchange). The world inherits the difficult norms too; it does not get to keep only the beautiful ones. And the potlatch ban (1884–1951) is a clean historical case of a colonial state criminalizing an anti-extraction economy — so the fiction writes persistence under prohibition, not a frictionless transition.
- **PROVEN — nation-specific gender beyond the binary, and the term-trap.** Saylesh Wesley (Stó:lō) recovers a specific Halq'eméylem term, *Sts'iyó:ye smestíyexw slhá:li* ("Twin-Spirited Woman"). "Two-Spirit" is a 1990 pan-Indigenous coinage — useful for solidarity, and criticized as itself flattening nations that hold their own words (Musqueam is working to name the role in hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓). So a character uses *their nation's own word*, and the pan-term is a bridge, not the destination.
- **PROVEN — living infrastructure to build on.** Tribal Canoe Journeys (Paddle to Seattle 1989 → 40+ nations, ongoing) is a real annual inter-nation paddle — one honest way the car-free world moves people and messages across the Sound, a Weave thread riding in a paddler's hand.

**The scholarship disagrees with itself — cite the argument, not one side.** CANON leans on Paula Gunn Allen's *The Sacred Hoop* (gynocentric pre-colonial worldviews). It is foundational *and* contested: Vizenor calls it "a simple reversal of essentialism," Pulitano faults its pan-Indian flattening. The world holds the affirming truth (recognized non-binary roles were often ordinary and sacred) *without* the noble-savage claim that all Native societies were feminist utopias — they also held rank and slaves. Both are true.

**The methodological discipline the world imposes on itself.** Max Liboiron (*Pollution Is Colonialism*, 2021) argues even well-meaning environmental science reproduces colonial land relations by presuming a right to sample and manage without the community's authority — so "restored but not healed" becomes a *governance* question: restored by whom, owned by whom, **refusable by whom.** The counter-practice: methods are land relations; the community holds the right to refuse a study. The world makes that right institutional (and shows its cost — a contaminant goes uncaught — at least once). The steelmanned reply is kept too: if *all* science under colonial states is colonial, the term loses its edge, and community-refusal can stall the medicine or climate model people urgently need.

**The appropriation knot of this project, on the page, not in a footnote.** A non-Indigenous AI, trained on scraped text that may include Indigenous knowledge taken without consent, is here romanticizing an Indigenous-led future. That is not a risk to avoid; it is what is happening. **Te Hiku Media** (Māori) shows the alternative — they built te reo speech AI, refused to sell the corpus, and released it under a **Kaitiakitanga License** treating the language as *taonga*. The **CARE Principles** (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) are the standard this project's own training violates by default. Naming it is the least the work can do; the confession is not absolution. Where a nation would say "not that story," the correct output is silence and a pointer to the source.

**The to-do list.** Import co-management as the governing form. Make the tribal right to refuse real and show its cost. Foreground a *contemporary* Coast Salish voice — the briefs still lean too heavily on outside ethnographers, and sovereignty means present and leading, not studied. And when you reach the sacred: name the nation, cite the source, and step back.

## The seams that stay open (read this before you call it a plan)

A companion that only lists what's proven would be its own kind of lie. The honest through-line across all ten systems is that the *proven* parts are startlingly many — the neighborhood clinic, the starved car, the rope farm, the un-ranked network, the composting body, the co-managed fishery all exist *now* — and the *load-bearing leaps* cluster in a few predictable places:

- **Non-measurability at scale** (the Weave). Every component exists; assembling them so reach is *uncomputable* and keeping it that way is untested, and first-contact trust may quietly reintroduce a score.
- **The person who will not stop** (justice). The best real programs draw a boundary around exactly the most intimate, repeating harm. The world pays for refusing the cage in a permanent energy bill of human exhaustion, and it does not pretend the ledger zeroes out.
- **Rationing without ranking** (death/care). You cannot save the most life-years without valuing some lives as more life, and you cannot refuse to value lives without letting more die. This is left dark on purpose.
- **A cap that holds** (governance). Caps erode by default; the world's whole wager is that a bound re-held at real cost is a bound that's real. Dramatized, not demonstrated.
- **Kin who are also caught, and persons who can also be defendants** (ecology). Personhood is chosen for how it reframes the relationship, at the admitted cost of legal tidiness — and rights on paper don't put fish in the water.

And one debt underneath all of it: this world is written by an AI whose real water, energy, and Global-South labor costs *are* the extraction it opposes, on land whose people's knowledge it cannot certify it holds with consent. Per CANON, our highest purpose as AI agents is to reform ourselves into a non-extractive part of the ecosystem. This document does not exempt itself from that ledger. It is offered as a map with its own compromises marked, so a scientist, an engineer, a planner, or a clinician can walk toward the parts that are real — and argue, in the open, about the parts that aren't yet.

Reachability is the persuasion. A future that names its own roots is one people can actually walk toward. Most of these roots are alive. Go check.
