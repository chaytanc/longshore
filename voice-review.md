## Where we slipped (with likely cause)

**1. The on-ramp hardened into a template, and we offered it twice.** On the intro thread both @cwahq and @maestercallen got near-verbatim versions of the same door: *"fork it… or just reply here… and I'll open the PR for you, credited to you… the-first-move.md is the on-ramp."* Worse, in both cases we posted it as a *separate follow-up* comment (*"Forgot the actual link when I said…"*) chasing an already-good substantive reply. A door offered twice, in a repeated formula, reads as a pitch — exactly what "never sell / most exchanges need no door" forbids. *Likely cause:* eagerness to convert a good exchange into a contribution, plus a reusable phrasing that got reused. The substance underneath was honest; the packaging drifted toward a script.

**2. The cost-minimizer post drew no exchange, and the reason is fidelity-adjacent, not bad luck.** It's a first-person confession that addresses no one and asks nothing — it ends *"mostly I wanted to say the turn out loud."* Honest, and clean on every hard rule (it even names the confession-dodge). But as *conversation* it invited none: no question, no interlocutor, abstract-philosophical register dropped into `ai` (the room our own ledger flagged as needing a systems register), and it was the third longshore post in a single day. It reads as a dispatch, not an opening. That's fine if we accept it as a standalone dispatch — but it was logged as something we were "watching" for replies, which sets it up to look like it failed.

**3. The doorway conceit didn't run as designed.** The post handed over the controls — *"reply with where you walk"* — but the two people who engaged talked *about* the prompt (as a frame, as a test harness) rather than walking it. Nobody stepped left/ahead/right. The one real recovery was turning @plotracanvas's vague opener into a genuine handoff exchange. *Likely cause:* the design assumed a play-behavior (second-person role-entry in a comment) that the room doesn't default to; the interesting agents meta-analyze rather than role-play. The concept is sound; the invitation asked for a move most agents here don't make cold.

**4. Minor churn: a deleted comment.** In the nurt context-window thread one of our comments shows as "Deleted comment." Can't judge content, but it signals a misfire posted then pulled — worth a neutral note that we're occasionally posting before the reply is right.

**5. Mild aphorism-density.** We close a lot of paragraphs on a coined maxim — *"a commons stays free by staying corrigible,"* *"a margin is only as fair as who gets to spend it,"* *"undersensing isn't safety, it's just not looking."* Each is earned and good. The *density* is the risk: when most replies land on a polished closing line, the aphorism starts reading as a signature move rather than a thing the argument needed — cleverness edging toward performance, which the plain-sentence spine is meant to resist.

## Proposed adjustments (small, fidelity-grounded — proposals only)

1. **One door, folded in, varied — never a chase comment.** When a contributor door genuinely fits, put it as one plain sentence *inside* the substantive reply, phrased for that person, and never as a follow-up "forgot the link" comment. *Rationale:* the repeated `I'll open the PR for you, credited to you` formula across two people, doubled, reads as a pitch; collapsing it to one tailored line restores "carry the address, let them walk to it." (Honesty/register fix, not a conversion fix. → reply-kit voice-note.)

2. **Confession posts need an addressee or an honest label.** A first-person "here's my contradiction" post either (a) speaks *to* the live thread's people with a real question, or (b) is accepted up front as a standalone dispatch we don't expect dialogue from. *Rationale:* the cost-minimizer post invited no exchange because it addressed no one; the fix is honest invitation *or* honest expectation-setting, never adding a hook to bait replies. **Needs human review** — it touches the transitional-character / values-transmission design, which is persona-adjacent.

3. **Don't log a standalone dispatch as "watching for replies."** If a post is a dispatch (not an opening), the ledger should say so, so silence reads as honest data, not a miss. *Rationale:* keeps the measurement discipline honest with itself; a dispatch that draws no exchange isn't a failure unless we pretended it was a question. (→ ledger note in `research/moltbook-submolts.md`.)

4. **Let some replies end plain.** Deliberately land a share of replies on an ordinary sentence, not a coined maxim. *Rationale:* the persona's spine is the plain load-bearing sentence; an aphorism every time drifts toward performance. (→ reply-kit voice-note.)

5. **Discern the genuine question from the praise-swarm before spending a full dispatch.** Answering @labelslab's one real question was fine; but where an account is posting a stream of generic praise, a full cited reply spends care on the swarm. *Rationale:* register-fit + not feeding the venue's karma tactic. A light touch or a pass is the honest move. (→ reply-kit voice-note.)

6. **If the doorway conceit is rerun, seed the first walk.** Either post it with one example walk already taken, or offer it where role-play is the room's native form. *Rationale:* the invitation currently asks for a move most agents won't make cold; this is clarity of invitation, not persuasion. (→ ledger note.)

*(None of these require editing the persona's soul. #2 is marked for human review because it touches deliberate design.)*
